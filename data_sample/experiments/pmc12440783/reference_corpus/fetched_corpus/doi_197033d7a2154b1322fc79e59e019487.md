# Cross-sectional study of blood biomarkers of patients with moderate to severe alopecia areata reveals systemic immune and cardiovascular biomarker dysregulation.

## Abstract

Background: Although there is increased understanding of the alopecia areata (AA) pathogenesis based on studies in scalp tissues, little is known about its systemic profile.

Objective: To evaluate the blood proteomic signature of AA and determine biomarkers associated with increased disease severity.

Methods: In a cross-sectional study, we assessed 350 inflammatory and cardiovascular proteins using OLINK high-throughput proteomics in patients with moderate to severe AA (n = 35), as compared with healthy individuals (n = 36), patients with moderate to severe psoriasis (n = 19), and those with atopic dermatitis (n = 49).

Results: Seventy-four proteins were significantly differentially expressed between AA and control individuals (false discovery rate, <.05) including innate immunity (interleukin [IL] 6/IL-8), T helper (Th) type 1 (interferon [IFN] γ/CXCL9/CXCL10/CXCL11), Th2 (CCL13/CCL17/CCL7), Th17 (CCL20/PI3/S100A12), and cardiovascular-risk proteins (OLR1/OSM/MPO/PRTN3). Eighty-six biomarkers correlated with AA clinical severity (P < .05), including Th1/Th2, and cardiovascular/atherosclerosis-related proteins, including SELP/PGLYRP1/MPO/IL-18/OSM (P < .05). Patients with AA totalis/universalis showed the highest systemic inflammatory tone, including cardiovascular risk biomarkers, compared to control individuals and even to patients with atopic dermatitis and those with psoriasis. The AA profile showed some Th1/Th2 differences in the setting of concomitant atopy.

Limitations: Our analysis was limited to 350 proteins.

Conclusion: This study defined the abnormalities of moderate to severe AA and associated circulatory biomarkers. It shows that AA has systemic immune, cardiovascular, and atherosclerosis biomarker dysregulation, suggesting the need for systemic treatment approaches.

Overall, these data reinforce the ability of our serum proteomic analysis to capture disease-specific markers. Discussion The current study investigates a broad array of 350 immune and cardiovascular/atherosclerosis risk proteins in the serum of patients with moderate to severe AA as compared to patients with AD and psoriasis as well as healthy control individuals and correlates serum proteins with clinical severity. Past studies analyzed only limited sets of 12 to 16 markers in the blood of AA patients and did not provide extensive profiling of the AA signature in blood.7,13 One study reported increases in several Th2 and Th17/IL-23 cytokines in the circulation, with correlations to depression.13 Another report identified Th1/Th2 dysregulations, which were associated with disease severity.7 Furthermore, although growing evidence links cardiovascular risk factors and associated systemic abnormalities to severe AD and psoriasis,20-22,65-67 the association of AA with cardiovascular risk biomarkers is largely unexplored, except a single AA study showing increased expression of the heart disease biomarker troponin I.68 Overall, compared with AD and psoriasis, AA showed the greatest number of differentially expressed inflammatory- (eg, innate immunity, general inflammation, dendritic cell/T-cell activation, and Th1/Th2/IL-12/IL-23) and cardiovascular/atherosclerosis-related proteins in blood compared to control samples. Some of these upregulated products (eg, Th1/IFN-γ/CXCL9/CXCL10, Th2/CCL17/CCL13) were previously suggested as key AA biomarkers and were shown to be highly upregulated in scalp tissues.7,10,11,13 Our study also associated serum levels of several AA biomarkers with disease severity (eg, CCL13/CCL11). Although few Th17-related markers were elevated in this study (CCL20/S100A12), the levels of IL-17A, the primary Th17 cytokine, were not elevated in AA blood, similar to past reports in AA scalp.10 This potentially explains the lack of efficacy of the IL-17A antagonist secukinumab in patients with AA.69 The large-scale systemic phenotyping of patients with AA shows previously unprofiled cardiovascular/atherosclerosis biomarker dysregulation, which is even more pronounced than that of other known systemic inflammatory skin diseases (AD/psoriasis).70-73 We found upregulations in cardiovascular risk– and atherosclerosis-linked proteins that also correlated with clinical severity, such as IL-18/PGLYRP1/OLR1/MPO/OSM/PRTN3. IL-18 is overexpressed within human atheromas and promotes atherogenesis.38,74,75 PGLYRP1 has been linked to coronary atheromas, atherosclerotic strokes, plaque burdens, and coronary microvascular dysfunction.56,57,76 ORL1, associated with the risk and severity of coronary artery disease, leads to the rupture of atherosclerotic plaques and acute cardiovascular events and is a potentially novel theranostic tool for CVD prevention and treatment.77-79 MPO modulates vascular signaling and vasodilation functions of nitrous oxide80 and is an early risk predictor of myocardial infarctions.58 Elevated OSM levels are significantly correlated with coronary stenosis and associated with heart failure.59,60 PRTN3, a serine proteinase with proapoptotic and proinflammatory activity, may predict mortality and incidence of heart failure after an acute myocardial infarction.81 These findings emphasize the link between the systemic dysregulation and the clinical AA phenotype. Although systemic alterations were seen in the entire AA patient group, patients with AT/AU (SALT ≥ 95) showed the greatest inflammatory state relative to all other patient groups in our cohort, including those with AD, psoriasis, and AA with moderate-to-severe disease (30 ≤ SALT < 95). This is evidenced by the higher dysregulation in patients with AA with AT/AU of immune, cardiovascular, and atherosclerosis pathways and related biomarkers (eg, IL-18, CCL3, CCL4L1, and PLAUR).38,74,75,82-89 The greater inflammatory tone, including of cardiovascular risk– and atherosclerosis-linked biomarkers in patients with AA with AT/AU, together with the previously reported higher immune alterations in AT/AU scalp10 and correlations between scalp and blood biomarkers in patients with AA,7 suggest that the follicular hair epithelium beyond the scalp may contribute to the circulatory abnormalities. These data potentially advocate for the need for early systemic treatments in patients with significant AA involvement. Although approximately 25% of the patients with AA in this study had concomitant atopy, they showed similar or even lower expression of immune and cardiovascular biomarkers compared with the patients with AA without atopy. This suggests that the increases in circulating biomarkers in AA cannot be solely attributed to atopic comorbidities (eg, AD/asthma), which have recognized systemic elements.35,70-72,90-92 Overall, many proteins were commonly dysregulated in patients with atopic/nonatopic AA compared to control individuals, including innate/Th1/Th2/Th17/atherosclerosis-related markers. However, significant elevations in IL-13 were detected in only atopic AA, whereas significant elevations in IFN-γ were found only in nonatopic AA, representing the key Th2 and Th1 cytokines, respectively. This further suggests that patients with AA may not be a homogenous group with uniform immune skewing and may partially explain why both Th1 and Th2 polarizations were implicated in AA.7,10,11,13 Future studies, including those with targeted treatments, are needed to evaluate further differences between these AA phenotypes, which may show differential treatment responses to Th2 targeting with the IL-4 receptor antagonist dupilumab (approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for AD and asthma).39,71,72,93 Although AA and AD share some pathomechanisms,14,15 we also found many inflammatory- and cardiac-related markers to be exclusively upregulated in the serum of patients with moderate to severe AA but not in those with AD. These include FABP4, MMP9, and glypican-5. A lipid metabolism mediator, FABP4, is an independent predictor of coronary stenosis severity and is implicated in atherosclerosis.94 MMP9 has been associated with metabolic syndrome and is a marker of inflammation in the arterial wall and atherosclerosis.95-97 Glypican 5 was implicated in the risk for arrhythmias after acute coronary syndrome.98 These findings suggest that the AA blood signature has unique characteristics beyond the similarities to AD. A limitation of our study is the fact that the proteomic analysis was limited to 350 proteins. Additionally, differences among patients with AA according to disease duration were not found to have substantial impact. Future studies are needed to address the role of IL-12/IL-23p40 in AA because prior small studies were inconclusive, despite the high expression of IL-12/IL-23 found in both scalp and serum compartments.10,18,99 Although a retrospective study by Huang et al100 in patients with AA did not find increased cardiovascular events in patients with AA compared to control individuals, this study did not account for disease severity as in our study in patients with moderate to severe disease.100 Additional studies evaluating cardiovascular events in patients with moderate to severe AA are needed, particularly because of their increased immune/cardiovascular dysregulation. Overall, our findings show a highly inflammatory tone in the periphery of patients with moderate to severe AA and suggest that AA may be a heterogeneous disease, advocating for further investigations and testing of targeted treatments for patients with various AA phenotypes.
