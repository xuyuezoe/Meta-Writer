# Serum CCL20: A novel potential marker of cardiovascular risk in alopecia areata patients

## Abstract

Background: Alopecia areata (AA) is a prevalent autoimmune skin disease that may be associated with systemic disorders. The connection between cardiovascular risks and AA is not widely investigated. Chemokine (C-C motif) ligand 20 (CCL20) has been found to be related to cardiovascular comorbidities. Objective: To investigate the role of CCL20 in AA patients and its relation to cardiovascular comorbidity. Patients and methods: This study enrolled 80 AA patients and 40 age and sex-matched control subjects. Serums CCL20, high sensitive C- reactive protein (Hs-CRP), lipid profile in addition to carotid intima-media thickness (CIMT) were investigated in all subjects. Results: AA patients expressed significantly higher serum CCL20 levels than healthy controls (29.6±20.4 vs. 10.3±6.7, P p value: 0.002, 0.044, <0.001, <0.001, respectively). Regression analysis was conducted for prediction of CV increased risk and revealed that older age, higher LDL, TC, TG, HS-CRP, CCL2, lower HDL and presence of alopecia were associated with risk of higher CIMT in univariable analysis. Conclusion: Serum CCL20 might have a role in AA pathogenesis. It may serve a new possible link between AA and both systemic inflammation and cardiovascular risk observed in AA patients.

As far as we can tell, this is the first study to investigate CIMT in AA patients and its correlations with disease criteria. CIMT on right, left sides and mean CIMT were significantly higher in cases compared with control groups. CIMT measurement has emerged for the monitoring of CVD and stroke risk and may extend to the recommended guidelines for atherosclerosis assessment [18,19]. Moreover, Fabi et al . [20], declared that CIMT could be considered as a surrogate marker of pre-clinical cardiovascular implication and incite to start a personalized management plan. The current explanation for why AA patients have elevated cardiovascular hazards include accumulative exposure to the state of disturbed systemic inflammatory profiles, besides dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, hypertension and platelet stimulation resulting in endothelial dysfunction, atherogenic state and ultimately cardiovascular complications [21]. In the current study, AA patients had significantly higher serum CCL20 levels. This confirms earlier research showing CCL20 is a reliable biomarker in a variety of inflammatory diseases. Although the biological role of CCL20 is not completely elucidated, it seems to be a marker of inflammation and autoimmunity. The increased serum level of this biomarker of inflammation in patients of AA may highlight its role in disease pathogenesis. It was found to be elevated also in different inflammatory diseases [6,22–25]. Alteration of CD4 +T cell subsets, including Th17 cells has been detected in AA subjects. CCL20 is a Th17 associated chemokine. Therefore, it is supposed to play a role in AA pathogenesis [26,27]. Moreover, CCR6-CCL20 axis modulates immune tolerance in healthy subjects and its disruption will alter the balance in the favor of pro-inflammatory cell population, including Th17. It is already established that the immunological impact of this axis affects various body organs, including the cardiovascular system via immune mechanisms [27]. CCL20 expression has a remarkable association with vascular endothelial inflammation, mirrors systemic inflammation and may function as a reliable biomarker of compromised vascular status. Therefore, CCL20 levels in AA patients may also be supposed to reflect the cardiovascular consequences that are associated with this dermatosis [28]. As its concentration is associated with AA severity (SALT score) in the current study, it should be emphasized that the greater level of CCL20 may reflect the degree of systemic inflammation in AA patients. As patients with higher SALT scores express the greatest degree of systemic inflammation compared to the other AA patients [28] The current results disclosed that serum CCL20 had a significant positive correlation with BMI, LDL, TC, and TG. Previous studies have explored the association of CCL20 with dyslipidemia and identified this chemokine as an LDL-responsive gene and examined the molecular pathways by which this chemokine is regulated by LDL. Moreover, LDL has a crucial role in regulating the migration and trafficking of inflammatory cells and chemokines expression [29]. In cultured human vascular cells, LDL induced biologically reasonable levels of this chemokine via a transcriptional mechanism mediated by NF- κB [30]. The significant positive correlation between serum CCL20 and CIMT in our study was in agreement with Elnabawi et al. [6] study which showed that serum CCL20 displayed a significant correlation with vascular endothelial inflammation among psoriatic patients. Previous studies have reported that CCL20 is upregulated in atherosclerotic plaques from coronary artery disease patients [31] The study has some limitations as small sample size, not including different types of AA and measurement of other cardiovascular biomarkers. However, this is the first study to link CIMT as a measure of subclinical atherosclerosis in AA patients and link it to disease criteria and related cardiovascular comorbidities.
