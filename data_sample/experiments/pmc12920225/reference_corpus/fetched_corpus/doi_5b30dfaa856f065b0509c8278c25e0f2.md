Leventhal, MDb Send email to Jonathan.leventhal@yale.edu Affiliations & Notes Article Info Outline Get Access Cite Share Set Alert Get Rights Reprints Previous article Next article Cutaneous immune-related adverse events (cirAEs) due to immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy are common and encompass pruritus and inflammatory eruptions, such as morbilliform, lichenoid, psoriasiform, and immunobullous.1 Although most cirAEs respond to topical steroids and antipruritic therapy,1 high-grade or recalcitrant rashes may disrupt oncologic therapy and negatively impact quality of life. Although studies link cirAEs with prolonged survival, managing high-grade cirAEs conventionally involves systemic corticosteroids, which may limit ICI therapy continuation and reduce survival by disrupting ICI efficacy.2 Further, cirAEs may be recalcitrant to systemic steroids, necessitating additional therapies. There are increasing reports of targeted immunomodulation with biologic agents for the management of cirAEs with promising efficacy and safety.3-5 More studies evaluating biologic therapy in this setting are needed to standardize the management of high-grade and recalcitrant cirAEs.
