# The Effects of an Integrated Exercise Intervention on the Attenuation of Frailty in Elderly Nursing Homes: A Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial.

## Abstract

Objectives: The frail elderly have an increased risk of frailty because of reduced physical activity, cognitive ability and quality of life. This study aims to evaluate the effects of integrated exercise intervention on the attenuation of frailty in elderly nursing home residents.

Design: This study was a cluster randomized controlled trial.

Setting and participants: 146 elderly frailty people from 8 elderly nursing homes in Harbin, China, were randomly assigned into the intervention group and control group after obtaining their informed consent.

Intervention: The intervention group performed integrated exercise interventions for 12 months, while the control group only continued with their daily activities.

Measurements: Sociodemographic, health-related data, frailty levels, gait parameters, cognition, and quality of life were evaluated.

Results: The mean age of participants was 80.74± 2.89 years, and 70.37% (n=95) were female. The Difference-in-difference regression showed that, compared with the control group, phenotypic frailty score (β3 =-1.40, p < 0.001) and stride time (β3 = -0.38, p <0.001) decreased significantly in the intervention group, stride velocity (β3 = 0.24, p < 0.001), step length (β3 = 0.08, p <0.001), cadence (β3 = 17.79, p < 0.001), MMSE total score (β3 = 1.90, p < 0.001) and QOL total score (β3 = 11.84, p < 0.001) increased significantly in the intervention group.

Conclusion: The integrated exercise intervention can effectively improve the attenuation of frailty, gait parameters, cognitive function, and quality of life in elderly nursing homes. We can use the findings of this study as a reference for the design of activities for the elderly nursing home residents, to provide them with appropriate exercises, improve their physical functions, and improve or delay their frailty level, which is principally important for developing countries in east Asia where rehabilitation resources are generally scarce.

## Introduction

Introduction

Frailty refers to the non-specific state in which the decline of physiological reserve capacity and multisystem dysfunction of the elderly lead to the increase of vulnerability and the decline of anti-stress ability ( 1 ). A recent review showed that the global prevalence rates of pre-frail and frail elderly were 34.6% — 50.9% and 4.9% — 27.3%, respectively ( 2 ). Pre-frailty prevalence (40% to 72%) and frailty prevalence (5% to 28%) in Asian countries are higher than those in the world ( 3 , 4 ). At present, China has entered an aging society. According to the latest data from China’s National Bureau of Statistics, by the end of 2019, China’s 65-year-old population had reached 176 million, accounting for 12.6% of China’s total population ( 5 ). The results of the Fourth Sample Survey on the Living Conditions of Elderly People in Urban and Rural China released in 2016 show that there were about 40.63 million pre-frail and frail elderly in China, accounting for 18.3% of the elderly population, 11.2% of the elderly aged 79 and below need care services, and 41.0% of the elderly aged 80 and above need care services ( 6 ).

Frailty is closely related to disability, and can lead to adverse health outcomes, such as falls, depression, cardiovascular events, disability, increased hospitalization rate and death in the elderly ( 7 ). Frailty, disability and comorbidity affect each other, which can affect the health status and quality of life of the elderly and increase the risk of adverse health outcomes of the elderly. A follow-up study of 7439 elderly people over 65 years old showed that frailty was a powerful predictor of disability and high comorbidity rate. More than half of them fell within one year, and 2/5 of them were hospitalized. For each additional frailty item, the risk of cardiovascular disease increases by 25%. After adjusting for age, sex and comorbidity, the three-year mortality risk of frail elderly was significantly higher than non-frail elderly ( 8 ).

A systematic review shows that the incidence of frailty in elderly care institutions is 52.3% ( 9 ). Hou Xiaolin found that the incidence of frailty of the elderly in elderly care institutions was 55.69% ( 10 ). Zhao Yayi investigated 147 elderly people in elderly care institutions in Nanjing, and the detection rate of weakness was 30.7%. Among them, the detection rates of unidentified weight loss, self-reported fatigue, weak grip strength, slow gait speed and low energy expenditure were 7.1%, 15.7%, 79.7%, 72.9% and 24.2% respectively ( 11 ). In general, compared with the community elderly, the frailty status of the elderly in long-term nursing homes deserves more attention.

The occurrence and development of frailty is a dynamic process. In the early stage of frailty, the body is in the balance between the maintenance of health and physiological functions and defects, and its performance is not obvious and difficult to be found. If effective intervention is provided at this stage, it can strengthen the reserve capacity of the elderly body, stop or delay the occurrence and development of frailty, and reduce disease susceptibility and adverse outcomes. Therefore, it is of great significance to monitor and intervene the occurrence and development of frailty in the early stage of elderly, timely predict the risks of various adverse health outcomes, and formulate the health intervention model for the debilitated elderly from all levels (individuals, families, communities, medical institutions and government functional departments), so as to improve the health level and quality of life of the elderly.

With appropriate intervention, physical frailty may be reversible. Exercise intervention is the main means to prevent and treat frailty in the elderly( 12 , 13 ). At present, there are many sports intervention methods for senile frailty abroad, such as resistance training, aerobic training, balance training, flexibility training and multi-component training, which have achieved good results( 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 ). The purpose of this 12-month follow-up study was to compare the effects of 12-month integrated interventions and the combination of these interventions with routine care control in reducing the frailty of frail elderly people in nursing homes. We hypothesized that integrated physical exercise can effectively improve the level of frailty, gait parameters and secondary outcomes (including cognitive level and quality of life) of frail elderly people in elderly nursing homes.

## Material and methods

Material and methods

Study Design

The study design is a cluster, single-blind, parallel-group, randomized trial of an individually tailored multi-component intervention for frailty living in elderly nursing home residents with a one-year follow-up. In order to avoid the possibility of contamination of participants in the intervention group and the control group, we adopted a cluster random design in the grouping - the randomization units were nursing homes rather than the individual classification of participants ( 19 ).

After randomization, we conducted intervention and follow-up for 12 months to determine whether the intervention effect can be sustained. At the end of the intervention (after 12 months), we compared the baseline measurement changes of frailty, gait, cognitive ability and self-reported quality of life in the intervention group and the control group. We used the CONSORT Statement extensions for trials as a guideline for this study ( 20 ).

Participants and recruitment

The study was conducted in 8 elderly nursing homes from March 2019 to March 2020, involving 146 frail elderly over the age of 75 from 3 different districts in Harbin, China. Prior to the start of the study, we explained the purpose and methods of the study to the participants and sought the written informed consent of all study participants. The study has been approved by the Institutional Review Board of Heilongjiang University of Chinese Medicine (protocol number: 2020-K131).

The inclusion criteria were: (1) aged 75 or above; (2) able to communicate normally; (3) meet one or two frailty phenotype score; (4) willing to take part in the intervention program with informed consent; (5) able to ambulate without personal help. The exclusion criteria were: (1) self-reported chronic diseases and mental illnesses (severe cardiovascular, pulmonary diseases, musculoskeletal, or major depression); (2) severe cognitive impairment (MMSE score < 18) ( 21 ); (3) sensory impairment (visual & hearing); (4) any fracture and surgery (hip, vertebrae) in past two months; (5) unable to walk independently for over 10 meters.

Figure 1 shows the process of sample recruitment, randomization, and implementation for this study. Participants with elderly nursing homes were randomly assigned into two groups: 4 elderly nursing homes were included in the intervention group and the other 4 elderly nursing homes were included in the control group. After the intervention lasted for 12 months, 135 frail elderly completed the pre-test and posttest, including 67 in the intervention group and 68 in the control group. The sample retention rate was 92.47%, and there was no significant difference between participants who withdrew from the study and those who remained in the study with similar baseline data. Intervention

Briefly, the participants in the intervention group received an integrated exercise intervention plan and control group participants received the usual activities. 4 trainers (local nurses who work in the unit) with unified training and rich work experience carried the intervention out. Before the intervention, they took part in the training trainers plan related to physical activities uniformly organized by our team. We divided the participants with elderly nursing homes into integrated exercise intervention group and control group. The intervention group received exercise intervention for 12 months, and the control group did not make any intervention within 12 months.

Physical activity, providing a well-established aerobics exercise program in each elderly nursing home, a 40-minute session five times a week for 12 months, conducted by trainers. Training attendance will be recorded every session. The integrated physical exercise program includes Tai Chi, a traditional Chinese mind-body physical activity, appeals to many older adults and has been extensively studied ( 22 ), as well as chest extension, trunk extension, marching, squatting, and knee extension.

Before each training, each elderly person shall carry out enough warm-up preparation activities, including chest expansion, body rotation, double arm rotation, left and right knee rotation and front kick of both legs, for 5–10 minutes. After the exercise, the relaxation activities include deep breathing, standing still, tapping the shoulder well, massaging muscles, kicking both legs, kicking left and right legs and shaking the whole body for 5–10 minutes.

Data Collection

After obtaining informed consent, we performed a baseline (pre-) assessment. Data collection was during two time periods: in March 2019 (baseline) and 12 months later (end line) in March 2020. Before the experiment, we tested the general demographic data, frailty phenotype, the gait kinematics parameters, Mini-Mental Status Examination and quality of life. After the experiment, we measured the indexes again.

General demographic

The general data questionnaire for the elderly mainly includes general demographic data: age, gender, height, weight, marital status, education level, frequency and duration of weekly exercise.

Frailty phenotype (FP)

The first primary outcome measure was the difference in function between intervention and control group assessed by changes in summary ordinal score on the Frailty phenotype (FP) ( 23 ). (1) unidentified weight loss: weight loss of more than 5kg or weight loss of over 5% in recent 1 year; (2) self-reported fatigue: whether you have encountered any difficulties because of physical reasons when completing daily activities for 3 days or more in the past week; (3) weak grip strength: take the lower limit of grip strength within the range of corresponding gender and BMI with the grip strength tester. The measurement results of male < 26kg and female < 18kg are positive; (4) slow gait speed: if the time of two 6-meter walks < 1.0 m/s, it is positive; (5) low energy expenditure: the daily walking is less than 850m for men and less than 650m for women and feeling physically tired and needing rest is positive. The total score is 5 points, 0 indicates no frailty, 1–2 indicates early frailty, ≥ 3 indicates entering the frailty period, and the higher the score, the more serious the frailty of the elderly.

The gait kinematic parameters

We measured the gait kinematics parameters (step length, stride velocity, cadence and stride time) using floor cinematic parameters assessment. Following the command of the researchers, we invited each participant to walk 10 meters at their preferred speed and make five attempts to reach the end of the runway. For the record of cinematic data, eight passive markers, made of 15mm-diameter adhesive film reflective film were affixed to the following anatomical points: a) right lower limb in the larger femur exchanger, lateral condyle of the tibia, fibular malleolus, lateral face of the limestone and lateral face of the fifth metatarsus head; (b) the lower left limb of the tibial malleolus, the medial face of the limestone and the medial face of the head of the first metatarsal.

A digital camera (Canon brand, LEGRIA GX10) was positioned perpendicular to the center of the distance traveled to film the right sagittal plan of the participants, to view all markers and record a passing intermediary of the path of the lower right member. A video card attached to a computer captured the recorded images. In the following, they scanned automatically the markers at a pass of the floor (interval between two consecutive initial contacts of the right foot), with the use of the Digital Video for Windows program DVIDEOW (Unicamp ( 20 )).

## Discussion

Discussion

The results of this study show that integrated physical exercise can not only effectively improve the frailty and gait parameters of frail elderly in nursing homes, but also effectively improve their cognitive level and quality of life to a certain extent. Integrated physical exercise is suitable as an early rehabilitation intervention to enable individuals to improve or maintain their physical function. It can be carried out as health promotion activities in communities or elderly care institutions, which is specially important for developing countries where rehabilitation resources are generally scarce.

The results that after lasting 12 months of intervention, the score of frailty phenotype of the elderly in the intervention group is higher than that in the control group, and the score of frailty phenotype of the elderly in the intervention group is significantly lower than that before the intervention, showing that integrated physical exercise can improve the frailty state of the elderly to a certain extent and prevent the aggravation of frailty. In this integrated physical exercise intervention, we joined the traditional Chinese martial arts — Tai Chi, and many studies have also reported that Tai Chi can improve the health status of the elderly, including muscle strength, balance and flexibility ( 22 , 27 , 28 ). Our results support a previous study that showed that the prevalence of frailty decreased at the end of the multi-component physical activity program ( 29 ). Therefore, the integrated physical exercise might delay the elderly in the early stage of weakness into the weak period and promote them to get rid of the early stage of weakness and return to the state of no weakness.

Our results show that after 12 months of integrated physical exercise, the gait parameter score has been significantly improved. These results are consistent with previous publications ( 30 , 31 ). The control group was statistically different from the intervention group regarding stride velocity, step length, cadence, and stride time. The effect of the integrated physical exercise intervention on gait parameter is consistent with previous studies in frail older people, which show regular multi-component physical exercise over a prolonged period can improve gait parameter ( 32 ). Integrated physical exercise can effectively improve the leg strength and balance of the frail elderly, so it can significantly increase the stride velocity, step length, and cadence, and effectively reduce the stride time ( 33 ). Previous studies have shown that physical exercise can enhance the gait, balance, and prevent falls of the elderly ( 34 , 35 ). Therefore, continuous integrated physical exercise has obvious benefits for effectively improving the gait parameters of the frail elderly.

This study also confirmed the hypothesis that the integrated physical exercise intervention can effectively improve the cognition of the frail elderly, that is, after the intervention, the MMSE score of the elderly in the intervention group increased significantly, while the MMSE score of the elderly in the control group did not change or even decreased slightly after 12 months from baseline. These findings are consistent with the results of several trials ( 12 , 17 , 29 , 34 ), which show that the cognitive level of the elderly in the intervention group has been significantly improved after the intervention. The results of a review show that compared with the control group, the overall cognitive ability of the intervention group with comprehensive cognition and physical training has been significantly improved ( 36 ), which is consistent with the results of our DID regression, that is, compared with the control group, the net effect of integrated physical exercise on the cognitive improvement of vulnerable elderly is 1.90.

Moreover, our study also found that integrated physical exercise can effectively improve the quality of life of frail elderly in long-term nursing homes. Research on the healthy elderly shows that physical exercise helps to improve cognitive ability and quality of life ( 37 ). However, whether frail older people can show these benefits remains to be recorded. A review pointed out that although the positive impact of exercise on frailty has been fully demonstrated, the impact of exercise on the quality of life and activities of daily living of frail elderly people is still uncertain ( 38 ). Regular participation in sports activities is an important factor affecting the quality of life of the elderly ( 39 ). Previous studies have emphasized the close relationship between physical function and health-related quality of life (HRQOL) in the elderly ( 40 , 41 ). Increasing the amount of physical activity in leisure time helps to maintain health and prevent the decline of quality of life, highlighting the important link between physical activity and HRQOL ( 42 ). Many studies point out that frailty is negatively related to the quality of life ( 43 , 44 ), regardless of how both frailty and quality of life are measured. With the aggravation of frailty, the quality of life of the elderly shows a downward trend. For the elderly, living a healthy and high-quality life is much more important than simply prolonging life.

The analysis in this study shows that integrated physical exercise may significantly improve physical quality, to prevent, delay or change the weakness development of the elderly, and positively affect the quality of life. According to our findings, long-term nursing homes should provide guidance and support to elderly residents and promote activities, including physical training to elderly people. Using appropriate, regular and effective health promotion courses related to physical exercise in long-term nursing homes, as well as encouraging active participation in community activities, can not only improve the ability of daily activities, delay aging and promote interpersonal interaction, but also help the elderly pay more attention to their physical health and quality of life.

The intervention measures well-received, and 86.5% of the participants participated in each session on time under the supervision and inspiration of the trainers, and the attrition rate was low, almost all the participants (92.47%) returned for follow-up evaluation. However, this study has some limitations. First, the sample size of this study is relatively small, which may limit the interpretation and promotion of our findings. Therefore, a randomized controlled trial with a larger sample size may be more proof of the effect of integrated exercise intervention on alleviating frailty. Second, the average age of the participants in our study was around 80 years old, and 70% of them were women, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Third, our data collection team leaders were not mask to the intervention allocation of clusters, but the enumerators under their command were mask to this point. Although the enumerators for the baseline and follow-up surveys remain the same, they have all received rigorous training and promised to strictly follow the survey guidelines when collecting data.

## Conclusion

Conclusion

The 12-month integrated physical exercise intervention was found to reduce the frailty level of the elderly, significantly improved the gait parameters, and effectively improved the cognitive level and quality of life of the elderly, which can be used as a supplement to disease management. Integrated physical exercise is suitable as an early rehabilitation intervention to enable individuals to improve or maintain their physical function. Furthermore, it can be carried out as health promotion activities in elderly nursing homes, which is particularly important for developing countries in east Asia where shortage of resources rehabilitation resources is generally scarce.
