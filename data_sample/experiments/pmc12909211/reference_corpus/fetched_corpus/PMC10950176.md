# Prediabetes and Associated Risk of Cardiovascular Events and Chronic Kidney Disease Among Adult Survivors of Childhood Cancer in the St Jude Lifetime Cohort.

## Abstract

Purpose: Little is known about the prevalence of prediabetes and associated risk of cardiovascular events and chronic kidney disease (CKD) with this reversable condition in survivors.

Methods: Prevalence of prediabetes (fasting plasma glucose 100-125 mg/dL or hemoglobin A1c 5.7%-6.4%) and diabetes was clinically assessed in 3,529 adults ≥5 years from childhood cancer diagnosis and 448 controls stratified by age. Cox proportional hazards regression estimated progression from prediabetes to diabetes, and risk of future cardiac events, stroke, CKD, and death.

Results: Among survivors, median age 30 years (IQR, 18-65), and the prevalence of prediabetes was 29.2% (95% CI, 27.7 to 30.7) versus 18.1% (14.5 to 21.6) in controls and of diabetes was 6.5% (5.7 to 7.3) versus 4.7% (2.7 to 6.6). By age 40-49 years, more than half of the survivors had prediabetes (45.5%) or diabetes (14.0%). Among 695 survivors with prediabetes and longitudinal follow-up, 68 (10%; median follow-up, 5.1 years) progressed to diabetes. After adjustment for demographic factors and body composition, risk of progression was associated with radiation exposure to the pancreatic tail ≥10 Gy (hazard ratio [HR], 2.7 [95% CI, 1.1 to 6.8]) and total-body irradiation (4.4 [1.5 to 13.1]). Compared with survivors with normal glucose control, adjusting for relevant treatment exposures, those with prediabetes were at increased risk of future myocardial infarction (HR, 2.4 [95% CI, 1.2 to 4.8]) and CKD (2.9 [1.04 to 8.15]), while those with diabetes were also at increased risk of future cardiomyopathy (3.8 [1.4 to 10.5]) or stroke (3.4 [1.3 to 8.9]).

Conclusion: Prediabetes is highly prevalent in adult survivors of childhood cancer and independently associated with an increased risk of future cardiovascular and kidney complications. Prediabetes, a modifiable risk factor among childhood cancer survivors, represents a new target for intervention that may prevent subsequent morbidity and mortality.

INTRODUCTION In the modern era, over 85% of children diagnosed with cancer will become 5-year survivors. 1 However, survival comes at a cost, including increased risk of chronic health conditions, with over 80% of childhood cancer survivors experiencing a severe chronic health condition by age 45 years. 2,3 Among these chronic health conditions is an increased risk of diabetes mellitus, 4-6 estimated to be nearly twice that of noncancer populations. 7,8 In the general pop- ulation, diabetes is well established to increase risk of cardiovascular disease and death. 9,10 In survivors of child- hood cancer, diabetes has been shown to potentiate the risk of heart failure in a near-multiplicative fashion 11 and in- crease risk of death independent of treatment exposures. 12,13 Therefore, new onset of diabetes in survivors is a critical marker identifying a high-risk state. On the basis of this knowledge, prevention of diabetes to prevent cardiovascular disease and death now becomes a high priority. We hypothesized that if prevalent and progressive, prediabetes, given its reversable nature, may be an early target for prevention strategies among survivors. Limitations to be considered when interpreting results in- clude that, despite being the largest population of survivors to our knowledge with laboratory-based assessment for prediabetes, laboratory values were taken at a single time point and are at risk of misclassifying some individuals. The prevalence of diabetes if diagnosed by two assessments may be slightly lower than reported; however, these individuals would have been considered prediabetic in analyses. Oral glucose tolerance testing, not available in our cohort, would likely have identified additional survivors with prediabetes by impaired glucose tolerance and normal fasting glucose, as this has been demonstrated in survivors exposed to abdominal irradiation 38 or TBI.49 Although our analysis was not designed to include intermediate treatment-related chronic conditions, prediabetes in survivors may co-occur with other prevalent endocrine deficiencies that affect insulin sensitivity and lean mass, including growth hormone de ficiency and Leydig cell failure, 50,51 and for an individual patient, recognition of these conditions and appropriate hormone replacement may improve metabolic risk. Potentially eligible participants who died before the inception of SJLIFE were unable to be included in this analysis. We hypothesize this would lead to an un- derestimation of the prevalence and late events associated with prediabetes and diabetes. Finally, our follow-up time for mortality, major cardiovascular events, and CKD was relatively short, and longer follow-up is needed to con firm results; however, there were >100 deaths observed, with nearly half occurring in those with diabetes or prediabetes. Even within this limitation, we demonstrated that prediabetes or diabetes is associated with cardiovascular and renal complications and death. In conclusion, prediabetes is highly prevalent in adult sur- vivors of childhood cancer, progressed to diabetes at a high rate over observed follow-up, and occurs at a younger age than in controls. We have identi fied that prediabetes and diabetes each increase risk of future cardiovascular events and CKD in survivors, independent of cancer treatment and other traditional cardiovascular risk factors. Furthermore, the de- velopment of diabetes in survivors is associated with an in- creased risk of death from health-related causes, including deaths related to complications of previous treatment. On the basis of thesefindings, it is now clear that future interventions are needed to target prediabetes as a modifiable risk factor and determine the optimal strategy for prevention of diabetes and subsequent early morbidity and mortality.
