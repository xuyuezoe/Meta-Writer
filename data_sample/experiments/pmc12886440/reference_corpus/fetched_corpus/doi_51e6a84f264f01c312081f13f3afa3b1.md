However, limited studies demonstrated the effects on patients with acute musculoskeletal injuries although these injures are usually painful and stressful, even with minor injuries, and the patients usually have long waiting time in the emergency department. This study examined the effects of music therapy on the anxiety level, pain control and satisfaction of Chinese patients with minor musculoskeletal injuries in the emergency department of Hong Kong. A quasi-experimental design was adopted. All participants in the experimental group received the usual care plus music therapy by head phone, whereas those in the control group received only the usual care. The music therapy consisted of fifty different light music or folk songs as assigned by the project panel. The usual care consisted of standard medical and nursing care for patients who sustained musculoskeletal injury. Main outcome measures, including pain level (horizontal numerical rating scales 0-10), anxiety level (short form of the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory) and patient satisfactory level (numerical rating scales 0-10scale) were taken at baseline (after triage), 30 minutes after triage and on discharge. One hundred and twenty one eligible participants (59 experimental and 62 control) completed the study. There is no significant different at baseline in term of demographic data and pain level, anxiety level. Participants of the experimental group showed a significant less pain level (P<0.001) and less anxiety level (p<0.001) and better satisfaction level (P<0.001) at 30 minute after triage and on discharge. Music therapy is useful as an adjunctive nonpharmacologic treatment for the patients with minor musculoskeletal injuries in the emergency department.
