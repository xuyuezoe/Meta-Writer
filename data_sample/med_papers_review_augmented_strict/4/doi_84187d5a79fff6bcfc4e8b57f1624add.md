# Current technological advancement in asthma care.

## Abstract

Introduction: Asthma is a common chronic respiratory disease affecting 262 million people globally, causing half a million deaths each year. Poor asthma outcomes are frequently due to non-adherence to medication, poor engagement with asthma services, and a lack of objective diagnostic tests. In recent years, technologies have been developed to improve diagnosis, monitoring, and care.

Areas covered: Technology has impacted asthma care with the potential to improve patient outcomes, reduce healthcare costs, and provide personalized management. We focus on current evidence on home diagnostics and monitoring, remote asthma reviews, and digital smart inhalers. PubMed, Ovid/Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus and Google Scholar were searched in November 2023 with no limit by year of publication.

Expert opinion: Advanced diagnostic technologies have enabled early asthma detection and personalized treatment plans. Mobile applications and digital therapeutics empower patients to manage their condition and improve adherence to treatments. Telemedicine platforms and remote monitoring devices have the potential to streamline asthma care. AI algorithms can analyze patient data and predict exacerbations in proof-of-concept studies. Technology can potentially provide precision medicine to a wider patient group in the future, but further development is essential for implementation into routine care which in itself will be a major challenge.

Introduction Asthma is a major noncommunicable respiratory disease and the commonest chronic disease in children [ 1 ]. Asthma affects all ages and ethnic groups [ 2 ]. An estimated 262 million children and adults had asthma worldwide in 2019 causing 455 000 deaths according to the latest WHO figures [ 3 , 4 ] These numbers are likely a substantial underestimate of the real figures. The condition is characterized by variable airflow obstruction and inflammation that cause the typical asthma symptoms of cough, wheeze, shortness of breath and chest tightness [ 5 ]. Uncontrolled asthma is responsible for substantial economic, healthcare, and societal burden [ 6 , 7 ]. Whilst the highest preva - lence of asthma is reported in high-income countries, low- income countries have the highest mortality from asthma [ 8 ]. In the UK, 5.4 million people live with asthma [ 9 ]. Each year this results in over 6-million primary care consultations, nearly 100,000 hospital admissions, over 1,000 deaths/year (20/year in children under 14 years) [ 10 ] at a cost to the NHS in excess of GBP 1 billion. Each year, 29% of adults with asthma are prescribed a course of oral corticosteroids and 4% are admitted to hospital [ 11 ]. Recent data show that 16% of children are poorly controlled with deprivation as a key risk factor [ 12 , 13 ]. These UK asthma outcomes are amongst the worst in Europe [ 14 , 15 ]. Despite effective treatments being available, the number of preventable hospital admissions and asthma deaths remains too high [ 7 ]. Poor asthma outcomes are associated with non- adherence to preventer asthma medication [ 16 , 17 ], poor engage - ment with routine asthma reviews in primary care [ 12 , 13 , 16 , 18 ], poor asthma reviews [ 19 ], and a lack of routinely available objec - tive tests to diagnose asthma [ 20 , 21 ]. Despite these poor out - comes, recent UK data shows that after the COVID pandemic, basic asthma care levels have dropped for the first time in eight years with an estimated 3.53 million people, equivalent to 35% of patients with asthma not receiving basic asthma care. Poor asthma control is particularly prevalent in socioeconomically dis - advantaged populations [ 19 ]. 1.1. Inequalities in asthma care and clinical outcomes Underserved populations have worse asthma outcomes [ 22 , 23 ]. Asthma affects underserved groups and ethnic minorities dispro - portionately with a greater proportion of uncontrolled asthma, asthma attacks [ 24 , 25 ], hospitalization and death. In addition, tobacco smoke exposure actively or passively, air pollution and poor housing disproportionately affect deprived populations [ 26 ]. People from geographically remote communities, and ethnic min - ority groups have poor access to even basic care for their asthma [ 23 ]. Technology has greatly impacted asthma care in recent times. Digital smart inhalers are now available to help manage asthma more efficiently, while telemedicine allows patients to consult healthcare professionals remotely, mini - mizing the need for in-person visits and enhancing access to care [ 28–30 ]. Moreover, remote monitoring technologies, such as home spirometry, can be utilized to keep track of patients’ symptoms and lung function over time, which enables healthcare providers to monitor the disease pro - gression and make necessary adjustments to the treatment [ 31 ]. Wearable devices can monitor environmental factors such as air quality and pollen levels, which can trigger asthma attacks [ 32 ]. Overall, the increased use of technol - ogy has the potential to improve patient outcomes, reduce healthcare costs and most importantly provide more per - sonalized and effective patient management [ 33 , 34 ]. 2. Remote asthma diagnosis Large numbers of patients with respiratory symptoms compati - ble with a diagnosis of asthma are seen in primary and secondary care. Historically, the diagnosis of asthma was based on clinical history, resulting in misdiagnosis with both over- and under- diagnosis being prevalent. Misdiagnosis can place a significant burden on patients by subjecting them to unnecessary symp - toms when under-treated or potential side effects of treatments that are not needed, and the psychological impact of living with a misdiagnosed condition [ 20 , 21 , 35 ]. Recent European Respiratory Society guidelines in chil - dren [ 5 ] and adults [ 36 ] and UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence [ 37 ] highlight the need for objective testing to support an accurate asthma diagnosis in children and adults. The first-line tests spirometry, bronchodilator reversibility and FeNO are however rarely available in pri - mary care and when performed only confirm the diagnosis in 15 to 20% of patients [ 12 , 13 , 16 ]. For additional tests such as indirect and direct bronchial challenges, there is a serious capacity issue and direct bronchial challenges are rarely available even in specialist settings [ 38 ]. Protocols for home diagnosis using repeated spirometry measurements are being developed to overcome the barriers to performing objective tests [ 39 ]. The Association for Respiratory Technology & Physiology (ARTP) advise that spirometry should be conducted under the supervision of professionals who have completed comprehen - sive training [ 40 ]. However, home spirometry has been advo - cated by healthcare providers and they have been positively received by patients [ 41–44 ]. 3.1. Overview of home diagnostic devices for asthma Pulmonary function testing such as spirometry plays a pivotal role in guiding the diagnosis of asthma. Despite its impor - tance, access to spirometry remains an obstacle, particularly in primary care [ 45 ]. To address this problem, several portable home spirometers have been developed and provide the patient with greater autonomy in their care [ 46 ]. The system’s usability was rated by users as ‘good,’ indicating that participants found it user- friendly and effective in the self-managing of their asthma. The OUTERSPACE study [ 103 ] investigated the feasibility and potential benefits of using a smart spacer for personalized inhaler education in 42 adults with asthma. The study demon - strated that smart spacer-driven education led to a significant reduction in inhalation errors compared to usual care. The findings suggest that smart spacers have the potential to enhance asthma manage - ment by addressing inhalation errors and providing personalized education. The usability of the smart spacer was rated high by both patients (System Usability Scale score of 71) and nurses (System Usability Scale score of 89). Although, it is important to note that the study did not specifically report on the improvement in adherence with NEMDs compared to tradi - tional methods. The audio component of the vDOT videos also allows for an assessment of the inspiratory flow and the release of medica - tion from the inhaler device [ 112 ]. A pilot study in healthy volunteer children aimed to evalu - ate the practicality of using the remote approach to capture the use of ‘dummy’ inhalers in a small cohort of 12 healthy volunteer children over a 14-day study period [ 113 ]. The results of this feasibility study confirmed that the monitoring system for inhaler use is feasible and could be suitable for monitoring child inhaler use within a clinical setting. Children with poorly controlled asthma showed significant improve - ment in the two main markers of successful asthma manage - ment: the ACT and Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO) suggesting that vDOT could be an effective tool for improving asthma control in children. In terms of clinical practice, the use of vDOT could provide a cost-effective approach to direct observation of therapy administration, which is one of the most accurate methods of evaluating adherence. A study investigating the long-term cost-effectiveness of digital inhalers found that they are cost- effective in difficult-to-treat asthma, largely due to the reduc - tion in the need for biological therapies [ 114 ]. 6.4. Challenges and limitations associated with the implementation of digital smart inhalers Challenges in implementing monitoring technology such as digital smart inhalers are multifactorial at the patient, clinician, healthcare systems and societal levels. A recent study explored perceived barriers to the imple - mentation of digital smart inhalers within in the context of the Dutch healthcare system [ 115 ]. The authors report that the cost of the patient having to purchase a device coupled with uncertainty about their effectiveness given the novelty of the technology is an important barrier to implementation. In addi - tion to this in an insurance-based healthcare system, it may prove challenging for insurers and policymakers to prioritize which technology to fund given the constant evolution of technology. Healthcare services are slow to adopt change. The reasons for this are multifactorial. New technology will cost money up front in most cases and this money is often not available even if money is saved later. The other problem is integration into existing health platforms and primary care systems. And finally, most of the technology is simply not developed to a level that is robust enough for health care adoption. At a patient level, a qualitative study [ 116 ] found that many patients found the electronic monitoring devices (EMDs) to be bulky, which was associated with decreased ease of use and increased social embarrassment. Patients also reported con - cerns about de-personalization with fully remote, automated systems, as participants highlighted the importance of main - taining personal connections with clinical teams. Patients also expressed a desire to maintain control over decision-making, even during acute deterioration. They preferred phone calls and in-app alerts over paramedics responding in person to automated alerts. Setting up and running programs such as the vDOT con - stitute an additional workload for clinicians, who must review two videos per day for each patient they are monitoring on the application. This is time-consuming and may require addi - tional resources. Clinicians have expressed concerns about data overload in remote monitoring programs. There are con - cerns that information overload leads to difficulty in identify - ing the most relevant information in patient reports and the burden of processing and prioritizing a large amount of data collected from patients. Potential for data overload can cause information fatigue and reduce the effectiveness of remote monitoring interventions. These concerns highlight the impor - tance of streamlining data collection, processing, and presen - tation in remote monitoring programs to ensure that clinicians can effectively utilize patient-generated health data to make informed decisions and provide personalized care to patients. At a societal level with increased technological advance - ment, this has led to growing concerns about data privacy. Another qualitative study [ 117 ], found that technical issues with data uploading and recording as well as privacy and surveillance worries were a major obstacle to wider implemen - tation of digital smart inhalers. To alleviate these concerns, healthcare providers and software developers must put rigor - ous data security and privacy safeguards in place, such as data anonymization, access restrictions, and encryption. Patients should also be given the choice to refuse data collection and be informed about how their information will be gathered, stored, and used. 6.5. Artificial intelligence in asthma care 7.1. Introduction to the role of artificial intelligence (AI) in asthma management There is a growing integration of artificial intelligence within respiratory medicine [ 119 ]. The primary focus of research has been on evaluating radiological imaging, particularly in condi - tions such as lung cancer and fibrotic lung disease given the central role imaging has in guiding diagnosis and manage - ment [ 120–122 ]. However, in a study examining medication self-administration (MSA) errors, an AI system embedded in a sensor was utilized to analyze radio reflections from the environment. The AI technology employed in the study focused on tracking specific movements associated with MSA events, such as the use of inhalers and detecting whether patients followed the required steps for using these medica - tion devices. The AI system was designed to operate in a contactless and passive manner, without imposing any bur - den on the patient, caregiver, or healthcare personnel. The study found that the AI-based solution was able to reliably detect the occurrence of MSA events, specifically for inhaler use. The experimental results showed that the AI system achieved an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.992 for detecting the use of an inhaler. This high level of accuracy indicates that the AI system could effectively monitor whether patients were using their inhalers according to the prescribed time and frequency. Additionally, the AI model was able to detect errors in inhaler use, such as instances where the individual failed to comply with administration duration requirements, such as not holding the inhaler for the required amount of time after inhaling the dose. The use of artificial intelligence in the diagnosis of asthma is more nuanced given that the diagnosis relies on more than imaging. ERS guidelines for asthma diagnosis in both children and adults emphasize the importance of a comprehensive approach consisting of clinical history, examination findings together with pulmonary function tests to guide asthma diag - nosis [ 5 , 36 ]. Although most respiratory physicians may be comfortable interpreting lung function data, this may not be the case for clinicians in primary care with more limited experience. Therefore, AI may have a role here in aiding with pulmonary function testing interpretation. In addition there is novel research investigating the use of AI in the early predic - tion of exacerbations and in predicting control [ 123 ] some - times in combination with digital inhaler data [ 124 ]. In addition to this, there is a movement in developed healthcare systems toward utilizing Electronic Health Records (EHRs) [ 125 , 126 ]. Leveraging this vast amount of clinical information efficiently is important for conditions like asthma where the diagnosis is typically a clinical one. Data from EHRs can be leveraged by utilizing AI techniques, particularly nat - ural language processing (NLP), for data mining and knowl - edge discovery. By extracting valuable information from clinical narratives within EHRs, clinicians and researchers can unlock a wealth of detailed longitudinal patient data, includ - ing clinical history, investigations, and interventions. These approaches can enhance clinical practice by improving the understanding of patient outcomes and disease pat - terns [ 127 ]. 7.2. Overview of AI applications, including predictive modelling, personalized treatment plans, and data analysis Personalized early prediction of asthma exacerbations using machine learning approaches has been reported in the litera - ture. Finkelstein and Jeong [ 128 ] recently reported that they were able to utilize three different types of machine learning algorithms to early predict asthma exacerbations. These AI applications can help healthcare providers identify patients who are at high risk of experiencing asthma exacerbations and intervene early to prevent them. Furthermore, a big data approach in the United States has been used to predict the number of asthma-related Emergency Department visits in a particular location. This is based on surrounding environ - mental and social medical data with a precision of 70% [ 129 ]. Through the analysis of extensive and intricate datasets that comprise diverse sources of data, artificial intelligence can also assist in identifying treatment responders for various therapeutic techniques. AI techniques can be used to identify patterns and relationships between patient characteristics, disease phenotypes, and treatment outcomes [ 130 ]. This can help healthcare providers predict which patients are likely to respond to a particular treatment and which patients are not. For example, AI can be used to predict a patient’s response to biological treatments in severe asthma based on phenotypic characteristics. By identifying treatment responders, health - care providers can personalize treatment plans for patients with asthma, leading to improved patient outcomes and reduced healthcare costs. Additionally, AI can help identify new therapeutic targets and develop new treatments for asthma by analyzing large and complex datasets [ 131 ]. The DQ4HEALTH model is a data quality evaluation system proposed by Kim et al. [ 132 ] for assessing the quality of multi - centre clinical epidemiological information linked to human materials. This model breaks down the concept of data quality into four dimensions: completeness, validity, accuracy, and uniqueness. Completeness focuses on ensuring that all neces - sary data elements are present and not missing. Validation rules related to completeness assess whether essential infor - mation, such as unique identifiers, medical history, or demo - graphic data, is complete and available for analysis. Validity checks the accuracy and correctness of the data values. Validation rules ensure that data elements fall within accepta - ble ranges, formats, or categories. Accuracy pertains to the timeliness and correctness of data entries. Rules in the accu - racy dimension evaluate the temporal relationships between 506 A. HAKIZIMANA ET AL.

data elements, such as ensuring that certain events occur in the correct sequence or within a specific timeframe. Uniqueness focuses on preventing duplicate or redundant data entries. By utilizing the DQ4HEALTH model, researchers and healthcare professionals can systematically evaluate the quality of clinical epidemiological information linked to human materials across multiple institutions. This model pro - vides a structured framework for assessing data quality in healthcare settings, enabling the identification and resolution of data quality issues to enhance the reliability and utility of healthcare data for research and decision-making purposes. The efficient and precise diagnosis of asthma in children is essential. Emphasizing the need for an automated asthma detection mechanism. A study investigating asthma diagnoses among pediatricians at various levels revealed an average accuracy of only 59.29% [ 133 ]. Notably, the diagnosis rates in primary healthcare units were significantly lower compared to specialist care. To enhance the accuracy of asthma diagnosis, different modeling systems, including those based on machine learning, have been designed. These models leverage retrospective electronic medical records (EMRs) to identify asthma, aiming to improve diagnostic rates in pediatric cases. 7.3. Advantages and limitations of AI The application of AI systems holds the potential to signifi - cantly enhance the accuracy of asthma diagnosis, thereby improving the overall management of the condition [ 134 ]. By swiftly and precisely diagnosing asthma, there is an optimistic vision that such models could evolve into personalized diag - nostic tools, aiming to decrease the misdiagnosis rate of asthma and preventing undetected cases [ 135 ]. This can miti - gate the inappropriate use of antibiotics and systemic corti - costeroids. However, it’s important to note that current AI models tested are limited to identifying the presence of asthma and lack the capability to classify disease severity or provide treatment recommendations. Additionally, these mod - els do not prompt clinicians to complete medical records or auxiliary examinations at present. Ongoing advancements in AI technology may address these limitations in the future. A retrospective birth cohort study of 500 [ 136 ] participants assessed a Natural Language Processing (NLP) algorithm’s per - formance in predicting asthma status, demonstrating high sen - sitivity, specificity, and predictive values. The NLP algorithm accurately identified known risk factors for asthma, such as allergic rhinitis. Agreement between the NLP algorithm and manual chart review was high, indicating strong criterion validity. The algorithm’s ability to determine the asthma index date accurately allows for temporality assessment in epidemiologic studies. Similarly, a retrospective cross-sectional study con - ducted at the Mayo Clinic in Minnesota, United States of America, between 2003 and 2016 utilizing an NLP algorithm extracted key components of asthma guidelines from EHRs on a birth cohort with asthma [ 137 ]. The NLP algorithms demon - strated high sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value in identifying guideline-congruent ele - ments, with challenges noted in assessing medication compli - ance and inhaler technique due to varied descriptions. This highlights the potential of AI tools in enhancing clinician adherence to guidelines through automated analysis of EHR data, offering insights into improving asthma care quality and patient outcomes. A mixed methods study [ 138 ] examining the attitudes of children as well as their parents on the use of AI and digital twins for the daily management of asthma found that the major - ity of children were open to using AI in the form of digital twin systems for managing their asthma. Specifically, half of the chil - dren (56%) were willing to use a digital twin system for the daily management of their asthma if it was as effective as current care, and up to 93% were willing to use it if it was more effective. However, parents were more reluctant than their children to use a digital twin system for the daily management of their children’s asthma. While 31% of parents stated that they would never choose a digital twin system, 43% were willing to use it if it was as effective as the physician, and up to 69% if the system was perceived to be 30% better than the physician’s usual manage - ment. This indicates that parents were generally less open to the idea of using AI technology like digital twin systems compared to their children. Currently, the AI models tested are only suitable for identi - fying the existence of asthma, and it cannot provide disease severity classification, treatment suggestions or prompt clin - icians to complete medical records and auxiliary examinations. The AAAAI Work Group Report on Augmented Intelligence in Allergy and Immunology Practice and Research highlighted the potential transformative impact of these technologies on patient care, diagnostics, and treatment decisions [ 139 ]. They stressed the need for rigorous evaluation, approval processes, and ongoing assessment of AI’s impact on research and clinical prac - tice. The workgroup highlights the diverse clinical applications of AI in allergy and immunology, ranging from disease diagnosis to data reduction in electronic health records and immunological datasets. Key challenges identified include the incorporation of data science and bioinformatics into the training of future aller - gists, as well as the need for specialists to be actively involved in the design, validation, and implementation of AI tools in the field. They call for the engagement of various stakeholders, including clinicians, informaticists, data scientists, and policymakers, in the development and deployment of AI solutions in allergy and immunology. 8. Conclusion In conclusion, technology has significantly advanced asthma care by facilitating early detection and diagnosis, enabling remote monitoring and telemedicine, promoting personalized treatment approaches, and empowering patients through digital therapeutics and asthma management apps. These technological innovations have the potential to revolutionize asthma management, offering improved disease control, bet - ter health outcomes, and enhanced quality of life for indivi - duals living with asthma. As technology continues to evolve, the future of asthma care holds promise for further advance - ments in precision medicine, predictive analytics, and patient- centered interventions, ultimately transforming the landscape of asthma care. 9. Expert opinion The use of advanced diagnostic technologies has made it possi - ble to detect asthma at an early stage. Portable equipment for evaluating lung function provides quick and accurate results. In addition, wearable sensors and mobile health applications help individuals monitor their symptoms and keep track of lung function parameters, allowing for early intervention and perso - nalized treatment plans. Mobile technology, including mobile apps, virtual reality simulations; wearable devices and digital inhalers empowers patients to manage their condition and improve adherence to their treatment plans. These interactive tools offer personalized action plans, symptom trackers, and educational resources, which promote patient engagement and enhance disease awareness. Furthermore, gamification and social support features may allow for the encouragement of behavioral changes and facilitate long-term adherence to asthma management strategies, especially for pediatric patients. Digital therapeutics and asthma management apps offer inter - active tools and educational resources to empower patients in self-management and adherence to treatment regimens. These apps provide personalized action plans, symptom trackers, med - ication reminders, and access to educational content, enabling patients to better understand their condition and take proactive steps to control their asthma. Moreover, these platforms facilitate communication between patients and healthcare providers, fos - tering shared decision-making and continuous support. The utilization of telemedicine platforms and remote monitoring devices has the potential to improve asthma care by eradicating geographical limitations and providing seamless access to healthcare professionals. This develop - ment has been accelerated during the COVID-19 pandemic. Patients can now receive virtual consultations, access educa - tional resources, and receive personalized management plans from the comfort of their homes. Remote monitoring devices, such as ldigital smart inhalers and connected spi - rometers, allow healthcare providers to monitor medication adherence, identify triggers, and adjust treatment regimens remotely, resulting in better disease control and reduced hospitalizations. As environmental factors have a substantial impact on asthma exacerbations, there will be a greater emphasis on environmental monitoring and inter - ventions to enhance air quality. The use of smart home devices and air pollution monitoring may enable real-time tracking of indoor and outdoor air quality parameters, allow - ing individuals to identify and avoid triggers. Public health initiatives and urban planning strategies aimed at reducing pollution levels and promoting clean air will contribute to the reduction of asthma attacks. Remote patient monitoring systems and AI algorithms ana - lyze patient-generated data, such as symptoms, adherence to medication, and environmental factors, to identify patterns and predict asthma exacerbations. AI-powered predictive ana - lytics allow for early detection of worsening symptoms and prompt intervention, reducing the likelihood of asthma attacks and hospitalizations. Moreover, AI algorithms can ana - lyze large data sets to identify trends, risk factors, and treat - ment outcomes, informing evidence-based practice guidelines and enhancing overall asthma care. Advancements in technology have facilitated the develop - ment of personalized treatment strategies tailored to individual patient needs. Pharmacogenomics and biomarker testing help identify genetic variations and biomarkers associated with asthma severity and treatment response, guiding the selection of appropriate medications and dosages. Machine learning algo - rithms analyze patient data to predict attacks, optimize treat - ment strategies, and identify personalized triggers, allowing for targeted interventions and improved disease management. Advances in genomics and personalized medicine will enable healthcare providers to tailor treatment approaches based on individual genetic makeup, biomarker profiles, and disease char - acteristics. Precision medicine will lead to more targeted thera - pies with improved efficacy and fewer side effects, ultimately optimizing asthma management and outcomes. In summary, the future of asthma care holds promise for personalized, technology-driven approaches that empower patients, optimize clinical outcomes, and enhance the quality of life for individuals living with asthma. By leveraging robust innovative technologies and evidence-based interventions and driving the implementation of the most promising technologies into routine care the healthcare community can continue to advance asthma management and improve health outcomes for patients and reduce health inequalities worldwide if com - bined with effective education on digital health literacy.
