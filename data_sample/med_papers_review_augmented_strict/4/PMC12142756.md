# Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis on the Accuracy of Artificial Intelligence Algorithms in Individuals Gender Detection Using Orthopantomograms.

## Abstract

The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into dental imaging has led to significant advancements, particularly in the analysis of panoramic radiographs, also known as orthopantomograms (OPGs). One emerging application of AI is in determining gender from these radiographs, a task traditionally performed by forensic experts using manual methods. This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to evaluate the accuracy of AI algorithms in gender determination using OPGs, focusing on the reliability and potential clinical and forensic applications of these technologies. A systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted according to PRISMA guidelines. The study included research articles that utilised AI algorithms for gender detection based on OPG images. Five major databases were searched, and studies were selected based on strict inclusion and exclusion criteria. The analysis focused on studies that reported accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of AI models. Statistical analyses were performed using R software, including forest plots and funnel plots, to evaluate the diagnostic performance and potential publication bias. The meta-analysis included 13 studies, yielding a pooled accuracy estimate of 88.66%. The results demonstrated high specificity among the AI models, with some studies achieving accuracy rates as high as 99.20%. However, there was variability in sensitivity across different studies, indicating that some models are more reliable than others depending on the dataset and features used. The funnel plot analysis suggested slight asymmetry, indicating potential publication bias or heterogeneity. AI models show significant potential in accurately determining gender from OPG images, with some models achieving near-perfect accuracy. Continued research is needed to enhance model consistency and expand the applicability of these tools across different demographic groups.

## Introduction

Introduction

The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in medicine and dentistry is rapidly transforming various aspects of medical diagnostics and treatment. 1 Dentistry, in particular, is experiencing significant advancements due to the application of AI, especially in the analysis of radiographic images. 2 , 3 AI technologies such as deep learning have enhanced the accuracy and efficiency of diagnosing dental conditions by automatically detecting and interpreting complex patterns in imaging data. 4 These technologies assist clinicians in identifying objects like anatomical landmarks, fractures, and oral cancers at earlier stages, leading to improved patient outcomes. 5 , 6 , 7 Moreover, AI-driven systems are streamlining treatment planning and personalised care by providing precise measurements and predictive models that support clinical decision-making. 8 The continuous development of AI applications in dentistry improves diagnostic reliability and reduces the cost and time associated with traditional diagnostic methods. 9

OPGs are widely used in dentistry to provide a complete overview of the oral region. 10 Traditionally, these images have been used to identify dental issues and skeletal abnormalities, but recent developments suggest that OPGs could also be valuable in determining a person's gender. 11 The ability to accurately determine gender from OPG images has important implications in forensic science, anthropology, and personalised dental care. 12 Conventional methods for gender determination often involve the manual examination of specific traits in the jaw and skull by experienced forensic odontologists. 13 AI algorithms, particularly those utilizing deep learning, offer a promising alternative by potentially providing more consistent, faster, and accurate determinations. 14

Gender determination from OPG images is based on the analysis of various anatomical features in the mandible and surrounding structures. Key features include the shape and size of the mandibular ramus, the gonial angle, the mandibular body's height, and the overall robustness of the jaw. 11 , 12 , 13 Males typically have more prominent and larger widths of the ramus, maximum height of the condyle, height of the ramus, and coronoid and bigonial width, compared to females, whose mandibular features are generally smaller. 15 Additionally, the size and shape of teeth, especially the canines, which tend to be larger in males, can also provide clues. 16 By analysing these features, AI algorithms can be trained to distinguish between male and female OPG images with a certain level of accuracy.

This study aims to investigate the accuracy of AI algorithms in identifying gender based on OPG images. By compiling and analysing the existing data, this review seeks to provide a clear understanding of the current capabilities and limitations of AI in this area, as well as its potential clinical applications. Given the growing role of AI in dental practice, it is crucial to assess the reliability and accuracy of these tools for gender determination from OPG images. The results of this review could guide the future use of AI in dental imaging and contribute to the development of more accurate algorithms, ultimately improving both forensic and clinical outcomes.

The null hypothesis is that AI algorithms cannot accurately detect the gender of individuals using their OPG images.

## Eligibility criteria

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion criteria listed below were used for the meta-analysis: 1) research using AI algorithms to determine gender; 2) analysis and review of OPG images; 3) findings and accuracy claims; and 4) publications until August 2024. Only research meeting these prerequisites was included. Excluded were studies that: 1) conducted a scoping review, systematic review, or meta-analysis; 2) were not able to find the full text of the article; 3) used other types of radiographic images; and 4) did not focus on gender detection in radiographic images by various AI algorithms.

## Research strategy and screening

Research strategy and screening

Using five different databases—Web of Science, Embase, Scopus Secondary, Scopus, and PubMed—we identified and evaluated study articles using a thorough process, with an emphasis on publications that were accessible until August 2024. PRISMA criteria were followed in the selection procedure of the research that was included in the meta-analysis. The keywords employed for each database are detailed in Supplementary File 1 , selected with precision to facilitate the analysis of articles across various disciplines. Unbiased assessments of the titles and abstracts were conducted by Z.Kh. and S.A., with conflicts being resolved by M.D., the third reviewer. The analyses included all the papers that met the qualifying requirements and had complete texts available.

The data that was taken from the research papers and summarised in Table includes details on the research's attributes, including authorship, publication year, origin country, imaging type used, model architecture, dataset size (test set), and major conclusions. Extensive test information or model-type research was carefully retrieved for additional examination.

## Quality evaluation

Quality evaluation

Using the QUADAS-2 (Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies-2) structure, the methodological rigor of the research that was part of this evaluation was evaluated. The four main components of this evaluation tool are the following: the index tests that are used, the criteria for reference that are applied, the patients' flow during the research (including the exact order in which the index tests and reference standards are performed), and the patient's inclusion. Z.Kh. and S.A., the two writers, carefully examined every aspect for any possible prejudice, and M.D., the third assessment, addressed any discrepancies. Once bias concerns were found, they were categorised as “high,” “low,” or “unclear,” and a third author was consulted where needed. Analysis of the data was done according to “adequacy of outcomes,” “patient participation,” and “methodological quality,” and it also looked at the variation in findings amongst the included studies (see Supplementary File 2 ).

## Summary measures and data synthesis

Summary measures and data synthesis

A meta-analysis was conducted using data from studies that reported accuracy along with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Four studies provided both sensitivity and specificity, allowing for a separate meta-analysis of these metrics. One study that did not report accuracy was excluded from the meta-analysis. In cases where publications evaluated multiple artificial intelligence models, each model was analysed individually and named by the number and the name of the Author.

Statistical analyses were performed using R 4.4.1 and RStudio. Forest plots were constructed to illustrate the estimates of accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity across various studies. Additionally, a funnel plot was created to graphically assess the potential for publishing bias. To ensure uniformity, accuracy values expressed as percentages in the studies were transformed into decimal format. In cases where studies reported accuracy with an associated error margin, only the absolute values were considered. The heterogeneity degree among the studies was studied using the inconsistency index (I2). 18

## Risk of bias assessment

Risk of bias assessment

A QUADAS-2 assessment was conducted on 14 studies to evaluate the risk of bias and applicability concerns in AI-based dental age and gender estimation methods. The analysis revealed a consistently low risk of bias for patient selection and index test domains across all studies, indicating robust study designs and appropriate test conduct. This suggests that the patient populations were well-chosen and the AI algorithms were applied correctly. While some studies showed unclear risk in certain domains, several demonstrated low risk of bias across all categories, particularly in reference standard and flow and timing. Notably, studies 06 and 07 exhibited low risk of bias in all domains, exemplifying high-quality research in this field. Regarding applicability, all studies showed low concerns across all domains, suggesting that the selected patients, conducted tests, and reference standards were highly relevant to the review question. This uniformly low applicability concern indicates that the findings from these studies are likely generalisable to the target population and clinical setting. Overall, while there is room for improvement in some areas, the QUADAS-2 assessment suggests a generally good quality of evidence in AI-based dental age and gender estimation research, with strong foundations in patient selection and test application ( Supplementary file 02 ).

## Discussion

Discussion

The findings from the systematic review and meta-analysis highlight the significant potential and current limitations of using artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms for gender determination based on orthopantomograms (OPGs). The results indicate that AI models, particularly those employing deep learning architectures, can achieve high accuracy in determining gender from OPG images, with a pooled accuracy estimate of approximately 88.66%. This suggests that AI can be a valuable tool in both forensic and clinical applications where gender determination is necessary. However, some studies 21 , 23 , 24 , 27 , 31 reached over 90% accuracy in detection of the individuals gender ranging from 91.82% up to 99.20%.

The analysis also revealed considerable variability in the sensitivity of these models across different studies. 20 , 22 , 25 , 27 Sensitivity estimates ranged from moderate to high, which indicates that while some AI models are very effective at identifying the correct gender, others may not perform as reliably with Park et al. 27 reaching 99% sensitivity. This variability may be attributed to differences in the datasets used for training the models, the specific features extracted from the images, or the architectures of the neural networks employed.

The study's funnel plot suggested some degree of asymmetry, pointing to potential publication bias, this can be a result in different datasets or used algorithms. Despite this, the majority of studies clustered around the summary accuracy estimate, reinforcing the overall robustness of the findings.

Moreover, the analysis of specificity in some studies 22 , 25 , 27 demonstrated that most AI models exhibit high specificity, indicating a strong ability to correctly identify true negatives (i.e., correctly identifying when an image does not belong to a particular gender). This is particularly important in forensic settings, where the consequences of false positives can be significant.

Park et al. 27 study reached the accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of 99.20%, 99%, and 99.30%, respectively, showcasing that AI algorithms can achieve an extraordinary achievement if we use a correct algorithm with an appropriate dataset.

The subgroup analysis focusing on individuals aged 15 years and above highlights the sustained effectiveness of AI algorithms in gender determination using OPG images within this demographic. The pooled accuracy for this age group was 87.65% (95% CI: 82.35%–92.95%), demonstrating that AI models perform robustly even among older populations where anatomical features are more developed and pronounced. Notably, studies by Park et al., 27 Ke et al., 24 and Ilić et al. 23 reported accuracy rates exceeding 90%, indicating that certain AI architectures, possibly those incorporating advanced deep learning techniques and larger datasets, are particularly adept at handling adult OPG images.

This high level of accuracy is clinically significant, as it suggests that AI models can reliably assist in forensic and clinical settings where gender determination in adults is required. However, the observed variability in accuracy among the studies—from approximately 75% to 99%—suggests that factors such as sample size, population diversity, imaging quality, and model complexity significantly influence performance. This underscores the necessity for standardisation in AI model development and evaluation, as well as the inclusion of diverse populations to enhance the generalisability of the models.

The quality assessment using the QUADAS-2 tool revealed that most studies had a low risk of bias in patient selection and index testing, suggesting that the findings are likely reliable and applicable to the target populations. However, some studies had unclear risks in certain areas, such as the reference standard and flow and timing, which could affect the interpretation of results in those specific cases.

## Limitations

Limitations

This study acknowledges several limitations that may impact the interpretation and generalisability of the findings. A primary concern is the high heterogeneity among the included studies. This heterogeneity stems from variations in anatomical features analysed (e.g., mandibular measurements, dental structures), differences in age ranges and demographic characteristics of the study populations, and the diverse AI algorithms and model architectures employed. Such variability makes direct comparisons challenging and may contribute to inconsistencies in the pooled accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity estimates.

Each study utilised different imaging protocols, resolutions, and preprocessing techniques, which could affect the performance of the AI models. Additionally, the algorithms ranged from traditional machine learning methods to complex deep learning networks with varying layers and training parameters. This diversity in methodological approaches introduces variability that could influence the outcomes independently of the models' true predictive capabilities.

The sample sizes of the studies varied significantly, with some studies having relatively small cohorts that may not adequately represent the broader population. This limitation can lead to overfitting in AI models and reduced external validity. The demographic diversity (or lack thereof) in datasets, particularly regarding ethnicity and age distribution, may also limit the applicability of the models to different populations.

Furthermore, the potential for publication bias exists, as studies with positive or significant results are more likely to be published. The slight asymmetry observed in the funnel plot supports this possibility, indicating that the meta-analysis might overestimate the true effect size due to underreporting of studies with negative or inconclusive findings.

Lastly, inconsistencies in reporting standards and evaluation metrics across studies complicate the synthesis of results. The use of different performance indicators and the absence of standardised thresholds for accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity hinder the ability to perform a cohesive and comprehensive analysis.

These limitations highlight the need for future research to adopt standardised methodologies, including uniform anatomical landmarks, consistent age groups, and standardised AI modelling techniques. Such standardisation would facilitate more accurate comparisons across studies and enhance the reliability of AI algorithms in gender determination from OPG images.

## Conclusion

Conclusion

Within the limitation of this study, we have concluded that: 1. AI algorithms can demonstrate high levels of accuracy, up to 99.20%, with a mean accuracy of 88.66%. 2. Further research are needs to be done and focus on enhancing model consistency, and expanding the diversity of datasets.
