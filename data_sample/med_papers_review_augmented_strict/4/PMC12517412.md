# Disitamab vedotin plus programmed death-1 inhibitor for pre-treated HER2 overexpressed advanced gastric cancer: a multicentre retrospective real-world study.

## Abstract

Background: The efficacy of pre-treated advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer (G/GEJC) with HER2 overexpressed (IHC 2+/3+) remains unsatisfactory. This retrospective real-world study aimed to analyse the safety and efficacy of disitamab vedotin (RC48) plus programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor in pre-treated patients with advanced G/GEJC.

Patients and methods: We retrospectively reviewed consecutive patients with HER2 2+/3+ advanced G/GEJC who received RC48 plus PD-1 inhibitor or RC48 monotherapy from four Chinese hospitals (July 2021 to September 2023). ORR and DCR were estimated by logistic regression. Survival was analysed via Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank tests. Subgroup and post hoc power analyses were performed.

Results: Fifty-seven patients were enrolled: 36 received RC48 plus PD-1 inhibitor (combination group) and 21 received RC48 alone (monotherapy group). The combination group demonstrated a significantly higher ORR than the monotherapy group (41.7% vs. 9.5%, p = 0.011). Although the DCR was not significantly different (75.0% vs. 57.1%, p = 0.162), the combination group showed a significantly prolonged mOS (13.2 months vs. 7.1 months, p = 0.040) and a trend towards longer mPFS (5.8 months vs. 2.9 months, p = 0.142). In the combination group, patients with initial HER2 3+, second-line treatment, or liver metastasis had more favourable outcomes. The incidence of grade 3-4 TrAEs was comparable between the two groups (41.7% vs. 38.1%).

Conclusions: RC48 plus PD-1 inhibitor for pre-treated advanced G/GEJC patients with HER2 2+/3+ demonstrated superior efficacy than RC48 monotherapy and favourable safety. This combination represents a potential therapeutic strategy, but further large-scale prospective studies are still needed.

## Introduction

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) positive accounts for about 16.6% [ 1 ]. Based on the ToGA trial findings, trastuzumab with chemotherapy has been established as the standard first-line treatment strategy for HER2 positive GC [ 2 ]. Recently, the KeyNote-811 study showed that trastuzumab combined with chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab brought a preferable response and significantly improved median progression-free survival (mPFS) [ 3 , 4 ]. Therefore, the guideline recommends a combination strategy of chemotherapy, target therapy and immunotherapy as the new first-line standard regimen for HER2 positive and programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) positive (combined positive scores (CPS) ≥ 1) advanced GC [ 5 , 6 ].

Recent studies suggest that HER2-targeting antibody drug conjugates (ADC) are preferred in subsequent-line treatment for HER2 positive advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer (G/GEJC) [ 5 ]. The Destiny Gastric-01 and Destiny Gastric-02 trials showed favourable efficacy of DS-8201 in second-line and third-line settings HER2 positive GC [ 7 , 8 ]. Disitamab vedotin (RC48), another anti-HER2 ADC, achieved a moderate objective response rate (ORR) of 24.4% in third-line monotherapy for HER2 overexpressed locally advanced or metastatic gastric cancer [ 9 ], leading to its regulatory approval in China. Emerging evidence indicates synergistic potential between anti-HER2 ADC and immunotherapy [ 10–12 ], thereby resulting in the effective treatment for certain tumours [ 13 , 14 ]. The recent prospective single-arm phase I/II clinical trial (RC48-C013) investigating the combination therapy for advanced GC in subsequent-line treatment also showed apparent benefit [ 15 ]. However, patients qualifying for third-line treatment in real-world settings often exhibit poorer performance status, highlighting the need for robust evaluations of the efficacy and safety of combination regimens in clinical practice.

This retrospective real-world study aimed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of RC48 combined with programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitors versus RC48 monotherapy in a Chinese cohort with previously treated HER2 overexpressed (2+/3+) advanced G/GEJC.

## Patients and methods

Patients and methods

Patients and study design

We retrospectively selected a consecutive cohort of unresectable or metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer (mG/GEJC) patients who received RC48 between 1 July 2021, and 1 September 2023, in four centres (including the Southern Medical University Nanfang Hospital, the Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Centre, the Affiliated Hospital of Guangdong Medical University and the Huizhou Central People’s Hospital). This cohort included patients with initially unresectable disease and those with postoperative recurrence. The inclusion criteria are as follows: 1. mG/GEJC confirmed by pathology or histology; 2. The initial HER2 status was detected as IHC 2+/3+, regardless of ISH status; 3. received RC48 with or without immunotherapy as subsequent-line treatment; and 4. the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) was 0–3. The exclusion criteria included: 1. the absence of measurable or evaluable lesions defined by the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1); 2. the lack of response evaluation during the follow-up period; and 3. the prior or co-occurrence of other malignant tumours.

Data collection and evaluation

Most of the data, including patients’ demographics, tumour characteristics, treatment histories, laboratory test and image examination results, and adverse events (AE), were retrieved from the hospital information system. Telephone, messaging and outpatient services were used to ascertain the patients’ survival outcomes.

Tumour stages were evaluated according to the 8th edition of the AJCC/UICC staging system. HER2 expression level was tested by immunohistochemistry (IHC) and in situ hybridization (ISH). IHC was performed on 4-μm-thick tissue sections using an automated IHC stainer (Ventana, Tucson, AZ, USA). IHC staining for HER2 was conducted using a monoclonal antibody (Ventana, 4B5) and scored on a 0 to 3+ scale. ISH was performed using the PathVysion HER-2 DNA Probe Kit (Abbott Laboratories, Chicago, IL) and categorized as either ISH-positive or ISH-negative. The interpretation of IHC and ISH results was based on the NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines for Gastric Cancer (version 2.2022) ( Supplementary Table 1 ) [ 5 ]. HER2 overexpressed was defined as IHC 2+ or 3+, while HER2 positive was defined as IHC 3+ or IHC 2+ and ISH positive. All patients underwent initial HER2 testing. For those with unresectable or metastatic disease, specimens were obtained from biopsies of primary or metastatic lesions, while surgical specimens were used for patients who had undergone primary tumour resection. Additionally, a subset of patients received repeat HER2 testing before RC48 treatment, with biopsy samples collected from primary or metastatic sites after prior-line therapy. PD-L1 expression was quantified using the anti-PD-L1 antibodies (Dako, 22C3 PharmDx) via immunohistochemistry and presented as CPS, which indicated the number of PD-L1–positive cells (including tumour cells, lymphocytes and macrophages) per 100 tumour cells.

After undergoing 2 to 4 successive cycles, researchers utilized image examinations (computed tomography (CT), positron emission tomography (PET) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)) to evaluate responses. These responses were categorized as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD) or progressive disease (PD) according to the RECIST v1.1. The ORR was defined as the proportion of patients who achieved CR or PR at least once following the administration of RC48. The disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the proportion of patients who achieved CR, PR or SD at least once after receiving RC48.

The date of the last follow-up was determined by whichever was later: the date of the last survival follow-up or the date of death. The PFS was defined as the interval from the first dose of RC48 to the initial occurrence of PD or death for any reason. The overall survival (OS) was defined as the interval from the first dose of RC48 to death for any reason. If patients failed to document any date of PD or death, the PFS or OS was censored at the latest follow-up.

AEs were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Version 5.0 (CTCAE v5.0) of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. AE rate was defined as the proportion of individuals experiencing certain AEs within the total population.

Drug and treatment

All patients received a cycle of intravenous infusion of RC48 every 2 to 3 weeks, with the standard dosage at 2.5 mg/Kg, until PD, intolerable toxicity or discontinuation of treatment determined by the doctor. RC48 was combined with programmed death 1 (PD-1) inhibitors, including tislelizumab, toripalimab, sintilimab, nivolumab and penpulimab.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and diagram creation were performed using PASS 2023 (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT, USA), SPSS 27.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA), MedCalc 22.017 (MedCalc Software Ltd., Ostend, Belgium), and Prism 8.0.2 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Descriptive statistics summarized patient characteristics, treatment history, ORR, DCR and safety data. Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test was used for unordered categorical variables, and the Mann–Whitney U test for non-normally distributed or ordinal variables. Binary logistic regression was applied to estimate odds ratios (OR) for ORR and DCR. Kaplan–Meier curves and the log-rank test were used to evaluate follow-up time, PFS and OS, with Cox regression used to estimate hazard ratios (HR). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to identify the factors associated with mPFS. Given the limited sample size, variables with p < 0.10 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate model. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all other statistical tests. Post hoc power analyses for ORR, PFS and OS were performed using Fisher’s exact and log-rank tests. Due to the study’s exploratory nature, no correction for multiple comparisons was applied. A sensitivity analysis addressing missing PD-L1 data yielded results consistent with the main analysis; therefore, detailed data are not shown.

## Results

Results

Patients

Between 1 July 2021 and 1 September 2023, among the consecutive patient cohorts treated in 4 centres, 57 patients were enrolled in this study. Patients were divided into the combination group ( N = 36) and the monotherapy group ( N = 21) based on whether the combination of RC48 with PD-1 inhibitor or not ( Supplementary Figure 1 ). Upon comparing the two groups, no significant differences were observed in terms of gender, age, primary tumour site, number of metastatic organs, prior radical operation, previous trastuzumab and immunotherapy history, number of previous treatment lines, PD-L1 status and HER2 expression level. Notably, the statistical analysis indicated the combination group had a better ECOG PS ( p = 0.030) and a lower proportion of liver metastasis ( p = 0.026) ( Table 1 ). In the combination and monotherapy groups, 41.7% and 42.9% of patients received RC48 as second-line treatment. Additionally, 83.3% and 76.2% of patients in these groups had previous immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy.

Efficacy

In the combination group, the ORR was 41.7% (95%CI: 24.7%−58.6%) compared to 9.5% (95%CI: 4.2%−23.2%) in the monotherapy group (OR 95%CI: 0.03–0.73, p = 0.011). The DCR reached 75.0% (95%CI: 60.0%−90.0%) in the combination group versus 57.1% (95%CI: 34.1%−80.2%) in the monotherapy group (OR 95%CI: 0.14–1.40, p = 0.162) ( Table 2 ).

In the combination group, subgroup analysis revealed that ECOG PS, PD-L1 status, distant lymph node metastasis and the history of trastuzumab or ICIs therapy did not significantly affect the ORR. Although not statistically significant ( p = 0.059), the ORR was higher in second-line treatment than in third-line or subsequent treatment. Moreover, patients with the initial IHC 3+ showed a trend toward a higher ORR compared to those with IHC 2+ (61.5% vs. 30.4%, p = 0.069) ( Table 3 ). The univariate analysis of the mPFS in the combination group indicated that gender, ECOG PS, primary tumour site, number of metastatic organs, liver metastasis, peritoneal metastasis, previous trastuzumab treatment history, number of previous lines of treatment, PD-L1 status and HER2 status prior to RC48 treatment were not significantly correlated with prognosis. ## Discussion

Discussion

Our retrospective study analysed the efficacy and safety of RC48 combined with PD-1 inhibitor as subsequent-line treatment for HER2 overexpressed mG/GEJC in a real-world setting. The ORR for the combination group was significantly higher, reaching 41.7%, compared to 9.5% for the monotherapy group. Similarly, the DCR was superior in the combination group, with 75.0% versus 57.1% in the monotherapy group. Furthermore, the mPFS was 5.8 and 2.9 months, respectively, and the mOS was 13.2 and 7.1 months, respectively. Notably, the safety profile of the two groups was favourable, with no TrAE-related deaths occurring.

Currently, the efficacy of chemotherapy and VEGFR inhibitors in subsequent-line treatment of advanced GC remains limited, with ORR ranging from 4% to 28% [ 7 , 16–19 ]. However, DS-8201 has achieved an ORR of 42% − 43% in second- or third-line treatments of HER2 positive advanced GC, without new safety signals observed besides interstitial lung disease (ILD) [ 8 , 20 ]. The single-arm Phase II RC48-C008 study evaluated the efficacy of RC48 monotherapy as third-line treatment in patients with advanced G/GEJC, showing an ORR of 24.8%, a DCR of 42.4%, a mPFS of 4.1 months and a mOS of 7.9 months [ 9 ]. Recently, the single-arm phase I/II RC48-C013 study further assessed the efficacy of RC48 combined with toripalimab for HER2 expressed solid tumours [ 15 ]. The results of its GC cohort ( N = 28) were aligned with our real-world findings (ORR: 43%, mPFS: 6.2 months, mOS: 16.8 months), while the ECOG PS (0–3) was worse in this real-world study. In this study, the combination group demonstrated significantly superior ORR and mOS compared to the monotherapy group. Furthermore, the impressive second-line ORR of 60.0% supported more exploration of this combination therapy for second-line treatment.

Considering the better ECOG PS of patients and the fewer cases of liver metastasis in the combination group, further statistical analysis of the combination group revealed that 33.3% (2/6) of patients with an ECOG PS 2–3 achieved an objective response, and 46.2% (6/13) of patients with liver metastasis also achieved an objective response ( Table 3 ). These findings suggested that RC48, when combined with immunotherapy, may exert a synergistic effect and maintain effectiveness in patients with poorer general conditions or liver metastasis. Specifically, patients in the combination group with liver metastasis demonstrated better ORR, mPFS and a significantly prolonged mOS. The Center for Drug Evaluation in China has approved a new indication for RC48 as a prospective breakthrough therapy for HER2 positive metastatic breast cancer patients with liver metastases previously treated with trastuzumab and paclitaxel. However, the results of the relevant clinical trial (RC48-C006, NCT03500380 ) have not yet been published. MMAE, the payload toxin of RC48, is primarily metabolized in the liver [ 21 ], which could contribute to the observed variability of its therapeutic efficacy. Given the generally poor responsiveness of liver metastases to immunotherapy [ 22 ], the observed efficacy of RC48 combined with immunotherapy in HER2 overexpressed advanced GC with liver metastases may represent a promising therapeutic breakthrough. However, further research and verification are imperative to assess its efficacy and related factors.

The results of this study demonstrated that patients with HER2 3+ showed superior efficacy compared to those with HER2 2+. The mPFS was significantly extended (6.1 months vs. 5.0 months, p = 0.037), along with a reduction of 59% in the risk of events ( p = 0.042). Additionally, the waterfall plot highlighted that all three patients with the deepest responses in the combination group were HER2 3+, including one CR ( Figure 1 ). The HER2 status emerged as an important prognostic factor in first-line treatment [ 2 , 4 ]. Consistent with these findings, RC48-C013 study also showed a favourable trend towards the improved outcomes in HER2 3+ patients compared to HER2 2+ (ORR: 71% vs. 22%) [ 15 ]. Therefore, initial HER2 3+ may serve as a positive prognostic factor for RC48 plus PD-1 inhibitor in subsequent-line treatment. Based on these findings, we recommend that patients with initial HER2 3+ consider adopting combination therapy with RC48 plus PD-1 inhibitors more proactively.

Previous research has shown no benefit from the continuous use of trastuzumab beyond progression [ 23 ]. After trastuzumab treatment, 29.1%−60.6% of patients may lose HER2 positivity [ 24–27 ], potentially contributing to the acquired resistance to anti-HER2 drugs. However, the recent HER-RAM study, which combined trastuzumab with ramucirumab and paclitaxel for patients resistant to prior trastuzumab therapy, reported excellent efficacy (ORR: 54%, mPFS: 7.1 months, mOS: 13.6 months). It was also observed that the loss of HER2 expression (HER 0/1+) or the maintenance of positivity after prior trastuzumab therapy did not significantly correlate with the efficacy of subsequent anti-HER2 treatment [ 25 ]. HER2 ADCs, known for their high drug-antibody ratio and bystander effect, are effective for partial patients with low HER2 expression [ 28 ]. Our data showed that patients with HER2 expression lose after prior trastuzumab therapy, all in the combination group reached PR, but none of the ones in the monotherapy group reached PR. These findings suggested that for patients with HER2 overexpressed, who were resistant to prior trastuzumab therapy and subsequently lost HER2 expression, the combination of RC48 with a PD-1 inhibitor may hold promise as an effective treatment option. However, further confirmatory trials with larger sample sizes are needed.

The PD-L1 status is an effective efficacy predictor of immunotherapy [ 29 ]. For patients with HER2 positive advanced GC, results from the KeyNote-811 trial indicated limited PFS benefits for those with CPS < 1 compared to those with CPS ≥ 1 (HR: 1.03 vs. 0.71) [ 3 ]. Subgroup analysis of the combination group in this study revealed no significant difference in ORR between patients with CPS ≥ 1 and those with CPS < 1 ( p = 0.720) ( Table 3 ). However, as this study focused on subsequent-line treatment, the CPS data obtained at the initial diagnosis might have been influenced by prior treatments, which could alter the PD-L1 status [ 30 ]. Therefore, the current results were insufficient to demonstrate a correlation between anti-HER2 combined immunotherapy and CPS.

The clinical benefit of immunotherapy rechallenge in advanced tumours remains uncertain [ 31 , 32 ]. In this study, the proportion of patients previously treated with ICIs in the combination and monotherapy groups was comparable (83.3% vs. 76.2%). However, the combination group’s ORR and mOS were significantly superior to those of the monotherapy group. This indicates that PD-1 inhibitor rechallenge in combination with RC48 could further improve efficacy, suggesting its clinical significance.

In the combination group, 61.1% of patients had distant lymph node metastases. In contrast, the ORR and mOS were similar for patients with and without distant lymph node metastases, but there was a trend towards shorter mPFS (5.0 months vs. 7.3 months, p = 0.059). Multivariate analysis indicated that distant lymph node metastasis was an independent risk factor for shorter mPFS in the combination group (HR: 2.44, p = 0.032). ## Conclusion

Conclusion

This retrospective real-world study compared the efficacy and safety of RC48 plus PD-1 inhibitors versus RC48 monotherapy in the previously treated patients with HER2-overexpressing advanced G/GEJC. The combination therapy showed significantly higher ORR and mOS, a trend towards longer mPFS, and a favourable safety profile. Subgroup analyses suggested greater benefit in patients with initial HER2 3+, liver metastases, and those receiving second-line treatment, while outcomes were poorer in those with distant lymph node metastases. Additionally, among patients with initial HER2 overexpression, changes in HER2 status following prior trastuzumab treatment did not appear to affect the efficacy of the combination therapy. Notably, even patients who had previously received immunotherapy could benefit from the combination therapy.

Nevertheless, this study is a retrospective, real-world analysis based on a relatively small sample size from a Chinese population. Despite the inclusion of multiple centres, a control group, and various statistical approaches to ensure the robustness of the findings, potential biases due to baseline imbalances, suboptimal power, and the study’s exploratory nature cannot be ruled out.

Even so, this study offers important preliminary evidence supporting the use of RC48 combined with PD-1 inhibitors in clinical practice, and highlights the need for larger prospective trials to confirm these findings.
