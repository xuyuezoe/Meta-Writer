# Disitamab vedotin plus toripalimab in patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (RC48-C014): a phase Ib/II dose-escalation and dose-expansion study.

## Abstract

Background: Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-targeted antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) such as disitamab vedotin (DV) and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) have emerged as effective treatment options and received regulatory approvals for HER2-expressing locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (la/mUC). In addition, ADCs in combination with immunotherapy have demonstrated antitumor activity. The current study aimed to evaluate the combination of DV and toripalimab in patients with la/mUC.

Patients and methods: This open-label phase Ib/II study enrolled patients with untreated or chemo-refractory la/mUC. During the dose-escalation phase, DV was administered at escalating doses of 1.5 and 2.0 mg/kg in combination with toripalimab 3.0 mg/kg once every 2 weeks. Primary endpoints were safety and the recommended phase II dose (RP2D). Secondary endpoints included objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS).

Results: From August 2020 to December 2021, a total of 41 patients were enrolled, including 6 in the dose-escalation phase and 35 in the dose-expansion phase. Sixty-one percent of patients were treatment naive. No dose-limiting toxicity was observed. The RP2D was determined as DV (2.0 mg/kg) plus toripalimab (3.0 mg/kg). By the data cut-off date of 1 March 2024, the confirmed ORR was 73.2%. The median PFS was 9.3 months, and the median OS was 33.1 months. The most common treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were aspartate aminotransferase increased (65.9%), alanine aminotransferase increased (63.4%), and peripheral sensory neuropathy (63.4%). Grade 3 or higher TRAEs occurred in 51.2% of patients, with the most common being γ-glutamyltransferase increased (12.2%), asthenia (9.8%), and alanine aminotransferase increased (7.3%). One treatment-related death (due to pneumonitis) was reported.

Conclusions: The combination of DV and toripalimab demonstrated promising response rate and OS results with a manageable safety profile in HER2-unselected la/mUC patients. This combination represents a promising first-line option for la/mUC. Randomized phase III study is currently ongoing.

Key words urothelial carcinoma antibody–drug conjugate HER2 immunotherapy disitamab vedotin Introduction Urothelial carcinoma (UC) ranks as the ninth most common cancer in the world, accounting for 614 298 new cancer cases and 220 596 deaths in 2021 worldwide according to Globocan 2022.1 In recent years, novel agents and combinations have significantly improved patient outcomes in advanced UC. Antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs), such as enfortumab vedotin (EV), sacituzumab govitecan, and disitamab vedotin (DV), have demonstrated promising clinical efficacy in patients with advanced UC. As previously reported, 9%-61% of UC are HER2 overexpressing.2-5 HER2-targeting ADCs such as DV and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) have emerged as effective treatment options for HER2-expressive UC.6-8 DV has been approved in China for chemotherapy-refractory advanced UC with HER2-positive expression [immunohistochemistry (IHC) 2+ or 3+]. The approval is based on the combined results of two phase II studies (RC48-C005 and RC48-C009), in which an objective response rate (ORR) of 50.5% and a median duration of response (DOR) of 7.3 months were observed.7 Similarly, in a phase II trial of DESTINY-PanTumor02, 41 patients with HER2-expressing bladder cancer received T-DXd and had an ORR of 39%.8 In recent years, ADC in combination with immunotherapy demonstrated synergistic antitumor effect for the treatment of advanced UC in clinical studies, exemplified by the landmark results of combining a nectin-4-targeting ADC EV with pembrolizumab for first-line treatment of advanced UC.9,10 In the current study, we combined a HER2-targeting ADC DV with toripalimab, a humanized anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) antibody, in patients with HER2-unselected locally advanced or metastatic UC (la/mUC). Here, we report the safety and efficacy results of this study with a long-term follow-up. Patients and methods Study design and participants This is an open-label, multicenter, investigator-initiated, phase Ib/II trial to evaluate the safety and antitumor activity of DV combined with toripalimab in patients with HER2-unselected la/mUC. During dose escalation (phase Ib), two doses of DV (1.5 and 2.0 mg/kg) were combined with toripalimab (3.0 mg/kg) to determine the recommended phase II dose (RP2D). The recommended dose was then evaluated in the dose expansion (phase II). Eligible patients were at least 18 years old with histologically confirmed unresectable la/mUC, who were either treatment naive or had progressed after systemic chemotherapy (including patients with recurrence within 12 months after receiving platinum-based adjuvant or neoadjuvant therapy). Patients must have measurable lesions as per RECIST version 1.1, a baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 0-1, adequate organ function, and available tumor tissue samples for the assessment of programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) and HER2 expression. Patients were excluded if they had active autoimmune disease or interstitial lung disease. There was no preselection for HER2 or PD-L1 expression. A full list of inclusion and exclusion criteria is provided in the study protocol (Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.12.002). The study was approved by the ethics committees of all participating centers and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice. All patients provided written informed consent at enrollment. Procedures In the dose-escalation phase, DV was administered at an escalating dose of 1.5 mg/kg or 2.0 mg/kg combined with toripalimab 3.0 mg/kg using a traditional 3 + 3 design. DV dose is based on calculations using bovine serum albumin (BSA)-based extinction coefficient (EC) in China. Outside of China, DV dose calculation is based on DV-based EC, which is equivalent to 1.07 (BSA-based EC) ÷ 1.41 (DV-based EC) × BSA-based EC dose. The dose would be escalated to 2.0 mg/kg if no dose-limiting toxicities were observed in the initial three patients at 1.5 mg/kg dose. Dose-limiting toxicities were defined as toxicities related to DV or toripalimab within 28 days after first administration, including non-hematological adverse events of grade 3 or worse, grade 4 neutropenia lasting >48 h, febrile neutropenia, and grade 4 thrombocytopenia (or grade 3 thrombocytopenia with bleeding) (protocol in the Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.12.002). The RP2D of DV determined in the dose-escalation phase was employed in the expansion phase. All treatment was given intravenously once every 2 weeks until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal. The protocols permitted continued treatment beyond initial progression if deemed beneficial by the investigators. If the combination resulted in intolerable toxicity, the investigators would decide whether to discontinue treatment altogether or continue individual treatment with DV or toripalimab. No dosage adjustment of toripalimab was permitted by the study protocol, while toripalimab could be delayed or discontinued, with a maximum treatment duration of ≤24 months. Adverse events were recorded and assessed using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA version 26.0) and Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 at each follow-up visit up to 28 days after the last study treatment. Vital signs, physical examination, laboratory tests, and 12-lead electrocardiograms were regularly monitored and recorded. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were determined by the investigators. Tumor assessment by the investigator was carried out using computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging at baseline, every 8 weeks in the first year and then every 3 months afterward until disease progression, subsequent new antitumor therapy, death, or patient withdrawal, whichever occurred first. The same tumor assessment method should be used for individual patients throughout the study. Complete response and partial response need to be confirmed by repeat measurements at least 4 weeks after the first documentation of objective response. HER2 expression was assessed by IHC staining using Ventana anti-HER2/Neu (4B5) Rabbit mAb (Ventana, Tucson, AZ) and the Ultra-Q2 View Universal DAB Detection Kit (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) and was classified into IHC 0, IHC 1+, IHC 2+, and IHC 3+ according to breast cancer standards.3,11 Gene amplification of HER2 was evaluated by FISH, following HER2 test guidelines for breast cancer.11 PD-L1 expression was assessed by IHC staining using the Dako PD-L1 22C3 PharmDx assay (Agilent Technologies Carpinteria, CA). PD-L1 positivity was defined as a combined positive score (CPS) of ≥10. Outcomes The primary endpoints were to assess tolerability and safety, and to determine the maximum tolerated dose/RP2D of DV based on safety data. The secondary endpoints included investigator-assessed ORR (defined as the proportion of patients who achieved a complete response or a partial response), progression-free survival (PFS, defined as the time from the date of first dosing to disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first), disease control rate (DCR, defined as the proportion of patients with a complete or partial response or stable disease), DOR (defined as the time from first observation of objective response to disease progression or death), overall survival (OS, defined as the time from the date of enrollment to death from any cause), association between HER2 and PD-L1 expression and treatment efficacy, and pharmacokinetics. Statistical analysis Six to twelve patients were needed for the dose-escalation phase to obtain sufficient safety data to determine the RP2D of DV. Previous clinical studies showed that the ORR of cisplatin-ineligible patients or those who were platinum refractory ranged from 14% to 30%.12-15 Therefore, for the expansion phase, assuming a historical control ORR of 30% and an alternative hypothesis of an ORR of 55%, a sample size of 31 patients would provide 80% power to detect a 95% confidence interval (CI) with a lower bound ≥30% in ORR with a two-sided level of significance of 0.05. Considering a 10% dropout rate, 34 patients were planned to be recruited. The safety analysis set included all patients who received at least one dose of DV or toripalimab. All patients were included for analysis of antitumor activity. ORR and DCR were calculated using the Clopper–Pearson method with 95% CIs. PFS, DOR, and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and corresponding two-sided 95% CIs were calculated using the Greenwood formula. Fisher’s exact test was used for evaluating nominal variables. SAS version 9.4 (SAS institute Inc., Cary, NC) was used for all statistical analyses. The study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov with the identifier NCT04264936. Results Patient disposition and baseline characteristics Between August 2020 and December 2021, 41 patients were enrolled, including 6 patients in the escalation phase and 35 patients in the expansion phase (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.12.002). The median age was 66 years, with 70.7% of patients having an ECOG PS of 1, and 58.5% with upper tract UC. Liver metastases were present in 10 patients (24.4%). HER2 expression was IHC 2+/3+ in 24 patients (58.5%; Table 1) and positive PD-L1 expression (22C3, CPS ≥10) was found in 16 patients (39.0%). Additionally, 25 patients (61.0%) were treatment naive as they were either cisplatin ineligible (26.8%) or unwilling to receive chemotherapy (34.1%). By the data cut-off date of 1 March 2024, the median follow-up was 33.2 (95% CI 29.0-37.8) months; 39 patients (95.1%) discontinued treatment, mainly due to disease progression (61.0%) or adverse events (14.6%).
