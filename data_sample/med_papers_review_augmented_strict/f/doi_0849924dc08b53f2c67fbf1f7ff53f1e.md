Available online July 5, 2018. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2018.06.030 An integrated model of alopecia areata biomarkers highlights both T H1 and T H2 upregulation To the Editor: Alopecia areata (AA) is a nonscarring hair loss disorder with a lifetime prevalence of approximately 2%. 1 V arious studies attempted to profile the cytokine pathways involved in the pathogenesis of AA, with a recent shift from T H1/IFN-g axis activation alone to potentially also include TH2s k e w i n g .1-3 A link to atopy has been further observed by genetic linkage to IL-13 in genome-wide association studies,1 increased IgE levels in patients with AA regardless of atopy,4 and increases in TH2-related serum measures (ie, IL-4, IL-13, and CCL17). 5 Nevertheless, extensive serum cytokine profiling, with appropriate correlations to clinical parameters and scalp biomarkers, is lacking. Because scalp tissues can be difficult to obtain, it is imperative to identify blood biomarkers that reflect both clinical severity and scalp expression to aid in therapeutic development. Our study evaluated both serum cytokines and scalp expression of lesional and nonlesional measures in patients with moderate-to-severe AA (n 5 30) compared with age-matched control subjects (n5 10) to integrate circulating and tissue biomarkers that correlate with AA severity (Severity of Alopecia Tool [SALT]). This study included 30 patients with AA (21 female and 9 male patients; age range, 20-72 years old; mean age, 41.8 years old) and 10 age-matched control subjects (see Table E1 in this article’s Online Repository at www.jacionline.org). Three (10%) of 30 patients had an atopic background, and all our control subjects lacked an atopic background. Fourteen (46.7%) of 30 patients had moderate-to-severe AA ( >50% scalp involvement), 14 (46.7%) of 30 had alopecia universalis (AU), and 2 (6.6%) of 30 had alopecia totalis (A T). Severity was assessed by using the SALT score, 6 ranging from 50 to 100 (mean, 87.8). Blood, lesional, nonlesional, and control scalp biopsy specimens were obtained under an Institutional Review Board–approved protocol (see the Methods section in this article’s Online Repository at www.jacionline.org for details). Electrochemiluminescence (ECL) immunoassays, which have greater sensitivity than standard ELISAs, were used in a multiplex format (Meso Scale Discovery, Rockville, Md) to analyze 9 serum chemokines (CCL2/CCL3/CCL4/CXCL10 for T H1 and CCL11/CCL13/CCL17/CCL22/CCL26 for T H2) and the cytokine IL-15. Because serum IFN- g and IL-13 cytokine levels are very low, we used Erenna immunoassays (EMD Millipore, St Louis, Mo), which have much lower quantification levels. RT-PCR was used to analyze mRNA expression of a panel of tissue AA biomarkers, 7 including TH1, T H2, TH17/TH22, IL-32, and hair keratins (see Table E2 in this article’s Online Repository at www.jacionline.org). The Student t test was used for comparisons, and Pearson correlation coefficients were used for correlation of serum cytokine levels with SALT scores, age, and with lesional and nonlesional mRNA expression (see Figs E1 and E2 and Tables E3-E5 in this article’s Online Repository at www.jacionline.org). A P value of less than .05 was considered significant (see the Methods section in this article’s Online Repository for details). We found significantly increased levels of T-cell/natural killer cell activation marker (IL-15), T H1/IFN-g–related markers (CCL2, CCL3, and CXCL10), and T H2–related markers (IL-13, CCL13, CCL17, CCL22, and CCL26) in sera from AA patients compared with sera from healthy subjects ( P 50), and insufficient patient subsets for disease stratification. Future studies should investigate larger populations and different AA phenotypes, including those related to duration, scalp involvement, ethnicity, and pediatric cohorts. Teresa Song, BSa,b Ana B. An oral food challenge (OFC), especially a double-blind, placebo-controlled OFC, is said to be the most reliable test for the accurate diagnosis of FA; however, it is inevitably accompanied by ambiguous subjective symptoms and false-positive reactions. In addition, interpretation of these symptoms can be influenced by observer bias. 3 It is essential to identify biomarkers that can detect allergic reactions caused by an OFC objectively. Serum concentrations of mast cell mediators, including platelet-activated factor, histamine, and tryptase, tend to be increased during anaphylaxis. 4,5 In particular, serum tryptase seems to be a useful biomarker and was reported to peak within 2 hour after onset of anaphylaxis. 5 From a practical point of view, blood sampling from children when they receive an OFC is not easy to carry out in routine clinical settings. Recently, we discovered that levels of 11,15-dioxo-9 a- hydroxy-,2,3,4,5-tetranorprostan-1,20-dioic acid (PGDM), a major prostaglandin D 2 metabolite in urine, were correlated with the severity of symptoms in an oral allergen–induced FA mouse model. 6 The aim of the present study was to verify the usefulness of urinary PGDM as a practical biomarker for assessing the severity of acute allergic reactions in human children with FA during OFCs. Children with suspected FA who were scheduled to undergo OFCs at the Division of Allergy, National Center for Child Health and Development, were recruited. OFCs were applied to them, as described in detail in the Methods section in this article’s Online Repository at www.jacionline.org. The severity of the immediate allergic reactions was classified into grades 1 to 5 by using clinical criteria for the diagnosis of anaphylaxis published by Sampson. 7 Patients without clinical symptoms were categorized as having grade 0 reactions. Urine samples were collected before the OFC and at 2, 4, 6, 8, and 18 hours after the OFC. Urinary PGDM levels were measured by using liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), and the concentration of urinary PGDM was expressed as nanogram per milligram of creatinine. 6 This study received ethical approval from the institutional ethics committee before patient recruitment. METHODS Patients and samples This study included 30 patients with moderate-to-severe AA (21 female and 9 male patients; age range, 20-72 years old; mean age, 41.8 years old) and 10 age-matched control subjects (6 female and 4 male subjects; age range, 23- 55 years old; mean age, 37 years old). Patients’demographics are shown in Table E1. Fourteen (46.7%) patients had patchy alopecia, 14 (46.7%) patients had AU, and 2 (6.6%) patients had A T. All our patients had greater than 50% scalp involvement, and all patients had to have an AA duration of greater than 6 months and up to 10 years (mean, 5 years). Three (10%) of 30 had a history of an atopic background. None of the healthy control subjects had any atopic or autoimmune personal or familial background. Blood (n 5 30) and lesional (n 5 30) and nonlesional (n 5 15) scalp tissue (from patients without total scalp involvement) were obtained from all 30 patients under institutional re- view board–approved protocols, and severity was assessed based on SALT E1 scores, ranging from 50 to 100 (mean, 87.8). Blood from 10 healthy control subjects was obtained for comparison. Serum cytokine levels were analyzed, and univariate Pearson correlations were performed to analyze correlations between serum cytokine levels and SALT scores, lesional scalp expression, and nonlesional scalp expression. Lesional and nonlesional scalp expression was also correlated with clinical severity (SALT scores) by using Pearson correlation. Patients had no evidence of hair regrowth and were excluded if they received phototherapy or systemic modulators within the last 4 weeks before enrollment or topical therapy within 2 weeks before enrollment and biologic agents within 12 weeks before enrollment. Serum immunoassays ECL immunoassays were noted for their superior sensitivity when compared with standard ELISAsE2 and were conducted in a multiplex format to quantify chemokine concentrations in 30 patients with AA and 10 healthy control subjects. Chemokines assessed included CCL2/monocyte chemoat- tractant protein 1, CCL3/macrophage inflammatory protein 1a, CCL4/macro- phage inflammatory protein 1b, CXCL10/IFN-g–induced protein 10, CCL11/ eotaxin, CCL13/monocyte chemoattractant protein 4, CCL17/thymus and activation-regulated chemokine, CCL22/macrophage-derived chemokine, and CCL26/eotaxin-3 and the cytokine IL-15. Biospecific antibodies were spotted at the bottoms of 96-well plates (Meso Scale Discovery) to quantify concentrations of 10 chemokines. After blocking the plates, serum was added for incubation for 2 hours. The plates were then put in the Sector Image Reader (Meso Scale Discovery) to measure their chemiluminescence. Serum levels for IFN-g and IL-13 were lower than detectable levels in ECL, and therefore Erenna immunoassays (EMD Millipore) was used to measure these levels. Erenna immunoassays (EMD Millipore) have a lower limit of quantification that is 10 to 100 times smaller than that of ECL.
