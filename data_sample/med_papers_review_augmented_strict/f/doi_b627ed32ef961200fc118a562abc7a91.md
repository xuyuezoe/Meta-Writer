# Culturally Tailored Social Media Intervention Improves Health Outcomes in Chinese Americans with Type 2 Diabetes: Preliminary Evidence from a Pilot RCT.

## Abstract

Background: Minoritized populations face many barriers to accessing evidence-based diabetes intervention.

Objectives: To evaluate the feasibility, acceptability, and potential efficacy of a social media-based intervention to improve glycemic control among Chinese Americans with type 2 diabetes.

Design: A pilot randomized controlled trial (RCT) with 3-month and 6-month follow-ups.

Participants: Chinese Americans (n = 60, mean age 54.3 years old) with limited education (70.0% with high school or less) and low income (50.0% with annual household income < $25,000), and 88.3% have limited English proficiency.

Intervention: Culturally and linguistically tailored diabetes videos (two videos/week for 12 weeks) delivered via social media and support calls from community health workers.

Main measures: Primary outcomes include feasibility (video watch rate, biweekly call completion rate, and retention rates), acceptability (patient satisfaction), and HbA1c. Secondary health-related outcomes include body weight, BMI, physical activity, and dietary intake. Video watch rate and biweekly call completion rate were assessed at baseline and 3 months, while others were measured at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months.

Results: We observed high feasibility and acceptability of the intervention, with retention rates over 87%, an 89% video watch rate, 80% biweekly phone call completion, and a satisfaction rating of 9 out of 10. The intervention group showed a significantly greater increase in fruit intake compared to the control group (0.15 cups vs. - 0.44 cups, adj_p = 0.023) at 3 months. While no significant differences in other outcomes were observed between the groups, the intervention group showed significant improvements in key outcomes, including reduced HbA1c levels (- 1.08%, adj_p < 0.001), weight loss (- 5.15 lbs, adj_p = 0.004), lower BMI (- 0.83, adj_p = 0.023), and reduced starchy food intake (- 0.33 cups, adj_p = 0.033) at 6 months.

Conclusions: The observed high feasibility and acceptability suggest the intervention's feasibility. However, due to the limited sample size, a larger-scale RCT is warranted to test the efficacy of the intervention.

Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03557697; https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT03557697.

CARE’s retention and intervention adherence rates (i.e., video watch rates and biweekly phone call completion rates) are comparable to those observed in a culturally tailored mobile health intervention targeting Filipino Americans with T2D.28 Notably, CARE’s rates are slightly higher than the retention rates of the ENHANCE study (86.5% at 3 months and 83.1% at 6 months) 11 and surpass adherence reported in some in-person29 and virtual real-time group session30 stud- ies with cultural adaptation targeting African Americans. These studies reported 74% completion of at least six out of seven in-person sessions and a 79% completion rate for real- time group sessions. This potentially stems from CARE’s use of pre-recorded videos. CARE’s short, flexible video modules allow participants to engage with the program at their convenience, potentially enhancing accessibility and contributing to the observed high adherence and retention rates. Given the pilot nature of this trial, we did not observe any between-group differences in most health-related outcomes. These findings align with the ENHANCE trial, where both intervention and control groups experienced reductions in HbA1c, but no significant between-group differences were observed.11 This lack of differences is not surprising given that our goal was to establish the feasibility and acceptability of the CARE intervention and to obtain pilot data to cal- culate the effect size for a future large study. Indeed, the findings of our pilot study are comparable to prior studies in Chinese Americans with T2D. 25,27 For example, Wang and Chan’s (2005) single-group feasibility study among 40 Chinese Americans with T2D observed HbA1c declined from 7.11 to 6.12% before and after the 3 months interven- tion, a similar decrease was observed in our intervention group (~ 1%). 25 While our study achieved similar results, these prior studies employed busy healthcare providers to provide relatively intensive in-person sessions, which can be challenging for many low-income Chinese Ameri- cans to attend. 31 Our pilot study demonstrates that even less resource-intensive interventions can yield measurable improvements in HbA1c levels, providing an important option for settings where labor-intensive programs may not be feasible. This finding underscores the potential of scalable and adaptable interventions to make meaningful impacts in diabetes management, particularly in diverse populations or areas with limited healthcare resources. These findings will also be crucial for informing the sample size calculation for a future, fully powered RCT to assess CARE’s effective - ness and its potential to translate these promising results to a broader population. Strengths CARE’s strengths lie in its innovative and culturally adapted approach, designed to increase access to evidence-based intervention and enhance participant engagement. The inter- vention stands out for its use of culturally tailored video content, such as a grocery store video showcasing healthy alternatives to common Chinese staples like rice. Recogniz- ing the financial and cultural needs of many mid-life or older Chinese Americans, 32 the program emphasizes practical, home-based exercises using household items, eliminating the need for expensive gym memberships. Delivered via the widely used WeChat platform, CARE ensures accessibil- ity and overcomes digital literacy barriers. Our study may represent a first step to reducing the participation burden while maintaining the core content of the program. The small sample size constrained the statisti- cal power necessary to detect significant differences between groups. Yet, this is a proof-of-concept pilot study with the primary goal of establishing the feasibility and acceptability of intervention and obtaining pilot data to calculate effect size. The reliance on self-report questionnaires for data collection could introduce response biases, affecting the accuracy of the reported behaviors and outcomes. In addition, the absence of data on the duration of T2D diagnosis, medication changes over time, and prior exposure to DSMES limits our ability to account for their potential influence on the outcomes, which may affect the interpretation of our findings. CONCLUSION This study provides preliminary evidence that the CARE intervention could be a viable and scalable solution for increasing access to DSMES programs and improving dia- betes management among Chinese Americans with T2D. Leveraging familiar social media platforms facilitated mean- ingful engagement and demonstrated potential for scale-up and dissemination. Our team is now expanding this pilot and testing the CARE intervention in a fully powered ran- domized controlled trial. This project could also serve as a model for other high-risk immigrant populations, such as Hispanic immigrants, who face similar barriers to DSMES access and frequently use platforms like WhatsApp. Figure 2 Changes in HbA1c levels and body weight.
