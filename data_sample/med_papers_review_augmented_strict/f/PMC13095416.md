# Effects of NAD^+ supplementation with oral nicotinamide riboside on vascular health and cognitive function in older adults with peripheral artery disease: Results from a pilot 4-week open-label clinical trial.

## Abstract

Peripheral artery disease (PAD) significantly contributes to increased morbidity and mortality among older adults. Characterized by generalized endothelial dysfunction, PAD is associated with a heightened risk for cerebral small vessel disease and vascular cognitive impairment. Together, these conditions exemplify the phenomenon of accelerated systemic vascular aging, highlighting the interconnections between vascular health and cognitive functions in the elderly. This pilot trial investigated the potential of nicotinamide riboside (NR), a precursor of NAD^+, to counteract age-related vascular and cognitive decline in individuals with PAD. Over 4 weeks, we administered NR to 8 participants and measured its effects on peripheral endothelial function, functional cerebrovascular responses, and cognitive performance (NCT06534944). Additionally, we assessed the effects of circulating factors present in patient sera on oxidative stress and mitochondrial function in cultured cerebromicrovascular endothelial cells. Preliminary results indicate a positive trend in peripheral endothelial function, significant improvements in cerebrovascular response in the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, and cognitive enhancement across multiple domains postsupplementation. NAD^+ supplementation with NR was associated with reduced oxidative stress and increased nitric oxide production and mitochondrial efficiency in endothelial cells treated with patient sera. These findings suggest that NR supplementation could ameliorate cognitive outcomes in older adults with PAD, potentially through mechanisms linked to improved endothelial health and decreased oxidative stress. This study underscores the importance of future research using controlled designs in larger cohorts to verify these effects and elucidate the long-term benefits of NAD^+ enhancement in the context of accelerated vascular aging and its cognitive consequences. SIGNIFICANCE STATEMENT: Despite growing interest in NAD^+ enhancement for vascular and cognitive health, research on nicotinamide riboside (NR) in peripheral artery disease (PAD) is limited. One clinical trial to date has assessed NR in PAD, focusing mainly on walking capacity. This pilot study provides critical evidence by examining peripheral endothelial function, cerebrovascular responses, and cognitive performance. Its multifaceted approach lays the groundwork for future trials to refine effect sizes and validate NR's potential to counter vascular aging and cognitive decline in PAD.

Peripheral artery disease (PAD) is a major age-related vascular condition that significantly contributes to cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in older adults ( Hirsch et al, 2001 ; Criqui et al, 2021 ). PAD is characterized by widespread endothelial dysfunction, which not only affects peripheral arteries, manifesting in symptoms such as intermittent claudication and resting pain ( Zemaitis et al, 2024 ), but also extends to the cerebrovascular system, contributing to vascular cognitive impairment ( Csiszar et al, 2024 ; Nyul-Toth et al, 2024 ). This association is largely due to cerebrovascular and neurovascular dysfunctions that arise from the systemic endothelial impairment seen in PAD ( Waldstein et al, 2003 ; Gardner et al, 2021 ; Owens et al, 2022 ), as discussed in our recent reviews on atherosclerosis and vascular cognitive impairment ( Csiszar et al, 2024 ; Nyul-Toth et al, 2024 ). Importantly, PAD exemplifies accelerated vascular aging, a concept that is crucial for understanding the interplay between vascular health and cognitive decline in this population ( Csiszar et al, 2024 ; Nyul-Toth et al, 2024 ). Accelerated vascular aging in PAD is marked by heightened oxidative stress and inflammation, which exacerbate endothelial dysfunction and are strongly correlated with cognitive and functional impairments ( Waldstein et al, 2003 ; Gardner et al, 2021 ; Owens et al, 2022 ).

One of the hallmarks of aging is the decline in NAD + levels, an essential cofactor for cellular metabolism, energy production, and survival ( Belenky et al, 2007 ; Massudi et al, 2012 ; Gomes et al, 2013 ; Yoshino et al, 2018 ; A. E. Kane et al, preprint, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.06.21.599604 ). NAD + and its phosphorylated form, NADP, are required by over 400 enzymes to carry out redox reactions vital for cellular function ( Belenky et al, 2007 ). Additionally, sirtuins—a family of NAD + -dependent deacetylases—rely on NAD + as a substrate to remove acetyl groups from proteins, including histones ( Imai, 2016 ). This deacetylation process is crucial for regulating gene expression, DNA repair, and mitochondrial function, all of which are key to maintaining cellular health and longevity ( Imai, 2016 ). Because NAD + levels decline with age, the activity of sirtuins diminishes, contributing to a reduction in the function of various organ systems, including the vascular endothelium ( Das et al, 2018 ; Csiszar et al, 2019 ; Tarantini et al, 2019 ; McReynolds et al, 2020 ).

Given the role of NAD + in maintaining vascular health, restoring NAD + levels has emerged as a promising strategy to counteract age-related endothelial dysfunction ( Das et al, 2018 ; Csiszar et al, 2019 ; Tarantini et al, 2019 ). Preclinical studies, including those from our group, have demonstrated that NAD + supplementation can reverse aging phenotypes in cerebrovascular endothelial cells through mechanisms involving sirtuin 1 activation, mitochondrial rejuvenation, and reduction in oxidative stress ( Kiss et al, 2019a , b , 2020 ; Tarantini et al, 2019 ). This mitochondrial rejuvenation leads to improved endothelial function, as evidenced by enhanced angiogenesis, reduced endothelial production of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS), improved endothelium-mediated vasodilation, and better neurovascular coupling (NVC) responses in aged mice ( Kiss et al, 2019a , b , 2020 ; Tarantini et al, 2019 ). Preclinical studies employing mitochondria-targeted antioxidants provide strong evidence that the reduced mtROS production plays a causal role in restoring neurovascular function ( Gioscia-Ryan et al, 2014 ; Tarantini et al, 2018 ; Freeberg et al, 2023 ; Murray et al, 2023 ).

Current evidence suggests that NAD + levels can be effectively increased in humans through oral supplementation with nicotinamide riboside (NR), a NAD + precursor ( Dellinger et al, 2017 ; Conze et al, 2019 ). This commercially available dietary supplement is well tolerated even in high doses (1000 mg daily) in humans ( Martens et al, 2018 ), with no reported adverse effects after 4–8 weeks of treatment ( Dellinger et al, 2017 ; Conze et al, 2019 ). NR supplementation may offer vascular benefits, such as improved blood pressure and aortic stiffness, although data specific to its effects on cerebrovascular health in patients with PAD remain sparse ( Martens et al, 2018 ; Elhassan et al, 2019 ; Freeberg et al, 2022 ; Jensen et al, 2022 ). Given that PAD represents a state of accelerated vascular aging ( Csiszar et al, 2024 ; Nyul-Toth et al, 2024 ), it is logical that treatments targeting the mechanisms of vascular aging, such as NAD + supplementation, could also be effective in managing PAD. A recent clinical trial ( NCT03743636 ) found that NR supplementation enhances walking performance in patients with PAD ( McDermott et al, 2024 ). However, it remains unknown whether these benefits are associated with improvements in endothelial function and whether NR also positively affects cerebrovascular health.

In this pilot open-label clinical trial, we tested the hypothesis that NAD + supplementation with its precursor NR would improve endothelial function in the peripheral circulation, enhance cerebrovascular hemodynamics, and boost cognitive performance in older adults with PAD ( Fig. 1 ). Additionally, we hypothesized that these beneficial effects would be mediated by the attenuation of endothelial cell oxidative stress and the improvement of mitochondrial dysfunction.

In this open-label, single-arm controlled clinical trial, symptomatic PAD participants were recruited through the Vascular Medicine Program, Department of Medicine at the University of Oklahoma Health Sciences. Participants eligible for this study were required to be patients in the Vascular Medicine Program, Department of Medicine at the University of Oklahoma Health Sciences Center, and meet the following condition: vascular laboratory evidence or angiographic evidence of PAD with a severe intermittent claudication. Individuals were excluded from the study if they did not meet the inclusion criteria outlined earlier and those with pharmacologically uncontrolled conditions, including type 2 diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, hypertension, and major depressive disorder. Current or prior cerebrovascular complications (including large-vessel ischemic stroke with chronic functional impairment) or neurodegenerative diseases were also excluded. Additionally, non-English speakers were excluded because effective communication during study procedures was essential for participant safety ( Table 1 ).

During the consenting process, participants’ medical histories were collected, including a specific inquiry about any cognitive dysfunctions. Additionally, their medical records were reviewed using Epic. However, no cognitive tests (eg, Mini-Mental State Examination or Montreal Cognitive Assessment) were used to evaluate possible cognitive impairment as part of the screening process. None of the participants self-reported cognitive impairment, nor did their medical records indicate any such concerns. Furthermore, our goal was to evaluate individual changes in participants’ cognitive function before and after the intervention, comparing these results using assessments from the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Toolbox.

Several findings indicate that NR is generally safe for the liver and typically does not impact liver enzyme levels. However, in some cases, there have been occasional reports of elevated triglyceride levels associated with niacin, another member of the vitamin B3 family ( Nadalin et al, 2020 ). In our trial, medical records were reviewed as part of the screening process, and this was confirmed by recent laboratory results (within 6 months prior to visit 0) that included kidney function and liver enzymes. If these records were not available, all participants were evaluated for liver and kidney enzymes during screening.

Our clinical study took place from June 1, 2023, to May 30, 2024. We recruited participants through the Vascular Medicine Program, Department of Medicine at the University of Oklahoma Health Sciences Center, targeting 106 individuals with PAD and severe intermittent claudication, who were assessed for eligibility. The first enrollment and consent occurred on July 14, 2023. We aimed to screen and possibly enroll approximately 10 participants per month for this study. This monthly screening goal ensured that we maintained a steady enrollment rate.

To evaluate the effect of NAD + supplementation on cognitive function, endothelial function in the peripheral and central circulation in PAD, both male and female participants were screened and recruited into the 4-week open-label clinical trial with oral NR 1000 mg daily. To avoid the possibility that negative outcomes could be attributed to underdosing, we used 1000 mg/day as a high but realistic dose. Current evidence demonstrates a dose-dependent increase in blood cell NAD + levels in response to NR treatment. Specifically, daily consumption of 1000 mg of oral NR leads to a 42% increase in NAD + levels in whole blood within 2 weeks of treatment initiation, which is then maintained for at least 8 weeks ( Dellinger et al, 2017 ; Conze et al, 2019 ). In addition, this dose has been included in all published safety trials ( Airhart et al, 2017 ; Conze et al, 2019 ) and exceeds the amount delivered by most nutraceutical products. Approximately double the dose administered to community-dwelling adults of similar ages over 12 weeks in a series of studies that reported no adverse events ( NCT02303483 ) ( Dollerup et al., 2018 , Dollerup et al., 2019 , Dollerup et al., 2020 ). Furthermore, 1000 mg of NR was recently used in the only clinical trial involving patients with PAD, where it was shown to improve the primary outcome measure (walking distance) without significant side effects ( McDermott et al, 2024 ). We chose to administer a single dose in the morning because this aligns with how most supplement users take NR and may also mimic the natural circadian rhythm of NAD + availability ( Nakahata et al, 2009 ; Ramsey et al, 2009 ).

All enrolled participants were assessed before supplementation and within 7 days of completion of the 4-week intervention. All participants were instructed to take supplements (3 × 300 mg capsules and 1 × 100 mg capsule) in the morning hours. On the days of baseline and final visits, participants were asked to refrain from drinking caffeinated beverages for at least 6 hours before the assessments. All participants were right-handed.

In conducting this study, all procedures involving human participants were performed in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and/or national research committee and with the 1964 Helsinki declaration and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards. All procedures and protocols were approved by the Institutional Review Board of the University of Oklahoma Health Sciences (IRB# 15457). This study was registered with the clinicaltrials.gov under the NCT06534944 . Written informed consent was obtained from each participant prior to participation.

All participants were enrolled into a daily reminder messaging system (Mosio, Inc) to enhance adherence to the study protocols. In brief, the messaging protocol included daily morning reminders to take the supplement with the request to respond with yes/no to indicate whether they took the study drug that day. If the response was not recorded, a follow-up reminder was delivered every 2 hours until the response was recorded or until 5:00 pm of that day. In addition to the individual responses recorded via an automated messaging system, compliance was evaluated based on the number of pills remaining in the bottles returned to the investigators during the final visit.

All reported adverse events were systematically categorized based on their severity (mild, moderate, severe), seriousness (serious or not serious), relationship to the study procedures (related, possibly related, unrelated), relationship to the study drug (related, possibly related, unrelated), and expectedness (expected, unexpected).

Peripheral endothelial function was measured in the brachial artery using the gold standard flow-mediated dilation (FMD) approach as previously described ( Owens et al, 2023 ). Briefly, blood pressure was measured, and participants were instructed to rest supine for 20 minutes prior to brachial artery insonation. Following cuff placement distal to the antecubital fossa and ultrasound visualization of the vascular intima, 1 minute of baseline brachial artery diameter was recorded. Blood flow was occluded at 50 mm Hg above systolic blood pressure or at a maximum of 200 mm Hg for 5 minutes. After 5 minutes of occlusion, the cuff was released, and changes in brachial artery diameter were measured for 2.5 minutes. FMD% was calculated as previously described ( Owens et al, 2023 ). Briefly, continuous change of brachial artery diameter was sampled at 15 frames per second, and Brachial Analyzer software employed automatic edge detection to determine intimal boundaries for analysis. Automatic quality control was performed by defining a 70% confidence threshold to exclude frames with measurement confidence less than the threshold. Cardiac cycle R-peaks were removed from all frames to eliminate the influence of the cardiac cycle prior to final diameter determination. To calculate the FMD response, the relative maximal diameter change following occlusion is expressed in percentage: FMD% = ([Max diameter – avg. baseline diameter]/[avg. baseline diameter] × 100].

After assessment of macrovascular endothelial function, microvascular endothelial function was evaluated on the contralateral arm using laser speckle contrast imaging ( Csipo et al, 2019 ). In brief, a sphygmomanometer cuff was placed above the left antecubital fossa, and after recording of 60 seconds of stable baseline perfusion, occlusion was performed by inflating the arterial cuff to 50 mm Hg above the current systolic blood pressure for 5 minutes. After the release of cuff occlusion, vascular responses were recorded for 3 minutes using a Perimed PSI System (Perimed). Skin temperature was measured with a noncontact thermometer (Thermoworks TW2) from 95%) to the study intervention. We also monitored compliance by counting remaining supplements at the end of the intervention period. Most participants had 100% compliance with the study prescription. One participant stopped taking the supplement after 1 week due to an episode of diarrhea, and 1 participant consumed a lower dose (300 mg instead of 1000 mg) throughout the entire intervention period ( Table 4 ). The diarrhea episode was the only adverse event reported during the study period. Side effects of NR supplementation are usually mild. A recent study found that participants did not experience any serious side effects even at a higher dose of 2000 mg of NR for 12 weeks ( Dollerup et al, 2018 ), which is an encouraging result for those considering NR at higher doses. Furthermore, 1000 mg of NR was recently used in the only clinical trial involving patients with PAD and was shown to have no significant side effects compared to the control group ( McDermott et al, 2024 ). According to the manufacturer’s brochure, reported possible side effects are temporary and may include nausea, bloating, diarrhea, and skin issues such as itching and sweating. All participants were informed of potential side effects, including diarrhea, during the consent process. Our observations align with previous findings because only 1 participant experienced an episode of mild diarrhea, which was classified as unrelated to study procedures but possibly related to the study drug. The remaining 7 participants tolerated the intervention well and reported no side effects. Data presented in the following subsections were analyzed using both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses.

To determine peripheral endothelial function, we employed the gold standard FMD protocol during insonation of the brachial artery. Out of 8 participants enrolled in the study, 7 successfully completed both baseline and postintervention assessments. One participant was not able to complete these assessments due to not being able to lift their body from the wheelchair to the examination table. In the intention-to-treat analysis ( Fig. 3 A), we found that NR supplementation significantly increased peripheral endothelial function in participants with PAD (2.2% ± 3.6% vs 9.0% ± 7.6%, P = .016, d = 1.14, n = 7). The per-protocol analysis ( Supplemental Fig. 1 A) has also shown a strong trend toward improvement of peripheral endothelial function in participants with PAD after intervention (3.5% ± 5.1% vs 12.0 ± 3.2, P = .063, d = 1.28, n = 5).

To investigate the possible underlying mechanisms preceding functional changes, we assessed the effect of NR-treated patients’ sera on endothelial mitochondria-produced ROS (mtROS), NO production, glycolysis, ATP production rates, oxidative phosphorylation, and mitochondrial respiration in cultured cerebromicrovascular endothelial cells. Out of 8 participants enrolled in the study, only 4 participants were successful with venipuncture procedure before and after completing the intervention. Of these, 3 participants adhered to the study protocol and 1 participant consumed 1 g daily for 1 week and then stopped due to a single episode of diarrhea.

In the intention-to-treat analysis ( n = 4), we found that cerebromicrovascular endothelial cells exposed to serum of study participants after NAD + supplementation with NR produced less cellular H 2 O 2 (0.6 ± 0.01 vs 0.4 ± 0.03, P = .125, d = –8.37) ( Fig. 6 A), evidenced by a decrease in resorufin fluorescence (Fluorescence Intensity/Hoechst). We also found that exposure to NR-treated serum increased the production of NO (5.3 ± 0.36 vs 7.9 ± 1.07, P = .125, d = 2.95) ( Fig. 6 B), evidenced by an increase in DAF-2T fluorescence intensity (Fluorescence Intensity/Hoechst). Exposure to NR-treated serum was also associated with increases in glycolysis (4.7 ± 0.3 [mpH/min/Hoechst] vs 7.3 ± 0.31, P = .125, d = 4.72), nonglycolytic acidification rate (2.3 ± 0.27 [mpH/min/Hoechst] vs 3.2 ± 0.67, P = .125, d = 1.77), glycolytic capacity (8.2 ± 1.85 [mpH/min/Hoechst] vs 11.9 ± 0.52, P = .125, d = 2.04), glycolytic reserve (2.4 ± 0.67 [mpH/min/Hoechst] vs 4.7 ± 0.26, P = .125, d = 3.51), mitochondrial ATP production rate (31.5 ± 48.56 [pmol/min/Hoechst] vs 114.3 ± 13.54, P = .125, d = 1.56), and total ATP production (189.1 ± 59.85 [pmol/min/Hoechst] vs 263.6 ± 14.59, P = .125, d = 1.90), as well as a trend toward increased oxidative phosphorylation (16.2% ± 24.64% vs 53.4 ± 10.07, P = .25, d = 1.35, Fig. 6 , C and D). Finally, exposure to NR-treated serum was associated with increased basal (18.9 ± 2.3 vs 28.9 ± 3.19, P = .125, d = 2.85) and maximal respiration (20.2 ± 2.11 [pmol/min/Hoechst] vs 35.3 ± 2.46, P = .125, d = 2.43), and spare respiratory capacity (1 ± 0.32 [pmol/min/Hoechst] vs 5.4 ± 3.79, P = .125, d = 1.83, Fig. 6 E).

Although all the changes observed in our in vitro experiments were not significant, it is important to note that NAD + supplementation with NR had uniform directionality in these changes, and given the limitations of the nonparametric Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test, the lowest P value that could have been achieved was of .125 for our intention-to-treat analysis and .25 for per-protocol analysis. Overall, these data strongly suggest that NAD + supplementation with NR can improve cerebrovascular endothelial function by improving cerebrovascular metabolism and decreasing oxidative stress, which are vital for optimal cognitive performance.

The major findings of this pilot study suggest that NAD + supplementation with NR in older adults with PAD may lead to notable improvements in both macrovascular and microvascular endothelial function, as well as enhanced cerebrovascular hemodynamics during cognitive tasks and modest improvements in cognitive performance. These benefits appear to be mediated by mechanisms, including reduced oxidative stress, increased NO production, and enhanced mitochondrial function in cerebrovascular endothelial cells.

Our study provides preliminary evidence that NAD + supplementation improves peripheral endothelial function in patients with PAD, a significant finding given that PAD is commonly associated with impairments in both macrovascular and microvascular endothelial function ( Owens et al, 2022 ; Csiszar et al, 2024 ; Nyul-Toth et al, 2024 ). In PAD, this endothelial dysfunction is known to extend beyond the peripheral arteries and penetrate the cerebral microcirculation ( Csiszar et al, 2024 ; Nyul-Toth et al, 2024 ), which is crucial because NVC responses—a key determinant of cognitive function—are largely mediated by endothelium-derived NO ( Tarantini et al, 2018 ). Although studies directly assessing the impact of NAD + supplementation on endothelial function in humans are limited, existing preclinical research supports the potential of NAD + boosters to rejuvenate endothelial cells and improve neurovascular health ( Das et al, 2018 ; Kiss et al, 2019a , 2020 ; Tarantini et al, 2019 ). There is strong clinical ( Mukli et al, 2024 ) and preclinical ( Tarantini et al, 2015 ) evidence linking NVC impairment directly to cognitive decline. Given the critical role of endothelial health in mediating NVC responses, it is not surprising that our previous studies have demonstrated a strong association between peripheral vascular health, NVC responses and cognitive function in older adults ( Csipo et al, 2019 ; Mukli et al, 2024 ). Improving vascular function, therefore, could have a positive impact on cognitive outcomes in this population.

Our data indicate that NAD + supplementation positively influences cerebrovascular hemodynamics, particularly in the LDLPFC, a brain region essential for cognitive tasks. This enhancement in cerebrovascular function is associated with modest cognitive benefits, as observed in our pilot clinical trial. Specifically, NAD + supplementation with its precursor NR demonstrated a positive effect on cognitive performance during a working memory task in older adults with PAD. However, although the NIH Toolbox assessment scores showed a trend toward improvement, they remained below the national average (100). This suggests that although NAD + supplementation may have a beneficial impact on cognitive function in this population, the effect was relatively modest. Although research on the effects of NAD + supplementation on cognitive function in humans is still emerging, early evidence suggests that it could be part of a viable strategy to improve cognitive function in aging ( Orr et al, 2024 ) and various age-related diseases ( Braidy et al, 2018 ). For instance, in aged mice, NAD + supplementation has been shown to rescue neurovascular function and improve cognitive outcomes by enhancing NO-mediated vasodilation and mitochondrial function ( Tarantini et al, 2019 ; Kiss et al, 2020 ). Similarly, in mouse models of Alzheimer disease and vascular cognitive impairment, NR also appears to confer cognitive benefits, likely through the same mechanisms of improved vasodilation and enhanced mitochondrial function ( Gong et al, 2013 ; Zhao et al, 2021 ).

A key mechanism driving these benefits appears to be the transcriptomic rejuvenation of endothelial cells, characterized by the upregulation of mitochondrial and neurovascular genes ( Kiss et al, 2020 ). This rejuvenation leads to improved mitochondrial bioenergetics, reduced oxidative stress, and enhanced endothelial function. Such improvements are particularly important in the context of aging, where endothelial dysfunction and oxidative stress are prevalent and contribute to cognitive decline ( Tarantini et al, 2018 ). The ability of NAD + restoration to rejuvenate endothelial cells is especially critical because it directly affects the health of the neurovascular unit, which is essential for maintaining cognitive function.

Previous research, including our own, has shown that NAD + restoration can lead to the upregulation of mitochondrial genes and rejuvenation of mitochondrial function, which plays a crucial role in reducing mtROS production ( Gomes et al, 2013 ; Das et al, 2018 ; Tarantini et al, 2019 ; Kiss et al, 2020 ). This reduction in oxidative stress is likely a key mechanism underlying the observed improvements in endothelial function and NVC responses following NAD + supplementation with NR ( Tarantini et al, 2019 ). Mitochondria-targeted antioxidants in preclinical studies have demonstrated similar benefits, further supporting the importance of mitochondrial health in preserving vascular function ( Tarantini et al, 2018 , 2019 ; Izzo et al, 2021 ). These findings are paralleled by evidence from human studies linking elevated mtROS levels with endothelial dysfunction, underscoring the critical role of maintaining mitochondrial health to preserve vascular integrity ( Gioscia-Ryan et al, 2014 ; Freeberg et al, 2023 ; Murray et al, 2023 ). Together, these results suggest that the transcriptomic and mitochondrial rejuvenation induced by NAD + restoration not only mitigates oxidative damage but also plays a pivotal role in the functional improvement of endothelial cells, offering a promising avenue for therapeutic intervention in age-related vascular dysfunction.

Moreover, our present and previous studies suggest that NAD + supplementation may enhance cellular energetics in endothelial cells by improving both oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis. This improvement in cellular energetics is also likely linked to enhanced mitochondrial quality because previous research on NAD + precursors has demonstrated their ability to promote mitochondrial biogenesis, increase mitophagy, and improve the overall mitochondrial network ( Fang et al, 2019 ; Lapatto et al, 2023 ; Myakala et al, 2023 ). These mitochondrial enhancements are crucial for maintaining efficient energy production and reducing oxidative stress, which are essential for the proper functioning of endothelial cells and the preservation of vascular health. This is particularly important not only for vasodilatory function but also for energy-dependent processes, such as the maintenance of the BBB. The integrity of the BBB is crucial because its disruption in atherosclerotic vascular diseases is associated with neuroinflammation, leading to white matter damage ( Nyul-Toth et al, 2024 ). Improving cellular energetics could, therefore, help in maintaining both NVC responses and BBB integrity, thereby preventing downstream neuroinflammatory processes and cognitive decline. Future studies should investigate whether NAD + supplementation in humans can improve BBB function, providing further insights into its potential as a therapeutic strategy for mitigating neurovascular dysfunction in aging and vascular diseases.

Overall, our findings indicate that NAD + supplementation with NR holds promise as a therapeutic strategy for enhancing endothelial function in older adults with PAD. This approach may also serve as a potential intervention for preventing or delaying vascular complications, including cognitive impairment, in this vulnerable population. Further research is warranted to confirm these benefits and explore the broader implications of NAD + supplementation for promoting vascular and cognitive health in aging individuals. Importantly, NR is well tolerated by patients, even at high doses, without the flushing, or gastrointestinal and liver abnormalities commonly associated with other NAD + precursors such as niacin ( Miettinen et al, 1969 ; Schwartz, 1993 ; Benyo et al, 2005 ). This favorable safety profile makes NR a particularly attractive option for long-term use in managing vascular health.

Although this pilot trial has several inherent limitations, it also lays a promising foundation for future research. The small sample size, although limiting the generalizability of the findings, provides valuable preliminary data that will guide the design of larger, more extended, and more robust studies with greater statistical power. Encouraged by these initial results, we are motivated to pursue studies with more substantial sample sizes to detect even smaller, yet clinically significant, effects of NAD + supplementation with NR.

The absence of a control group in this study does restrict our ability to definitively attribute observed changes to NAD + supplementation with NR alone because placebo effects cannot be excluded. However, the encouraging trends observed in this open-label design inspire the development of future randomized controlled trials that will rigorously test the efficacy of NR while eliminating potential biases.

The short duration of this trial (4 weeks) provides an important snapshot of the immediate effects of NAD + supplementation but may not fully capture the long-term benefits or potential adverse effects associated with prolonged use. The promising early outcomes observed here justify longer-term studies to assess the sustained impact of NAD + supplementation with NR on vascular and cognitive health in patients with PAD.

Finally, although we relied on surrogate endpoints such as endothelial function and cognitive tests, these measures offer crucial insights into the potential mechanisms of NR's effects. Future studies, informed by these findings, will aim to include a broader array of clinical endpoints to better capture the comprehensive benefits of NAD + supplementation in improving overall cardiovascular and cognitive outcomes in this population.

In summary, this pilot study provides a strong impetus for conducting more complex and comprehensive trials that will build on these preliminary findings. By expanding the scope and rigor of future research, we aim to confirm the potential benefits and fully understand the risks associated with NAD + supplementation in older adults with PAD.
