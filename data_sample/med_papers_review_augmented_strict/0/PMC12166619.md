# Prognostic value of atherogenic index of plasma on the all-cause and causes-specific mortality among patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease.

## Abstract

Background: The atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) has been proposed as a potential predictor of prognosis for atherosclerosis and cardiovascular disease (CVD). However, its prognostic value in patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) remains unclear.

Methods: This study included 4,062 participants with MASLD from the Third National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES III). Kaplan-Meier curves, the Cox proportional hazards regression model, and restricted cubic splines (RCS) were used to assess the associations between the AIP and both all-cause and cause-specific mortality in patients with MASLD. Additionally, subgroup analyses and interaction test were conducted.

Results: During a median follow-up of 311 months, a total of 1821 (44.83%) all-cause deaths, 606 (14.92%) CVD deaths and 313 (7.71%) diabetes deaths occurred. After adjusting for potential confounding factors, the hazard ratios (HR) for the highest tertile (AIP ≥ 0.30) compared to the lowest tertile (AIP < 0.01) were 1.41 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.17-1.68; P < 0.001) for all-cause mortality, 1.49 (95% CI: 1.07-2.09; P = 0.019) for CVD mortality, and 2.27 (95% CI: 1.28-4.01; P = 0.005) for diabetes mortality. Furthermore, subgroup analyses revealed that the highest tertile of AIP was associated with an increased risk of CVD mortality in MASLD patients with diabetes (HR = 2.46; 95% CI: 1.46-4.13; P < 0.001) or obesity/overweight (HR = 2.36; 95% CI: 1.41-3.95; P = 0.001).

Conclusions: The elevated AIP is independently associated with all-cause mortality, as well as CVD and diabetes mortality in patients with MASLD. AIP can be used as a prognostic indicator for patients with MASLD.

## Introduction

Introduction

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is the most prevalent chronic liver disease, and its prevalence is rising globally [ 1 ]. In 2023, a multi-society committee of international experts convened and adopted new terminology to avoid the stigmatizing term “fatty” and to acknowledge its association with a broad spectrum of metabolic abnormalities. The term “steatotic liver disease” (SLD) has replaced “fatty liver disease”, while NAFLD has been renamed as metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) [ 2 ]. MASLD affects over one-third of the adult population worldwide, presenting a significant burden on global public health [ 3 ].

The Atherogenic Index of Plasma (AIP), calculated as the logarithmically converted ratio of triglycerides (TG) to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), has been proposed as a biomarker for assessing atherosclerosis risk [ 4 ]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that AIP is a robust biomarker for predicting diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular diseases [ 5 – 7 ]. Elevated AIP levels also exhibit significant prognostic predictive power in those with diabetes [ 8 ]. Previous cross-sectional studies have found an association between the AIP and NAFLD [ 9 ]. Li et al. found that AIP is a strong independent risk factor for new-onset NAFLD among non-obese populations [ 10 ]. However, limited research has been conducted to investigate the predictive role of the AIP in the prognosis of the MASLD population. This study aims to clarify the associations between AIP and both all-cause and cause-specific mortality among patients with MASLD by linking National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data with the National Death Index (NDI) records.

## Materials and methods

Materials and methods

Data source and study population

This study analyzed data from the Third National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES III, 1988–1994). NHANES III employed a multistage, clustered, and stratified probability sampling strategy to represent the civilian, noninstitutionalized population of the United States. Of 20,050 participants from NHANES III, we excluded individuals based on the following criteria: The exclusions from the study are as follows (Fig. S1 ): (1) lacking ultrasound examination data ( n = 5,253), (2) absence of ultrasound images or presence of ungradable images ( n = 941), (3) failure to meet the hepatic steatosis criterion ( n = 8,840), (4) participants with excessive alcohol consumption ( n = 68), (5) failure to meet cardiometabolic adult criteria ( n = 478), (6) pregnant women ( n = 11), (7) missing data on the AIP ( n = 191), and (8) missing data on mortality and covariates ( n = 206). Finally, 4,062 participants were included. The NHANES study protocol was approved by the research ethics review board of the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS), and all participants provided written informed consent.

Definitions of MASLD

Hepatic ultrasound examinations were conducted to assess hepatic steatosis using a Toshiba Sonolayer SSA-90 A and a Toshiba video recorder [ 11 ]. SLD was diagnosed based on ultrasound findings indicating mild, moderate, or severe hepatic steatosis [ 12 ]. MASLD was defined as the presence of SLD along with at least one of the following cardiometabolic adult criteria, in the absence of other causes of hepatic steatosis or excessive alcohol consumption (≥ 30 g/day for males and ≥ 20 g/day for females). The cardiometabolic adult criteria were defined as follows: (1) body mass index (BMI) ≥ 25 kg/m2 (≥ 23 kg/m2 for Asia) or Waist Circumference > 94 cm for males and > 80 cm for females; (2) Fasting Blood Glucose (FBG) ≥ 5.6 mmol/L (100 mg/dL) or 2-hour post-load glucose levels ≥ 7.8 mmol/L (140 mg/dL) or glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) ≥ 5.7% (39 mmol/L) or a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes or treatment for type 2 diabetes; (3) Blood pressure ≥ 130/85 mmHg or the use of specific antihypertensive medication; (4) Plasma triglycerides (TG) ≥ 1.70 mmol/L (150 mg/dL) or the use of lipid-lowering treatment; and (5) Plasma high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) ≤ 1.0 mmol/L (40 mg/dL) for males and ≤ 1.3 mmol/L (50 mg/dL) for females, or the use of lipid-lowering treatment [ 2 ].

Definitions AIP

The AIP is defined as log10 (TG / HDL-C), with both TG and HDL-C levels expressed in mmol/L [ 4 ]. Subsequently, participants were categorized into three groups based on the tertile levels of AIP: Tertile 1 (T1), AIP < 0.01; Tertile 2 (T2), AIP ≥ 0.01 and < 0.30; and Tertile 3 (T3), AIP ≥ 0.30.

Ascertainment of mortality

Mortality status was determined using a probabilistic match linking NHANES III participants with National Death Index records through December 31, 2019. Follow-up time was defined as the period from the NHANES III examination date to the date of death, the date of loss to follow-up, or last follow-up date, whichever occurred first. Mortality outcomes included all-cause mortality, CVD mortality, and diabetes mortality. CVD mortality was defined as deaths attributed to major cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, as classified by the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Edition (ICD-10) codes I00-I90, I11, I13, I20-I51, and I60–I69. Similarly, diabetes mortality was classified as deaths caused by diabetes, corresponding to codes E10–E14.

Assessment of covariates

The covariates included in this study were age, gender, race, smoking, diabetes, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), BMI, total cholesterol (TC), and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR, calculated using the CKD-EPI creatinine equation) [ 13 ]. Participants were categorized by race into four groups: non-Hispanic white, non-Hispanic black, Mexican-American, and other races. Diabetes was defined by a FBG level of ≥ 7.0 mmol/L, an HbA1c level of ≥ 6.5%, self-reported physician diagnosis, or the use of anti-diabetic medication [ 14 ]. Hypertension was defined as SBP ≥ 140 mmHg, DBP ≥ 90 mmHg, self-reported physician diagnosis, or the use of antihypertensive medication [ 15 ]. Additional details regarding the measurement of these covariates are available on the NHANES website ( https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/index.htm ).

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data, or as median (interquartile range) for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies (%). Kaplan-Meier curves were created to illustrate the survival probabilities based on tertile of the AIP. Weighted Cox proportional hazards regression models were utilized to estimate hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) for the association between the AIP and both all-cause and cause-specific mortality among participants with MASLD. Three models were constructed with incremental adjustment for potential confounders: Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 was adjusted for age, gender, and race; and Model 3 was further adjusted for smoking, diabetes, SBP, DBP, BMI, and eGFR. Schoenfeld residual testing revealed violations of the proportionality assumption in some cases. Consequently, HR were interpreted as weighted averages of time-varying HR across the entire follow-up period [ 16 ] RCS were performed to assess the dose-response relationship between the AIP and both all-cause and cause-specific mortality. Additionally, subgroup analyses and interaction tests were performed based on age (≥ 60 versus < 60 years), gender (male versus female), diabetes mellitus (yes versus no), and BMI (< 25 versus ≥ 25 kg/m2). Statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.1.1), with a significance threshold set at P < 0.05.

During the follow-up period, a total of 1821 (44.83%) all-cause deaths, 606 (14.92%) CVD deaths, and 313 (7.71%) diabetes deaths were recorded. The Kaplan-Meier curves indicated that higher tertile of the AIP exhibited significantly elevated all-cause, CVD, and diabetes mortality compared to the lower tertile groups among the MASLD patients (Fig. 1 ; all P values for log-rank test < 0.001).

## Discussion

Discussion

In this large-scale, population-based cohort study, we investigated the association between the AIP and all-cause as well as cause-specific mortality in patients with MASLD. Our findings indicate that elevated levels of the AIP are independently associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality, CVD, and diabetes mortality in patients with MASLD. Elevated of AIP was associated with higher risk of CVD mortality in MASLD patients with diabetes or obesity/overweight. These results suggest that the AIP may serve as a useful prognostic biomarker in patients with MASLD.NAFLD/MASLD, characterized by fat accumulation in the liver in the absence of excessive alcohol consumption, is the most prevalent liver disease worldwide [ 17 ]. It poses a significant global health challenge due to its potential to progress to liver-related complications, including cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma, as well as cardiovascular disease [ 2 , 18 ]. Consequently, identifying potential prognostic biomarkers is crucial for optimizing clinical management of this population.

AIP, defined as the logarithm of the triglyceride to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (TG/HDL-C) ratio, has been identified as a significant indicator of atherogenic risk and is considered a superior predictor of cardiovascular risk compared to lipids alone [ 19 ]. Previous studies have demonstrated strong associations between elevated AIP and NAFLD/MASLD risk. For instance, Wang et al. found the high AIP group exhibited a 5.37-fold risk of NAFLD in obese participants [ 20 ]. Dong et al. identified AIP as a positive correlate of NAFLD in non-obese individuals across both Chinese and Japanese populations [ 21 ]. Furthermore, Li et al. found that the AIP was a significant independent risk factor for the new onset of NAFLD in non-obese individuals, with a HR of 4.49 for subjects in the highest quartile of AIP [ 10 ]. However, whether AIP could predict the long-term prognosis in MASLD patients has not been reported.

To our knowledge, we are the first study to determine the independent prognostic role of the AIP in the population with MASLD. Participants with higher levels of the AIP showed an increased risk for all-cause, CVD and diabetes mortality. Subgroup analyses indicated that the highest tertile of the AIP was consistently associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality across most subgroups. Cardiovascular disease is a leading cause of mortality in patients with MASLD [ 22 ]. In this study, we found that the AIP was independently associated with an increased risk of CVD mortality in patients with MASLD. Furthermore, our results suggest that the AIP may be a valuable biomarker for identifying MASLD patients at high risk of CVD mortality, particularly among those with concomitant diabetes or obesity/overweight. You et al. reported that AIP was not significantly associated with all-cause mortality or CVD mortality in the general population [ 23 ]. This discrepancy may reflect unique metabolic characteristics in MASLD patients that modify the relationship between the AIP and mortality [ 24 ].

The mechanisms underlying the relationship between the AIP and mortality risk in patients with MASLD remain unclear; however, several contributing factors may be involved. Firstly, the AIP correlates with small dense low-density lipoprotein (sd-LDL) [ 4 ]. The sd-LDL particles are regarded as more atherogenic because they can easily accumulate and penetrate the arterial wall. Additionally, these particles are more susceptible to oxidation, both properties contributing to atherosclerosis and subsequent cardiovascular events [ 25 – 27 ]. Secondly, type 2 diabetes mellitus and obesity are significant metabolic diseases that contribute to the occurrence and progression of MASLD [ 28 ]. Insulin resistance (IR) is a critical factor in the development of these conditions [ 29 ]. IR disrupts insulin signal transduction and metabolic processes in hepatocytes, resulting in metabolic disorders such as hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia, and hepatic steatosis [ 30 – 32 ]. Moreover, IR has been shown to activate pro-inflammatory pathways and increase oxidative stress [ 33 , 34 ]. Consequently, in MASLD patients, hepatic steatosis and chronic low-grade inflammation disrupt normal lipid metabolism, resulting in elevated AIP levels. This atherogenic dyslipidemia further exacerbates hepatic insulin resistance, establishing a self-perpetuating metabolic cycle that contributes to progressive systemic metabolic dysregulation and cardiovascular damage [ 35 , 36 ].

Strengths and Limitations.

The present study has several notable strengths. Firstly, this is first investigation assessing the association between the AIP and both all-cause and cause-specific mortality in patients with MASLD. Our findings indicate a promising potential for the AIP in the management of MASLD patients. Additionally, the large representative sample and prospective study design enhance the robustness of our conclusions. However, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of the study. Primarily, the AIP was recorded only at baseline. Temporal variability in AIP, influenced by lifestyle changes or interventions, may affect its prognostic accuracy [ 37 ]. Future studies should implement longitudinal AIP tracking to refine clinical risk stratification. Secondly, although we adjusted for multiple covariates, residual confounding factors, such as dietary patterns, genetic predisposition, or statin use, might persist and partially explain the observed associations [ 38 ]. Thus, rigorously designed prospective studies accounting for all relevant confounders are essential to validate our findings. Finally, while our study leverages a nationally representative sampling framework, the application of multiple exclusion criteria, particularly those involving missing data, may limit generalizability and external validity. These findings should be validated in broader populations. Moreover, although we identified an association between AIP and mortality risk in MASLD, its clinical applicability across diverse racial groups and geographical settings requires further verification. Thirdly, despite leveraging a nationally representative sampling framework, the application of exclusion criteria, particularly for missing data, may limit generalizability. Therefore, external validation in broader populations is critical. Furthermore, while we identified an AIP-mortality association in MASLD, its clinical applicability across diverse racial groups and geographical settings requires further verification. Finally, although this study focused on AIP as a prognostic marker, future research ought to explore its synergistic value with established liver fibrosis markers (particularly FIB-4 and APRI scores), as these measures provide complementary information for both staging MASLD severity and predicting liver-specific clinical outcomes [ 39 ].

## Conclusions

Conclusions

The AIP exhibited significant correlations with an increased risk of all-cause mortality, as well as CVD and diabetes mortality in patients with MASLD. Our findings demonstrated the application prospect of AIP as a prognostic indicator for MASLD patients.
