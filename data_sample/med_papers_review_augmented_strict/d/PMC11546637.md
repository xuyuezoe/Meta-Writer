# Meta-analysis of platinum chemotherapy combinations with immunotherapy in metastatic urothelial carcinoma.

## Abstract

The therapeutic landscape for metastatic urothelial carcinoma (mUC) has evolved significantly due to the development of innovative combination treatments, including enfortumab vedotin-pembrolizumab (EVP). Despite these advancements, the limited availability of EVP means that platinum-based chemotherapy regimens continue to serve as the primary treatment modality for many patients with mUC. We evaluated the effect of the type of platinum chemotherapy used in combination with immunotherapy (IO) on treatment outcomes in mUC. The meta-analysis showed that cisplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combination and carboplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combination improve progression-free survival compared to platinum-gemcitabine therapy (hazard ratio [HR] = 0.71, 95% CI: 0.62-0.82; P < .0001 and HR = 0.85, 95% CI: 0.73-0.98; P < .03, respectively). However, only the cisplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combination showed overall survival (OS) benefit (HR = 0.80, 95% CI: 0.69-0.93; P < .003). In comparison to the platinum-gemcitabine combination, neither the cisplatin-gemcitabine plus IO nor the carboplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combinations demonstrated an objective response rate (ORR) benefit. In summary, combining cisplatin-gemcitabine with immunotherapy offers significant overall survival benefits in mUC. The exact mechanisms-whether cisplatin's immunomodulatory effects or patient demographic differences-are yet to be determined, necessitating further research to understand these outcomes better.

## Background

Background

The prognosis for patients with advanced bladder cancer remains poor; the 5-year survival rate drops to 5% when metastasis occurs. 1 Urothelial carcinoma comprises approximately 90% of all bladder cancers in Europe and the United States. 2 Platinum-based chemotherapy has been the cornerstone for the treatment of metastatic urothelial cancer (mUC) for decades. While cisplatin and carboplatin can be used interchangeably in some cancers, cisplatin is recommended as the preferred agent in patients with mUC. 1 Although there is a lack of direct comparative data between cisplatin and carboplatin in mUC, cisplatin-based regimens have achieved higher complete response rates and objective response rates (ORR) compared to carboplatin-based regimens. 2 However, it is worth noting that patients with mUC often present with comorbidities, including renal dysfunction. As a result, a significant portion, ranging from 30% to 50% of patients, may be ineligible for cisplatin-based therapy and thus receive carboplatin instead. 3 With the advent of new combination therapies overall survival of urothelial cancer has been improved. In the Javelin Bladder 100 trial, avelumab maintenance showed a progression-free survival (PFS) benefit (5.5 vs 2.1 months) and overall survival (OS) benefit (23.8 vs 15 months) in patients who responded to platinum-based chemotherapy (at least stable disease) compared to best supportive care, regardless of the platinum agent used. 4 Recently, enfortumab vedotin-pembrolizumab (EVP) regimen has demonstrated a notable improvement in PFS and OS outcomes, extending PFS to a median of 12.5 months from 6.3 months and OS to a median of 31 months from 16 months, when compared to standard platinum-based chemotherapy for first-line treatment of mUC. 5 EVP benefit was consistent in subgroups. 5 Despite receiving rapid approval from regulatory authorities and beginning to be incorporated into clinical guidelines, it is pertinent to acknowledge that the high cost associated with the EVP combination may limit its accessibility in the near term. This economic consideration is an essential factor in the broader adoption and patient access to this promising therapeutic option. Concurrently reported results from the CheckMate 901 trial elucidate that the combination of cisplatin-gemcitabine and nivolumab yields a significant advantage in both PFS (7.9 vs 7.6 months) and OS (21.7 vs18.9 months) when compared to the cisplatin-gemcitabine regimen alone in the treatment of mUC. 6 With a 63% complete response rate, the ORR in LN disease reached 81%. Cisplatin-gemcitabine and nivolumab combination induced durable disease control and improved PFS (30.5 vs 8.8 months) and OS (46.3 vs 24.9 months) in the LN disease subgroup. 7 Meanwhile, platinum-based chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab or atezolizumab regimens failed to show OS benefit in Keynote 361 and IMvigor130 trials. 8 , 9 Unlike the CheckMate 901 trial, cisplatin-ineligible patients were also included in these trials. 6 To evaluate the differential impact of cisplatin versus carboplatin, when combined with immunotherapy, on the therapeutic outcomes of mUC, we conducted a meta-analysis of published randomized phase III trials.

## Methods

Methods

We performed a meta-analysis using the updated data from phase III randomized clinical trials, which compare the combination of IO and platinum-based chemotherapy against only platinum-based chemotherapy in the first-line treatment of unresectable locally advanced or mUC (la/mUC). The primary endpoints were PFS and OS, and the secondary endpoint was ORR. The meta-analysis used the generic inverse-variance method under a random-effects model to assess the risks of PFS and OS, using the hazard ratio (HR) along with its 95% confidence interval (CI). Additionally, response rates were evaluated using the Mantel-Haenszel method within a random-effects model, employing the odds ratio (OR) and its 95% CI. All statistical analyses were performed using Review Manager software version 5.3. Statistical significance thresholds were set at .05 for overall effect tests and .10 for heterogeneity tests. Heterogeneity among studies was quantified using the I 2 coefficient.

## Results

Results

A total of 2162 patients (1106 patients in experimental arms) with la/mUC were included in this meta-analysis. 6 , 8 , 9 Both carboplatin and cisplatin-eligible patients were included in the Keynote 361 and IMvigor130 trials. 8 , 9 However, the CheckMate 901 trial enrolled only cisplatin-eligible patients. 6 The baseline characteristics of the included trials are shown in Supplementary Table S1 . The meta-analysis of the studies showed that ( Figure 1 ) cisplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combination was associated with a 29% risk reduction in disease progression or death compared to cisplatin-gemcitabine therapy (HR = 0.71, 95% CI: 0.62-0.82; P < .0001). Similarly, the carboplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combination showed a PFS advantage compared to carboplatin-gemcitabine therapy (HR = 0.85, 95% CI: 0.73-0.98; P < .03). Cisplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combination was associated with OS gain (HR = 0.80, 95% CI: 0.69-0.93; P < .003). Contrarily, there was no OS benefit with carboplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combination (HR = 0.87, 95% CI: 0.75-1.00; P < .06). Compared to the platinum-gemcitabine combination, neither the cisplatin-gemcitabine plus IO nor the carboplatin-gemcitabine plus IO combinations demonstrated an ORR benefit.

## Discussion

Discussion

Cisplatin and carboplatin have a similar mechanism of action. They bind to DNA to form platinum-DNA adducts that disrupt DNA function and prevent DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis, initiating a DNA-damage recognition response that results in apoptosis. Traditionally, chemotherapeutic agents have been believed to induce an immunosuppressive environment. Recently, some studies showed that some antineoplastic agents create a proinflammatory environment. 10-13 One of these agents, cisplatin, has been demonstrated to generate immunomodulatory effects through multiple mechanisms. 14 , 15 In nasopharyngeal cancer treated with the cisplatin-gemcitabine combination, there was a decrease in the levels of immunosuppressive myeloid cells, terminally differentiated CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, and TGF-β1, while the levels of CD3+ T cells, interleukin (IL)-1, IL-2, IL-5, IL-6, and IL-8 significantly increased. These results suggest an immunomodulatory effect of the cisplatin-gemcitabine combination. 13 Cisplatin increases the levels of calreticulin, major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC-I), and T-cell infiltration in ovarian cancer models, thereby enhancing tumor immunogenicity. 12 Furthermore, cisplatin upregulates the expression of programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) in these models. 12 , 16

The immunomodulatory effects of cisplatin in urothelial cancer were investigated using biospecimens obtained from participants enrolled in the IMvigor130 study. Cisplatin-gemcitabine increased the expression of immune-related genes associated with nuclear factor-kB (NF-kB) signaling, IL-2 signaling, and interferon (IFN) signaling pathways. 17 These changes were not observed in carboplatin-gemcitabine treatment. Specifically, gemcitabine-cisplatin treatment led to significant upregulation of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I and II genes in circulating monocytes. 17 These findings suggest that cisplatin-gemcitabine enhances gene expression related to antigen presentation and T-cell priming. Modulation of the tumor microenvironment by cisplatin may increase the effectiveness of IO and make cisplatin a better partner than carboplatin in combination with immunotherapy. Adding atezolizumab to the cisplatin-gemcitabine treatment enhanced the expression of more immune-related transcriptional programs in more diverse circulating immune cell subsets than gemcitabine-cisplatin alone. 17 The cisplatin-gemcitabine plus atezolizumab combination showed a more common and broad upregulation of genes related to NF-kB, IL-2, and inflammatory responses compared to the carboplatin-gemcitabine plus atezolizumab combination. Additionally, an increase in the expression of several gene signatures related to T-cell activation was observed with cisplatin-gemcitabine plus atezolizumab but not with carboplatin-gemcitabine plus atezolizumab. 17

However, it is important to acknowledge certain limitations in our study, primarily the absence of individual-level patient data. This limitation prevents us from conducting more detailed subgroup analyses and personalized treatment recommendations based on specific patient characteristics. There may be variations in comorbidities across patient groups, with those in the carboplatin arm potentially having more comorbidities than those in the cisplatin arm. This discrepancy could impact the results of the study. Further research efforts should aim to incorporate individual patient-level data and conducted with matched groups will be needed to compare the results. On the other hand, this was an indirect comparison of the cisplatin and carboplatin-based combinations. It is difficult to compare the direct effects of cisplatin and carboplatin further, thus ignoring the primary effects of cisplatin and carboplatin in urothelial carcinoma.

In conclusion, although this meta-analysis suggests that the combination of IO and a cisplatin-based chemotherapy backbone appears to be more effective, there is a lack of direct data that compares the efficacy of cisplatin and carboplatin-based regimens in combination with IO. To clarify, we need more research comparing the IO combination with cisplatin- and carboplatin-based chemotherapy regimens. This effect may be attributed to the immunomodulatory effect of cisplatin. The clinical implications of these results underscore the necessity for a strategic selection of chemotherapy backbones in immunotherapy regimens for mUC, promoting cisplatin as the preferable option when clinically feasible. Future investigations should focus on unraveling the specific immunological mechanisms activated by cisplatin that contribute to enhanced therapeutic outcomes, thereby informing the development of more effective, tailored treatment paradigms for mUC.
