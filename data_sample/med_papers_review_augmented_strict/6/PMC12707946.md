# Rechallenge with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Hepatocellular Carcinoma: A Narrative Review.

## Abstract

Background: Rechallenge with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has recently emerged as a potential therapeutic strategy for patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who discontinue initial immunotherapy due to disease progression, immune-related adverse events (irAEs), or treatment completion. However, there are no standardized rechallenge regimen, patient indications, and few studies on its mechanism.

Summary: This review provided a comprehensive, up-to-date summary on the clinical evidence, treatment regimens, patient characteristics, and biological rationale underlying ICI rechallenge for HCC patients. Current studies have identified four main rechallenge strategies according to the combinations of agents used in the initial and rechallenge treatments, most of which involve targeted therapy combined with anti-PD-L1 or dual ICIs. Across published studies, ICI rechallenge has shown variable but notable antitumor activity with an acceptable safety profile. Clinical benefits appear to be more frequently observed in HCC patients with preserved liver function, age <60 years, and lower tumor burden, although these findings require cautious interpretation due to interstudy heterogeneity and potential selection bias. Mechanistic investigations suggest that renewed immune activation may result from immunogenic cell death, tumor microenvironment remodeling, and reexpression of inhibitory checkpoints such as PD-L1 or CTLA-4, thereby restoring antitumor immunity.

Key messages: ICI rechallenge represents a rational and feasible therapeutic option for selected HCC patients, providing an opportunity to achieve additional clinical benefit after initial ICI resistance or discontinuation. Although the frequency of irAEs may increase, most events remain manageable with vigilant monitoring and timely intervention. Future research should focus on optimizing regimen selection, refining predictive biomarkers, and elucidating the molecular basis of immune reactivation to guide individualized ICI rechallenge strategies and expand their clinical applicability.

## Introduction

Introduction

Primary liver cancer ranks as the sixth most prevalent malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [ 1 ]. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the predominant histological subtype, accounting for 75–85% of primary liver cancer [ 2 ]. Clinically, more than 50% of HCC patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, thereby excluding them from potentially curative therapeutic interventions, such as surgical resection or liver transplantation [ 3 – 5 ]. For this patient population, current treatment paradigms primarily rely on systemic therapy, particularly molecular targeted agents and immunotherapy [ 6 , 7 ].

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) has emerged as a cornerstone treatment for advanced HCC, primarily through its ability to potentiate T-cell-mediated antitumor immunity and counteract tumor immune evasion mechanisms by blocking key immunosuppressive pathways [ 8 – 10 ]. The current ICI arsenal for HCC management comprises monoclonal antibodies targeting three principal immune checkpoints: programmed cell death 1 (PD-1), its ligand PD-L1, and cytotoxic T lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) [ 11 ]. Since 2017, multiple international phase III clinical trials have consistently demonstrated the therapeutic benefits of ICI treatment in the prolongation of both overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with unresectable HCC ( Fig. 1 ; online suppl. Table 1; for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000549355 ) [ 12 – 22 ].

Despite the prognostic improvement in some HCC patients responding to initial ICI therapy, many responders ultimately required treatment discontinuation due to progressive disease (PD), immune-related adverse events (irAEs), protocol-defined treatment completion, or other reasons [ 23 , 24 ]. For these HCC patients, the available therapeutic options remain extremely limited [ 25 ]. In this context, ICI rechallenge has emerged as a novel investigational approach in HCC immunotherapy, defined as readministration of ICI therapy after either treatment failure or interruption of initial ICI regimens [ 26 , 27 ]. Although many experts have made outstanding efforts in this direction [ 26 ], its application in HCC remains poorly characterized. Currently, no standardized indications or treatment protocols exist for ICI rechallenge in HCC, with clinical decisions being guided primarily by empirical considerations incorporating regional regulatory policies, drug availability, individual patient characteristics, and treatment-related factors. Moreover, molecular mechanisms underlying ICI rechallenge efficacy in HCC progression have not been fully elucidated.

In this comprehensive review, we summarize current clinical evidence and preclinical data regarding ICI rechallenge in HCC, with a particular emphasis on six critical dimensions: therapeutic efficacy, safety profiles, current drug selection, rechallenge timing, patient-centered considerations, and molecular mechanisms. It is the first comprehensive synthesis that merges real-world clinical outcomes, treatment regimens, and cutting-edge mechanistic insights specifically for ICI rechallenge in HCC, thereby offering a roadmap for future trials and biomarker discovery.

## Current Evidence on the Effectiveness of ICI Rechallenge in HCC

Current Evidence on the Effectiveness of ICI Rechallenge in HCC

The existing clinical evidence for ICI rechallenge in HCC comprises 10 retrospective studies, 2 prospective studies ( Table 1 ; online suppl. Tables 2, 3), and 11 case reports (online suppl. Tables 4–6), collectively encompassing 383 HCC patients who had prior ICI exposure [ 28 – 50 ]. These studies were published between 2021 and 2025, originated from multiple geographic regions, including China ( n = 4) [ 29 – 31 , 34 ], Japan ( n = 5) [ 35 – 39 ], the USA ( n = 1) [ 32 ], Germany ( n = 1) [ 33 ], and one international multicenter investigation [ 28 ]. Among these HCC patients, 4 (1.1%) were at Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage A, 81 (21.1%) at stage B, 259 (67.6%) at stage C, 6 (1.6%) at stage D, and 33 (8.6%) had no staging information. The interruption causes of their initial ICI treatment included PD in 247 (64.5%) patients, adverse event in 14 (3.7%) patients, other reasons in 9 (2.3%) patients, and unreported reasons in 113 (29.5%) patients (online suppl. Fig. 1). The ICI rechallenge was administered as second-line treatment in 135 (35.2%) HCC patients, ≥third-line treatment in 150 (39.2%) patients, and unreported line in 98 (25.6%) patients. In these studies, the objective response rate (ORR) of the initial ICI treatment was 20.3% ( Fig. 2 a; online suppl. Table 2) [ 28 , 32 , 33 , 35 , 37 , 38 ], the disease control rate (DCR) was 69.0% [ 28 , 32 , 33 , 35 , 37 , 38 ], and the median PFS (mPFS) was 3.7–5.4 months ( Fig. 2 b) [ 28 , 32 ]. The ICI rechallenge achieved an ORR of 13.8% ( Fig. 2 c; online suppl. Table 3) [ 28 – 38 ], DCR of 50.7% [ 28 – 38 ], mPFS of 2.0–5.6 months ( Fig. 2 d) [ 28 – 34 , 36 – 38 ], and median OS of 7.4–16.7 months ( Fig. 2 e) [ 28 – 34 ]. Overall, HCC patients receiving the ICI rechallenge had lower ORR, lower DCR, and shorter mPFS than those receiving the initial ICI treatment. This reduced efficacy may be attributed to the higher tumor burden and poorer performance status at the time of rechallenge, both of which are associated with resistance to ICI therapy [ 51 , 52 ]. Nevertheless, we should not ignore the fact that these data represent an overall view of pooled data from multiple studies, and no optimal indication and treatment strategy of ICI rechallenge for HCC were utilized for these studies.

Notably, several clinical studies have reported enhanced antitumor responses following ICI rechallenge compared to initial immunotherapy. Wong et al. [ 31 ] reported that while the best overall response (BOR) to initial ICI treatment was PD, either complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) to ICI rechallenge was observed in a subset of HCC patients. Similarly, Scheiner et al. [ 28 ] observed superior ORR with rechallenge versus initial ICI therapy (26% vs. 22%), including 11 PR cases among 21 patients who had previously progressed on the initial ICI treatment. The therapeutic potential of ICI rechallenge was further substantiated by Alden et al. [ 32 ], reporting a markedly higher ORR with ICI rechallenge (22%) compared to initial ICI therapy (6%), with 4 out of 18 PD patients achieving objective response. Similarly, Roessler et al. [ 33 ] documented cases in which patients with only stable disease (SD) during initial ICI treatment subsequently achieved CR or PR upon rechallenge. Suzuki et al. [ 39 ] documented 2 cases that exhibited SD and PD, respectively, during initial ICI treatment, but subsequently achieved PR upon ICI rechallenge. These findings are also supported by evidence from case reports with 3 of 4 documented PD patients showing PR to ICI rechallenge therapy [ 41 , 45 , 46 ]. Collectively, these observations position ICI rechallenge as a viable salvage strategy for selected HCC patients who develop acquired resistance to initial immunotherapy, extending analogous findings reported in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), melanoma, esophageal squamous carcinoma, and metastatic renal cell carcinoma [ 53 – 56 ].

## Safety of ICI Rechallenge in HCC Patients

Safety of ICI Rechallenge in HCC Patients

The safety profiles of initial ICI treatment and rechallenge in HCC patients are shown in Figure 3 and online supplementary Tables 2 and 3. Among these studies, the percentages of HCC patients with irAEs and grade ≥3 irAEs in HCC patients during initial ICI treatment were 38.0% and 8.0%, respectively [ 28 , 32 , 33 , 35 , 37 , 38 ]; and those during ICI rechallenge were 54.1% and 19.5%, respectively [ 28 – 33 , 35 , 37 , 38 ]. This pattern of increased irAE frequency with ICI rechallenge aligns with observations across other malignancies [ 57 , 58 ]. The increased frequency of irAEs may be associated with prolonged use and repeated administration of ICI therapy [ 31 ]. Notably, the types of irAEs during ICI rechallenge were comparable with initial ICI treatment. Specifically, the 5 most common irAEs during initial ICI treatment were skin irAEs (10.6%), enteritis (9.2%), hypothyroidism (4.3%), arthritis (3.6%), and hepatitis (2.1%); the 4 most common grade ≥3 irAEs during initial ICI treatment were enteritis (1.4%), hepatitis (0.7%), skin irAEs (0.7%), and pneumonia (0.7%) [ 28 , 32 , 33 , 35 , 37 ]. Similarly, the 5 most common irAEs during ICI rechallenge were skin irAEs (17.3%), enteritis (8.7%), hepatitis (6.5%), hypothyroidism (3.0%), and arthritis (3.0%); the 5 most common grade ≥3 irAEs during ICI rechallenge were enteritis (3.0%), hepatitis (3.0%), skin irAEs (2.2%), pneumonia (1.3%), and hypothyroidism (0.4%) [ 28 , 29 , 31 – 33 , 35 , 37 ].

Despite a higher incidence of irAEs and grade ≥3 irAEs, ICI rechallenge was associated with fewer fatal irAEs. There was only 1 (0.3%) fatal irAE case across the 12 clinical studies and 11 case reports on ICI rechallenge [ 34 ], which was significantly lower than 3.1% following initial ICI treatment [ 59 ]. In practice, most irAEs occurring during ICI rechallenge can be effectively controlled through ICI discontinuity, systemic corticosteroid therapy, and other therapeutic approaches [ 28 , 32 , 35 , 37 ]. However, a caution is warranted when interpreting these findings, as these results may be influenced by selection bias, shorter follow-up periods, and smaller cohorts. For example, patients who experienced severe irAEs tended not to be readministered, suggesting that patients in the study population may tolerate continuing ICI therapy. Furthermore, greater attention should be paid to safety in clinical practice. Vigilant monitoring and proactive management are strongly recommended to enable timely recognition and effective control of irAEs, which is critical for optimizing clinical decision-making and improving patient outcomes.

## Therapeutic Regimens for ICI Rechallenge in HCC

Therapeutic Regimens for ICI Rechallenge in HCC

Drug Selection of ICI Rechallenge Regimens

In these 12 clinical studies, 378 (98.7%) HCC patients were treated with combined regimens for ICI rechallenge, and the remaining 5 (1.3%) patients received ICI rechallenge with a single immunotherapeutic agent of durvalumab, pembrolizumab, or ipilimumab [ 28 – 38 ]. According to the drug selection of initial ICI treatment and ICI rechallenge, there were 4 combination strategies in the currently reported ICI rechallenge studies ( Table 1 ): (1) combination of targeted therapy and anti-PD-L1 for patients with initial anti-PD-1 immunotherapy ( n = 138, 36.0%) [ 28 – 30 ], (2) combination of anti-CTLA-4 ipilimumab and anti-PD-1 nivolumab for patients with initial targeted and anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy ( n = 100, 26.1%) [ 31 – 34 ], (3) combination of anti-CTLA-4 tremelimumab and anti-PD-L1 durvalumab for patients with initial targeted bevacizumab and anti-PD-L1 atezolizumab immunotherapy ( n = 132, 34.5%) [ 35 – 38 ], and (4) combination of targeted and anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy after resistance to the same initial treatment ( n = 13, 3.4%) [ 39 ].

Table 3), and the incidences of irAEs and grade ≥3 irAEs were 60.9% and 18.8%, respectively ( Fig. 3 c, d; online suppl. Table 3). In addition, there was 1 reported HCC case, who achieved CR to ICI rechallenge with targeted bevacizumab and anti-PD-L1 atezolizumab after the patient failed to respond to initial anti-PD-1 pembrolizumab treatment [ 42 ]. Together, these findings indicate that for certain HCC patients who received anti-PD-1 monotherapy as initial treatment, rechallenge with combined targeted therapy and anti-PD-L1 therapy confers clinical benefit with a manageable safety profile.

In these studies, all 132 patients received a combination therapy of anti-CTLA-4 tremelimumab and anti-PD-L1 durvalumab after initial targeted bevacizumab and anti-PD-L1 atezolizumab (online suppl. Tables 2, 3). The ORR, DCR, and mPFS of the ICI rechallenge for 132 HCC patients were 6.8%, 40.2%, and 2.0–4.0 months, respectively ( Fig. 2 c–e; online suppl. Table 3). The percentages of irAEs and grade ≥3 irAEs were 51.4% and 24.3%, respectively ( Fig. 3 c, d, online suppl. Table 3). Consistently, there were 2 HCC cases that achieved PR and SD, respectively, following ICI rechallenge with anti-CTLA-4 tremelimumab and anti-PD-L1 durvalumab after initial targeted bevacizumab and anti-PD-L1 atezolizumab [ 46 ]. This regimen displays a limited efficacy but provides an alternative immunologic modulation. Furthermore, higher toxicity requires careful patient selection, vigilant monitoring, and proactive management.

Regimen 4 shows combination of targeted and anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy after resistance to the same initial treatment [ 39 ]. In the study, all 13 HCC patients received atezolizumab combined with bevacizumab as both the initial and rechallenge regimens (online suppl. Tables 2, 3). The ORR, DCR, and mPFS of the ICI rechallenge were 15.0%, 54.0%, and 4.7 months, respectively ( Fig. 2 c–e; online suppl. Table 3). The percentages of irAEs and grade ≥3 irAEs were 46.0% and 15.0%, respectively ( Fig. 3 c, d; online suppl. Table 3). This is currently the only evidence of ICI rechallenge using the same regimen as the initial treatment for HCC patients. These outcomes suggest that rechallenge with the same combination after an interval may still provide a beneficial effect in some selected patients, though its efficacy appears modest.

Among the 4 strategies, the regimen 1 (targeted therapy plus anti-PD-L1 following anti-PD-1 monotherapy) and regimen 2 (ipilimumab plus nivolumab after targeted and PD-L1 therapy) achieved relatively higher ORR and more favorable clinical outcomes than the regimen 3 (tremelimumab plus durvalumab after bevacizumab and atezolizumab) and regimen 4 (rechallenge with the same bevacizumab and atezolizumab regimen as the initial ICI treatment). Although irAEs were frequently observed across all four regimens, the percentages of severe (grade ≥3) irAEs remained within 15.0%–24.3%, indicating an overall manageable safety profile. Taken together, ICI rechallenge using combination strategies, particularly for the regimen 1 and regimen 2, appeared to offer a more favorable balance between efficacy and safety.

## Biological Rationale of ICI Rechallenge

Biological Rationale of ICI Rechallenge

The mechanisms underlying the action of successful ICI rechallenge are not fully clarified. Based on the mechanisms of ICI resistance and irAEs [ 8 ], we discuss 3 potential mechanisms of action: (1) combination of targeted therapy and anti-PD-L1, (2) switching treatment between anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1, and (3) dual immunotherapy with CTLA-4 and PD-(L)1 inhibitors ( Fig. 4 ). The ICI rechallenge regimen combining targeted therapy and anti-PD-L1 in patients previously treated with anti-PD-1 may involve, at least in part, the first and second mechanisms. The regimen combining anti-CTLA-4 and anti-PD-1 in patients previously treated with targeted therapy plus anti-PD-L1 may depend on the second and third mechanisms. The regimen combining anti-CTLA-4 and anti-PD-L1 in patients previously treated with targeted therapy plus anti-PD-L1, which displayed the poorest response and highest percentage of grade ≥3 irAEs, is likely mediated primarily through the third mechanism.

Biological mechanisms underlying successful ICI rechallenge in HCC. (1) Mechanism of targeted therapy combined with anti-PD-L1: induction of ICD – lenvatinib induces ICD in HCC cells, releasing DAMPs and a greater variety of tumor antigens. This enhances antigen presentation by APCs to T cells, boosting T-cell immunity against HCC cells. Remodeling of TME: lenvatinib, apatinib, or regorafenib remodels the abnormal tumor vasculature into a structure resembling non-tumor tissue. This vascular normalization increases immune cell infiltration, facilitating the ICI-mediated antitumor immunity. Upregulation of PD-L1 expression: sorafenib induces a hypoxic condition in HCC, activating the HIF-1α pathway in HCC cells, which upregulates PD-L1 expression, providing targets for anti-PD-L1 therapy. Lenvatinib also upregulates PD-L1 expression through the MCT1/NF-κB/COX-2 pathway in HCC cells. Cisplatin promotes PD-L1 expression by activating the HGF/c-Met pathway in HCC cells. (2) Switching treatment between anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1: anti-PD-L1 treatment upregulates the expression of PD-L2, leading to resistance to anti-PD-L1 therapy. Switching to anti-PD-1 blocks the interaction between PD-1 on T cells and PD - L2 on tumor cells and APCs, reactivating T cells in certain tumors. Similarly, anti-PD-1 can upregulate the expression of B7-1 on T cells, leading to resistance to anti-PD-1 therapy. Switching to anti-PD-L1 blocks the interaction between B7-1 on T cells and PD-L1 on tumor cells or APCs, relieving T-cell inhibition. (3) Dual immunotherapy mechanism: the expression of CTLA-4 on T cells may be upregulated in tumors resistant to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. Anti-CTLA-4 treatment blocks the interaction between CTLA-4 on CD8 + T cells and B7 molecules on APCs, enhancing tumor-reactive CD8 + T-cell immunity. Anti-CTLA-4 also blocks the interaction between CTLA-4 on Tregs and B7 molecules on APCs, reducing the inhibitory activity of Tregs. Additionally, anti-CTLA-4 treatment ameliorates TME immunosuppression by eliminating CTLA-4-positive Tregs via ADCC. Mechanistic Insights into the Efficacy of ICI Rechallenge Combining Targeted Therapy and Anti-PD-L1

Currently, the most common targeted therapy for HCC is TKIs, including small molecule agents such as sorafenib, lenvatinib, and regorafenib, as well as VEGF-A-targeting antibody of bevacizumab [ 74 ]. Treatment with targeted therapy can restore the sensitivity of immunotherapy through the following mechanisms.

Induction of Immunogenic Cell Death

Targeted therapy can induce immunogenic cell death (ICD) of tumor cells, releasing damage-associated molecular patterns and tumor antigens that activate innate and adaptive immune responses, respectively [ 75 , 76 ]. For instance, lenvatinib treatment induces ICD in HCC cells, leading to the release of damage-associated molecular patterns and a greater variety of tumor antigens. These tumor antigens are presented by antigen-presenting cells (APCs) to activate antigen-specific T-cell immunity against HCC cells [ 77 , 78 ]. However, no report has confirmed whether sorafenib or regorafenib can induce ICD in HCC cells. Additionally, chemotherapeutic agents, such as oxaliplatin, cyclophosphamide, adriamycin, and epirubicin, also induce ICD of HCC cells, enhancing antitumor T-cell responses [ 79 – 81 ].

Remodeling of the Tumor Microenvironment

Lenvatinib, apatinib, and regorafenib target VEGFRs or other angiogenic events of HCC endothelial cells, remodeling the abnormal tumor vasculature into one resembling healthy tissue. This is achieved by pruning immature vascular branches, enhancing vascular wall integrity, and improving vascular permeability [ 82 – 84 ]. This vascular normalization increases immune cell infiltration and provides an immune-cell-enriched tumor microenvironment (TME), enabling ICI to exert antitumor immunity [ 85 , 86 ]. In NSCLC, chemotherapy enhances the activity and number of CD8 + T cells in TME [ 87 ]. However, treatment with oxaliplatin and 5-fluorouracil in HCC significantly reduces T-cell infiltration while increasing myeloid-derived suppressor cell and regulatory T-cell (Treg) infiltration, resulting in an immunosuppressive TME [ 79 , 88 , 89 ].

Upregulation of PD-L1 Expression

Treatment with sorafenib, lenvatinib, or cisplatin upregulates PD-L1 expression on HCC cells, increasing targets for successful ICI rechallenge [ 90 – 93 ]. Mechanistically, sorafenib induces a hypoxic condition in HCC, activating the HIF-1α pathway to upregulate PD-L1 expression in HCC cells, thereby providing targets for anti-PD-L1 to exert antitumor immunity [ 94 ]. Lenvatinib enhances PD-L1 expression through activation of the MCT1/NF-κB/cyclooxygenase-2 pathway in HCC cells [ 95 ]. Additionally, cisplatin treatment promotes PD-L1 expression by activating the HGF/cellular-mesenchymal epithelial transition factor pathway in HCC cells [ 91 ].

Mechanistic Insights into the Efficacy of Switching Treatment between Anti-PD-1 and Anti-PD-L1

The PD-1/PD-L1/2 immune checkpoint proteins exhibit cell-specific expression patterns: PD-1 is primarily expressed on activated T cells, PD-L1 primarily on tumor cells and APCs, and PD-L2 predominantly on APCs [ 96 , 97 ]. Engagement of PD-1 by PD-L1 or PD-L2 inhibits the activity of T cells [ 98 , 99 ]. A previous study showed that anti-PD-L1 treatment significantly upregulates the expression of the alternative immune checkpoint PD-L2 on tumor cells and APCs, contributing to resistance to anti-PD-L1 therapy [ 100 ]. Switching to anti-PD-1 treatment blocks the interaction between PD-1 on effector T cells and PD-L2 on tumor cells and APCs, thereby enhancing the activity of effector T cells [ 101 , 102 ]. Accordingly, rechallenge with anti-PD-1 in patients initially treated with anti-PD-L1 may help overcome the expression of alternative immune checkpoint PD-L2 in HCC.

In addition, anti-PD-1 treatment upregulates B7-1 (CD80) expression on activated T cells, promoting their apoptosis through the interaction of B7-1 with PD-L1 on tumor cells or APCs [ 103 ]. Alternatively, blocking the B7-1/PD-L1 interaction with anti-PD-L1 treatment can restore antitumor responses [ 3 , 104 ]. Therefore, rechallenge with anti-PD-L1 in patients initially treated with anti-PD-1 therapy may restore antitumor immunity by overcoming the expression of alternative immune checkpoint B7-1.

Furthermore, HCC exhibits a high degree of heterogeneity, leading to varying responses to ICI treatments across different tumor cell clones [ 105 ]. Switching between anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1 therapies may target and eliminate distinct tumor cell clones. This represents another mechanism through which anti-PD-1 treatment can benefit HCC patients initially treated with anti-PD-L1 therapy, and vice versa.

## Future Research Directions

Future Research Directions

Although ICI rechallenge has shown both efficacy and acceptable safety in certain HCC patients, and several mechanisms underlying its success have been proposed, many clinical and scientific questions remain unresolved. Future research should focus on optimizing and expanding treatment regimens, refining rechallenge timing, improving patient selection, as well as developing response biomarkers.

Optimizing and Expanding ICI Rechallenge Regimens

Future expansion of ICI rechallenge regimens in HCC should focus on rationally designed combination strategies that integrate immune modulation, targeted inhibition, and TME remodeling. Beyond the currently reported regimens, several approaches merit further exploration. First, incorporating novel ICIs (e.g., anti-TIGIT, anti-LAG-3, or bispecific antibodies targeting PD-(L)1/CTLA-4) into rechallenge protocols may overcome immune exhaustion and enhance antitumor response [ 115 – 117 ].

Furthermore, integrating tumor stroma modulators into ICI rechallenge regimens may open a new frontier in overcoming immune resistance and achieving durable responses in HCC. For example, TGF-β pathway inhibitors such as galunisertib (LY2157299), SAR439459, and the bifunctional fusion protein bintrafusp alfa (M7824) can alleviate stromal-mediated immune exclusion [ 24 ]. Cancer-associated fibroblast-targeted approaches, including FAP-targeted therapies and BCL-2 inhibitor (navitoclax), may reduce the population of tumor-promoting fibroblasts [ 118 – 120 ]. Extracellular matrix remodeling agents such as PEGPH20 (hyaluronidase) and simtuzumab (LOXL2 inhibitor) can diminish matrix stiffness and enhance drug and lymphocyte penetration [ 121 ]. Moreover, future studies on the effectiveness and safety of ICI rechallenge should investigate the combination of systemic ICI therapy with various local treatments, including radiotherapy, transcatheter arterial chemoembolization, transcatheter arterial infusion, or hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy in HCC patients.

Refining Rechallenge Timing

In NSCLC, the duration of the interval between initial ICI treatment and rechallenge appears not to affect the effectiveness of ICI rechallenge [ 64 , 122 , 123 ]. Additionally, NSCLC patients who did not receive other antitumor therapies during the interval have significantly longer mPFS than those who received other therapies [ 124 ]. The optimal interval time between initial ICI treatment and rechallenge in HCC patients, as well as whether additional antitumor therapy should be administered during this interval, remains unclear. Future well-designed clinical trials comparing different rechallenge intervals and treatment regimens in HCC patients will be essential to establish evidence-based guidelines for optimal rechallenge timing.

Improving Patient Selection

In NSCLC, mPFS at ICI rechallenge was not achieved in patients who discontinued initial ICI treatment due to clinical decision. In contrast, mPFS at ICI rechallenge was 5.2 months in patients who discontinued due to irAEs, and 2.1 months in those who discontinued due to PD. Notably, mPFS in patients who terminated initial ICI therapy due to irAEs was significantly longer than those who discontinued due to PD [ 124 ]. In HCC, Miura et al. [ 35 ] reported that among the 11 patients who discontinued initial ICI treatment due to PD, nearly half had a BOR of PD upon rechallenge, whereas all 5 patients who discontinued due to adverse events achieved a BOR of SD. However, the small sample size in that study limited the reliability of these findings. Based on this evidence, we speculate that the efficacy of ICI rechallenge may differ between HCC patients who discontinue initial treatment due to tumor progression and those who discontinue due to adverse events. Additionally, the patterns of tumor progression, involving existing lesions, new lesions, or vascular invasion, may also influence the clinical benefit of ICI rechallenge therapy for HCC patients [ 26 ]. Future prospective, multicenter, large-scale clinical trials should address these factors to optimize patient selection and treatment design.

Head-to-Head Comparison of ICI Rechallenge and TKIs after Initial ICI Resistance

A recent multicenter phase II prospective study has shown that in patients with a response of PD after treatment with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, treatment with lenvatinib displays a promising efficacy and manageable safety, suggesting that TKIs may represent a potential therapeutic option for HCC patients after developing resistance to primary ICI treatment [ 125 ]. However, in HCC patients, there is no study on strictly “head-to-head” comparing the efficacy and safety of ICI rechallenge versus TKIs, most available data were derived from retrospective or real-world studies, and high-quality randomized controlled trials are still lacking. An international multicenter retrospective study reported that following first-line atezolizumab plus bevacizumab failure, the median post-progression survival for patients receiving ICI rechallenge and those receiving oral TKI monotherapy was 14.9 months and 8.4 months, respectively, although the difference was not statistically significant ( p = 0.25) [ 126 ]. Moreover, the phase III CONTACT-03 trial in renal cell carcinoma reported that ICI rechallenge after immunotherapy failure was not better than TKI monotherapy [ 127 ]. Therefore, physicians should make therapeutic decision on ICI rechallenge or TKI for individual patients, considering prior response, reasons for treatment discontinuation, PD patterns, liver function, and history of adverse events. Conceivably, prospective randomized controlled trials with a bigger population are urgently needed to directly compare the efficacy and safety of these two therapeutic strategies.

Exploration of Potential Biomarkers to Predict the Effectiveness and Safety of ICI Rechallenge

Recent advances in ICI response prediction have uncovered novel biomarker strategies and could be extended to ICI rechallenge. First, lymphatic vessels and tertiary lymphatic structures are crucial for antitumor immunity [ 128 – 130 ]. In melanoma patients, tumor-associated lymphatic vessel density was significantly and positively correlated with the degrees of CD8 + T-cell infiltration in tumors [ 131 , 132 ]. In patients with solid tumors receiving ICI treatment, high tertiary lymphoid structure density is significantly associated with higher ORR and longer mPFS [ 133 , 134 ]. Moreover, mature tertiary lymphoid structures can predict the effectiveness of ICI for solid tumors, independent of the levels of PD-L1 expression in tumor tissues [ 135 ]. However, the predictive value of lymphatic vessels and tertiary lymphatic structures in ICI rechallenge for HCC remains unclear. Second, liquid biopsy biomarkers, such as circulating tumor cells and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), may provide real-time, noninvasive monitoring of tumor load, clonal evolution, and tumor molecular characteristics [ 136 , 137 ]. Prospective studies have shown that early decreases in ctDNA levels predict durable responses to PD-1 inhibitors in melanoma [ 138 ], and in lung cancer, high baseline tumor mutational burden and interferon-γ-related gene signatures correlate with better ICI outcomes [ 139 ]. In addition, mutations in tumor suppressor genes (including TP53, CTNNB1, and TERT), activation of the WNT/β-catenin pathway, or resistance mutations such as B2M deletion can be detected by ctDNA and may guide rechallenge strategies [ 140 – 142 ].

Regarding safety, prospective cohorts in melanoma and renal cell carcinoma indicate that preexisting autoantibodies and certain HLA genotypes may predispose patients to develop severe irAEs, providing potential predictors for toxicity [ 143 ]. Moving forward, integrating spatial biomarkers such as multiplex imaging of tertiary lymphoid structures with longitudinal monitoring through ctDNA may offer a comprehensive approach to predict both benefit and risk of ICI rechallenge in HCC.

## Conclusion

Conclusion

ICI rechallenge represents a rational and feasible therapeutic option for selected HCC patients, providing an opportunity to achieve additional clinical benefit after initial ICI resistance or discontinuation. Although the frequency of irAEs may increase, most events remain manageable with vigilant monitoring and timely intervention. Future research should focus on optimizing regimen selection, refining predictive biomarkers, and elucidating the molecular basis of immune reactivation to guide individualized rechallenge strategies and expand their clinical applicability.
