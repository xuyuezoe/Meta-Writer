# Prehabilitation of frail elderly PAtients undergoing majoR surgEry at HOME (PREPARE-HOME): a superiority parallel-group randomised controlled trial protocol evaluating smart wearable enhanced prehabilitation versus usual care.

## Abstract

Introduction: Frailty is a key predictor of adverse surgical outcomes in older adults, contributing to increased postoperative complications, prolonged hospitalisation and delayed recovery. Prehabilitation-targeting improvements in physical function before surgery-can mitigate these risks. However, traditional programmes often face low adherence due to logistical barriers. Integrating smart wearable devices into tele-supervised, home-based prehabilitation may enhance adherence, engagement and clinical outcomes.This trial protocol describes the PREhabilitation of frail elderly PAtients undergoing majoR surgEry at HOME study with the objective to evaluate the effectiveness of a wearable-enhanced, tele-supervised prehabilitation programme (swSEP) versus standard care (unsupervised prehabilitation, uSEP) on improving preoperative functional capacity and postoperative outcomes in frail older adults undergoing major elective surgery.

Methods and analysis: This single-centre, prospective, randomised controlled trial will enrol 190 patients aged ≥65 years scheduled for major elective, non-cardiac surgery at Singapore General Hospital. Participants with frailty (Edmonton Frail Scale ≥6) will be randomised 1:1 to either the swSEP group (tele-supervised exercise with Fitbit Inspire 3 monitoring) or the uSEP group (standard physiotherapy education, exercise booklet and inspiratory muscle training if maximal inspiratory pressure <100 cm H2O). The primary outcome is change in 6 min walk test distance from baseline to 1-3 days presurgery. Secondary outcomes include 30 s sit-to-stand test, handgrip strength, postoperative complications (per American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program), hospital length of stay, readmissions, five-level version of the EuroQol five-dimensional questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L) and adherence. Data will be analysed using t-tests, analysis of covariance, logistic regression and Cox models, with stratification by baseline nutritional status.

Ethics and dissemination: Approved by the SingHealth Institutional Review Board (CIRB Ref: 2024/2242). Trial registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06633614). Results will be disseminated via peer-reviewed publications and academic conferences.

## Main text

STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS OF THIS STUDY

This is a single-centre, prospective, parallel-group randomised controlled trial with a superiority framework and allocation concealment.

The study incorporates both objective (Fitbit-derived) and performance-based measures (6 min walk test, 30 s sit-to-stand test, handgrip strength) to evaluate functional outcomes.

The intervention is designed for real-world scalability, using commercially available wearable technology and tele-supervision.

Blinding is not feasible for participants and investigators, which may introduce performance bias; however, outcome assessors and hospital staff are blinded where possible.

As a single-centre study, generalisability may be limited, although the inclusion of diverse surgical subspecialties enhances external validity.

## Introduction

Introduction

Background and rationale

Background

Frailty, a condition characterised by decreased physiological reserves and increased vulnerability to stressors such as surgery, is a significant predictor of poor outcomes in elderly patients. 1 Frail individuals are at a higher risk of postoperative complications, prolonged hospital stays and delayed recovery, largely due to their impaired ability to cope with the physical and metabolic demands of surgery. As the global population continues to age, managing the perioperative risks associated with frailty has become an increasingly important challenge for healthcare providers. 2

Prehabilitation, a proactive strategy that seeks to improve a patient’s functional capacity before surgery through physical exercise, nutritional support and psychological interventions, has emerged as a promising intervention to mitigate these risks. By optimising physiological reserves in the preoperative period, prehabilitation aims to enhance recovery, reduce complication rates and decrease hospital length of stay. However, despite its potential, the current evidence supporting the widespread efficacy of prehabilitation remains of low certainty. 3 4 A major limitation in existing studies is the variation in patient adherence to prescribed programmes. Non-adherence, often attributed to logistical barriers such as transportation difficulties, time constraints and the burden of frequent hospital visits, undermines the effectiveness of these interventions. 5 6

One potential solution to overcoming these challenges is integrating wearable technology and remote monitoring into prehabilitation programmes. Smart wearable devices (SWDs), such as smart devices that continuously track physical activity, heart rate and other physiological parameters, can potentially revolutionise prehabilitation by enabling home-based interventions. 7 This approach offers significant advantages over traditional hospital-based programmes. By eliminating the need for frequent hospital visits, patients can perform their prehabilitation exercises in the comfort of their own homes, at times that suit their schedules. Additionally, it gives patients greater flexibility and autonomy, which may improve their adherence and engagement in prehabilitation. 8

Rationale

The PREhabilitation of frail elderly PAtients undergoing majoR surgEry at HOME (PREPARE-HOME) study aims to evaluate whether a home-based supervised exercise programme enhanced with SWDs (swSEP) can improve preoperative physical function and postoperative outcomes compared with the standard unsupervised exercise programme (uSEP). This study will explore the efficacy of smart wearables in delivering real-time physiological feedback, increasing exercise adherence and improving clinical outcomes.

Objectives

Primary objective

To determine whether a smart wearable–enhanced, tele-supervised prehabilitation programme (swSEP) improves preoperative physical function—measured by change in 6 min walk test (6MWT) distance—compared with unsupervised prehabilitation (uSEP) in frail older adults undergoing major elective non-cardiac surgery.

Secondary objectives

To assess the effect of swSEP versus uSEP on additional functional outcomes, including handgrip strength (HGS) and the 30 s sit-to-stand test (30sSTS).

To compare the incidence of postoperative complications, hospital length of stay, ICU admissions, readmissions and reoperations between the two groups.

To evaluate the impact of the intervention on health-related quality of life (HRQoL), five-level version of the EuroQol five-dimensional questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L) at 8 weeks postoperatively.

To examine adherence to the exercise regimen and feasibility of implementing wearable-guided prehabilitation at home.

To explore potential harms, including exercise-related adverse events and challenges in remote monitoring.

## Methods and analysis

Methods and analysis

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not directly involved in the design, conduct or reporting of this trial. The study intervention and materials were developed based on clinical experience, institutional guidelines and relevant literature, with iterative input from multidisciplinary clinicians including anaesthetists, physiotherapists and nurses.

However, patient perspectives will be explored through a qualitative substudy involving semistructured interviews with trial participants. This will allow for post hoc incorporation of patient feedback into future iterations of the programme, particularly concerning usability of wearable technology, feasibility of home-based prehabilitation and perceived barriers to adherence.

No public involvement in trial oversight or manuscript development is planned at this time.

Trial design and setting

This is a single-centre, prospective, parallel-group, superiority randomised controlled trial with an allocation ratio of 1:1. The unit of randomisation is the individual participant.

The trial will be conducted at Singapore General Hospital, a tertiary hospital in Singapore. Eligible participants will be recruited from the Preoperative Evaluation Clinic (PEC). This clinic evaluates patients prior to major surgery, offering an ideal setting for timely identification and enrolment of frail elderly surgical candidates. The planned start and end dates for participant recruitment are 1 August 2025 to 1 August 2028.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

Participants in this study will consist of frail elderly patients aged 65 years or older who are scheduled to undergo major elective surgery. Frailty will be determined using the Edmonton Frail Scale (EFS), with a score of 6 or higher signifying eligibility based on frailty status. 9

To be included in the study, patients must be able to provide informed consent. They must be available at least 2 weeks before surgery to allow for participation in the prehabilitation programme.

The additional inclusion criteria are as follows: participants must be ≥65 years old and scheduled for major elective, non-cardiac surgery with an anticipated duration of over 2 hours or expected blood loss greater than 500 mL. Furthermore, eligible patients must be able to provide consent and follow exercise instructions. Technological requirements include owning a smartphone with Bluetooth and cellular connectivity.

Exclusion criteria

Exclusion criteria for the study will include patients with severe comorbidities that interfere with their ability to participate in the exercise programme, such as unstable angina, severe aortic stenosis or uncontrolled arrhythmias. Additionally, patients with cognitive impairments that prevent them from understanding or adhering to the intervention will be excluded.

Intervention and comparator

Participants in the control group (uSEP) will receive the standard PREPARE programme provided by the hospital, which includes preoperative physiotherapy education and a home exercise booklet. This unsupervised, home-based programme consists of full-body aerobic, resistance and respiratory exercises that participants will perform independently at home. Additionally, participants with a maximal inspiratory pressure (MIP) of less than 100 cmH2O will be given an inspiratory muscle trainer (IMT) to enhance respiratory function. An MIP threshold of <100 cm H2O was selected based on our institution’s prior protocols and previous literature. 10

The uSEP arm reflects the current standard of care for frail surgical patients at Singapore General Hospital. It includes a single preoperative physiotherapy education session, a printed home exercise booklet with aerobic and resistance exercises and IMT when clinically indicated. Participants complete the exercises independently without supervision or wearable tracking.

Participants in the intervention group (swSEP) will receive standard preoperative physiotherapy education in addition to a Fitbit Inspire 3 wearable device. The device was selected based on its user-friendly interface, long battery life, and validated accuracy in measuring key physiological parameters such as heart rate and physical activity. It is also compatible with widely used health platforms, enabling seamless integration with remote monitoring systems. This made it particularly suitable for an elderly population requiring ease of use and reliable feedback for tele-supervised exercise programmes. The device will track vital signs such as heart rate, physical activity and sleep patterns.

The personalised exercise regimen for the swSEP group will consist of three core components: (1) aerobic exercises (eg, walking or cycling) at ≥40% of heart rate reserve to improve cardiorespiratory endurance; (2) strength training involving full-body functional movements (eg, sit-to-stand, resistance band exercises) to build lower limb and core muscle strength and (3) flexibility exercises targeting the chest, arms, legs and trunk. While flexibility exercises are not expected to directly improve 6MWT outcomes, they are included to promote mobility, support warm-up and cool-down routines and minimise musculoskeletal strain, particularly in an older, frail population. The primary aim remains to improve functional capacity as assessed by the 6MWT through aerobic and resistance training.

The participants’ progress will be monitored through vital sign responses, including step count, workout summaries and heart rate. The physiotherapists will review the exercise regimen two times a week and make adjustments as necessary based on the participant’s progress and physical response. Participants with an MIP <100 cmH2O will be prescribed an IMT, specifically the POWERbreathe Plus Classic (POWERbreathe International, UK), a threshold loading IMT device. Training involves 30 breaths, two times per day, at 30% of the participant’s MIP, progressively increased based on tolerance and reassessment. A physiotherapist provides in-person training on the first visit, including hands-on demonstration and supervised practice.

Participants will be provided with a portable pedal exerciser (Mini Pedal Exerciser, Model PED1001, Vive Health, USA), a compact, floor-based cycling device with adjustable resistance, facilitating lower limb mobility and aerobic exercise in a home setting. To ensure safety, both participants and their caregivers will receive a comprehensive safety briefing during the initial visit, and a 24-hour emergency hotline will be available for any adverse incidents during exercise. The prescribed pedometer will also include a non-slip base to minimise the risk of falls.

Adherence to the exercise protocol will be monitored differently in each group. In the intervention group (swSEP), adherence will be objectively assessed using data retrieved from the Fitbit Inspire 3, including step count, duration and frequency of logged exercise sessions and heart rate data. Additionally, participation in scheduled tele-supervised physiotherapy sessions (target: ≥2 per week) will be tracked. In the control group (uSEP), weekly phone calls from the research team will serve to verify self-reported data and address barriers to adherence. Adherence rates will be calculated as a percentage of prescribed sessions completed and will be reported in the final results.

Qualitative interview

The qualitative component of this study will employ a semistructured interview design to explore the barriers to home-based prehabilitation from the perspectives of both patients and healthcare providers. The aim is to understand challenges such as the limited ability to prescribe intensive physiotherapy sessions due to safety concerns. The study will involve participants who have undergone home-based prehabilitation with wearable technology as well as healthcare providers involved in the design, implementation or supervision of such programmes. A purposive sampling method will be used to ensure a diverse range of perspectives, with 20–30 participants recruited, or until data saturation is achieved. Semistructured interviews will focus on personal experiences with home-based prehabilitation, perceived barriers to using wearable technology and the challenges faced during the rehabilitation process.

## Ethics and dissemination

Ethics and dissemination

Research ethics approval

This study will be conducted in accordance with the Singapore Good Clinical Practice guidelines, which are based on the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study has been approved by the Singapore General Hospital Institutional Review Board (SGH IRB) (CIRB Ref: 2024/2242) and is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT06633614 ).

Dissemination policy

The results of the study will be presented at international conferences and submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals. Additionally, the data collected will be made publicly available in a data repository.

Protocol amendments

In the event of significant protocol modifications, all investigators, the SGH IRB and trial participants will be promptly notified.

Consent or assent

Eligible participants will be approached by a research assistant during their visit to the PEC. The participants will be provided with an explanation of the study, a patient information sheet and a consent form. They will be given sufficient time to consider participation and will have the opportunity to ask any questions. If a participant expresses interest in the study, written informed consent will be obtained. The consent forms will be provided in English, but for participants from non-English-speaking backgrounds, a translator will be available. For illiterate participants, a family member will be approached to witness and verify the consent process, ensuring ethical standards are upheld throughout the study.

Confidentiality

Confidentiality of participant data will be strictly maintained throughout the study. Each participant will be assigned a unique study-related identification number, which will be used on all study documents, including the CRF. Personal details for each participant will be securely stored in an access-controlled, locked cabinet at the investigation centre. The deidentified study identification number will be used on all research documents to maintain anonymity.

Ancillary and post-trial care

Given the low-risk, non-invasive nature of the intervention, no ancillary or post-trial care is planned. Participants will continue to receive standard perioperative care through their clinical teams. No compensation will be provided for trial-related harm, as the risk of serious adverse events is minimal and limited to routine prehabilitation activity.

Trial registration

This trial is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov under the identifier NCT06633614 . The registration was completed on 9 October 2024.

Protocol and statistical analysis plan

The full trial protocol, including all Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials checklist items, will be published as a protocol paper in BMJ Open. Upon publication, it will also be accessible through the ClinicalTrials.gov registry (Identifier: NCT06633614 ) and institutional repositories upon request.

Data sharing

Deidentified individual participant data, including the trial dataset and accompanying data dictionary, will be made available upon reasonable request following publication of the primary results. Statistical code used for the main analyses will also be shared.

Access will be granted to qualified researchers with a methodologically sound proposal, subject to approval by the trial steering committee. Requests should be directed to the corresponding author via institutional email. Data will be transferred via secure file-sharing platforms in compliance with SingHealth’s data governance and cybersecurity protocols.

No additional consent for data sharing will be obtained from participants, as data will be fully anonymised prior to sharing.
