# Prediction of hepatic fibrosis using the aspartate transaminase-to-platelet ratio index in children and adolescents with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease.

## Abstract

Background: Aspartate transaminase-to-platelet ratio index (APRI) is an easy and useful predictor of hepatic fibrosis in patients with chronic hepatic disease, and it significantly correlates with the degree of hepatic fibrosis in adult patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). This study aimed to evaluate the use of APRI in assessing the severity of MASLD in children and adolescents.

Methods: Medical records of 115 patients (males: 78; females: 37) with MASLD were retrospectively reviewed. The correlations between the APRI and clinical parameters that indicate the severity of MASLD were analyzed. Their ages ranged between 7.3 and 18.8 years old. Patients with hepatitis B or C infections were excluded from the study. The APRI score was calculated, and transient elastography for liver stiffness measurement (LSM) was performed for all the patients.

Results: The mean APRI score was 0.46 ± 0.26, ranging between 0.16 and 1.57. The LSM values ranged between 2.94 and 7.72 kPa. Body mass index-standard deviation score, transaminase levels, and HbA1c levels were higher in a group with abnormal LSM when divided into two groups according to LSM. The APRI score was greater in the group with abnormal LSM (0.40 ± 0.17 vs. 0.64 ± 0.40, P < 0.001). The APRI score was associated with LSM (r = 0.354, P < 0.001). Area under the curve of APRI for predicting abnormal LSM was 0.650 (95% confidence interval, 0.517-0.784) with a cutoff value of 0.74.

Conclusions: APRI score showed weak correlation with LSM. Further study encompassing various severity of hepatic fibrosis is needed for identifying better and sensitive non-invasive indicators in pediatric MASLD.

## Introduction

Introduction

As the prevalence of obesity increases in children and adolescents, obesity-related morbidities are also increasing [ 1 – 4 ]. Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) encompasses a spectrum of diseases characterized by accumulation of fat in the liver, and it presents with various severities, including simple steatosis, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), hepatic fibrosis, and cirrhosis. Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease has become the most common cause of chronic liver disease, and it can progress to end-stage liver disease [ 5 ]. Additionally, MASLD is usually asymptomatic, and is suspected when elevated transaminase levels are unexplained. However, transaminase levels are not correlated with MASLD severity, and transaminase levels are often normal in MASLD, and may decrease as fibrosis progresses.

To date, liver biopsy is the gold standard for the diagnosis of MASLD or MASH. Owing to the limitations of biopsy, clinical parameters have been suggested as non-invasive and simple tools to diagnose MASLD and evaluate its severity. Ultrasonography is commonly used to diagnose pediatric MASLD, and a strong correlation has been reported between the degree of steatosis predicted using ultrasonography and histological findings [ 6 ]. Transient elastography (TE) is an ultrasound-based method used to evaluate liver stiffness. TE measures the velocity of a shear wave, which reflects liver hardness. The degree of liver stiffness can be obtained by measuring the speed of propagation because a harder medium makes the shear wave propagate faster. TE is used to identify fibrosis in patients with chronic hepatitis [ 7 ]. Currently, TE is recommended for the assessment of liver fibrosis in MASLD, owing to its high accuracy and non-invasiveness [ 8 , 9 ]. Therefore, TE is considered useful for assessing liver fibrosis in pediatric and adult patients with MASLD [ 10 ].

In addition to imaging tests, some non-invasive markers have been used to assess hepatic fibrosis with the benefits of cost-effectiveness and almost no risks. Aspartate transaminase (AST)-to-platelet ratio index (APRI) score was initially proposed as an easy-to-obtain tool for predicting liver fibrosis and cirrhosis in various liver diseases [ 11 , 12 ]. Thus, APRI demonstrated a significant correlation with the degree of hepatic fibrosis estimated from TE in adult patients with MASLD [ 13 ]. Additionally, APRI has been reported to be a predictive marker for MASLD diagnosis in younger adults with obesity [ 14 ]. There have been few studies on the usefulness of APRI in pediatric MASLD as a diagnostic tool that reflects disease severity. In this study, we aimed to investigate the association between APRI and clinical parameters, and evaluate the predictive value of fibrosis in MASLD that develops during childhood and adolescence.

## Materials and methods

Materials and methods

Participants

We retrospectively reviewed the medical records of all participants. The participants were patients who visited the pediatric endocrinology clinic of the Kangdong Sacred Hospital between January 2021 and May 2022, and were diagnosed with MASLD. Laboratory tests were performed on those who did not have acute illness or fever. Patients with alanine transaminase (ALT) levels > 26 IU/L in males and > 22 IU/L in females were enrolled in this study [ 15 ]. Participants suspected of having elevated transaminases due to viral, autoimmune, or drug-induced hepatitis were excluded from the study. MASLD was confirmed after abdominal ultrasonography, which was performed by a single experienced radiologist, and the ultrasound fatty liver indicator (US-FLI) score was recorded. The US-FLI was performed based on the following ultrasonographic findings. Mild or moderate steatosis was scored as 2, and severe steatosis was scored as 3, which was assessed using the intensity of the liver/kidney contrast. One score for each item was added if there were findings corresponding to the following items: posterior attenuation of the ultrasound beam, vessel blurring, difficult visualization of the gallbladder wall, difficult visualization of the diaphragm, and areas of focal sparing. The US-FLI ≥ 2 was regarded as having MASLD [ 16 ]. Liver stiffness measurement (LSM) value was measured using an Epiq series (Philips Healthcare, Amsterdam, Netherlands), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. A C5-1 MHz probe was used with frequencies ranging between 1 and 5 MHz. The test was performed in the supine or slightly lateral left position, with the arm raised above the head. The probe was placed on the skin of the 7–8th intercostal space. When measured with successful and continuous 6–10 times shot with an interquartile range of < 0.3, the median of the valid shots was regarded as a representative value. The degree of stiffness was expressed in kilopascals (kPa). The participants were classified into two groups based on the LSM results (normal and abnormal LSM groups). Reference values for normal liver stiffness was presented in an observational study of healthy children [ 17 ]. The cut-off values for liver stiffness were as follows: 4.59 kPa for 6–8 years old, 4.58 kPa for 9–11 years old, 4.88 kPa for 12–15 years, and 4.66 kPa for 15–18 years old [ 17 ].

Body measurement data, such as weight and height, were obtained from medical records, and BMI was calculated as body weight (kg)/height (m 2 ). Obesity was defined as body mass index (BMI) ≥ 95th percentile on sex- and age-adjusted charts [ 18 ]. Laboratory tests were performed after fasting for at least 8 h. Aspartate transaminase/alanine transaminase and lipid profile, including triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) levels, were measured using an automatic analyzer (Hitachi 7600, Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan). Non-HDL-C concentration was calculated by subtracting the HDL-C level from total cholesterol level. Serum insulin levels were measured using a Wizard 1470 gamma counter (Perkin Elmer). Levels of HbA1c were measured using high-performance liquid chromatography. Homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) was calculated as follows: (fasting insulin level [μU/mL] × fasting glucose level [mmol/L])/22.5. The APRI was calculated as AST (IU/L) × 100 divided by the platelet count (10 9 /L) [ 11 ].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 26.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). An independent t-test was performed to compare the mean clinical parameters according to BMI and LSM values. Normal distribution of data was checked before applying statistical tests. And Mann-Whitney U test was used to analyze non-parametric data such as glucose, HbA1c, and US-FLI. Chi-square tests were performed to analyze categorical parameters, such as sex, between the two groups. Correlations between the APRI scores and clinical variables were analyzed using Pearson’s correlation analysis. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to obtain the cut-off values and area under the curve (AUC) of the variables. A P -value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

Results

Baseline characteristics

In total, 115 patients (males: 78; females: 37) were included in this study. Their mean ± SD age was 11.42 ± 2.24 years, ranging between 7.3 and 18.8 years old. Their BMI and BMI-standard deviation score (SDS) were 26.7 ± 4.2 kg/m 2 and 2.42 ± 0.99, respectively. The mean AST and ALT levels were 57.01 ± 31.50 IU/L and 93.95 ± 56.36 IU/L, respectively. The mean APRI was 0.46 ± 0.26, ranging between 0.16 and 1.57. The LSM values ranged between 2.94 and 7.72 kPa. Of 115 participants, 87 (75.7%) were obese. The anthropometric and biochemical findings, according to BMI, are shown in Table 1 . Table 1 divides children with MASLD into patients with obesity ( n = 87) and the other patients ( n = 28). Twenty-eight non-obese patients had lower uric acid and insulin levels and higher HDL-C levels than those in 87 obese participants. The APRI score were 0.44 ± 0.23 and 0.46 ± 0.28 in non-obese and obese groups, respectively ( P = 0.740). The LSM values were higher in the obese participants ( P = 0.046). The US-FLI values were 3.64 ± 1.25 and 4.03 ± 1.24 in non-obese and obese groups, respectively ( P = 0.188). The levels of HbA1c ranged between 4.8 and 12.3% in 92 participants whose HbA1c levels were available. Four patients had diabetes with HbA1c ≥6.5%, and 17 had prediabetes with an HbA1c between 5.7 and 6.4%. Participants with prediabetes or diabetes had a higher BMI and BMI-SDS than those with normal HbA1c levels. The LSM values were higher in participants with prediabetes or diabetes compared to those in the rest of the participants (4.86 ± 1.17 vs. 4.39 ± 0.86 kPa, P = 0.024).

## Discussion

Discussion

Hepatic fibrosis is a consequence of liver scarring in response to hepatocyte inflammation and necrosis, consisting of excessive deposition of extracellular matrix proteins [ 19 ]. Fibrosis is a prognostic factor of mortality and liver-related morbidity in patients with MASLD [ 20 ]. When managing MASLD in children and adolescents, it is important to diagnose fibrosis early to prevent further progression. Thus, there is a need for non-invasive, simple, and cost-effective indicators that reflect disease severity in pediatric MASLD.

The APRI is derived from the theory that portal hypertension due to hepatic fibrosis is associated with increased platelet sequestration and a reduction in AST clearance [ 11 ]. The APRI score is a well-studied fibrosis predictor for chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection [ 21 ]. The diagnostic performance of APRI for predicting hepatic fibrosis in chronic liver diseases other than HCV hepatitis may be weaker than that of HCV infection [ 22 ]. Nevertheless, the APRI correlated with histologic fibrosis stage, identifying it as a suitable predictor of advanced fibrosis in a large adult MASLD cohort [ 23 ]. A meta-analysis revealed that the APRI is a useful tool for predicting advanced fibrosis [ 24 ]. Recently, a study of APRI showed its potential role as a valuable predictor of cardiovascular risk [ 25 ]. Furthermore, non-invasive scoring systems could be used to predict long-term outcomes in patients with MASLD, expanding the studies concerning non-invasive scoring systems [ 26 ].

Previous studies on the role of APRI in children and adolescents have reported different results. In a study conducted in children with intestinal failure, the APRI significantly predicted cirrhosis, but not fibrosis [ 27 ]. The APRI tended to increase in moderate fibrosis or cirrhosis groups compared to no/minimal fibrosis, but showed no significant correlation with the severity of fibrosis [ 28 ]. In contrast, the APRI correlated with the degree of histological hepatic fibrosis in children with chronic liver disease [ 29 ]. In studies performed in participants with pediatric MASLD, the APRI was not reported to be associated with steatosis severity, indicating that the APRI appears to be a biochemical marker of liver injury rather than MASLD or MASH [ 14 ]. On the other hand, the APRI was significantly different between patients with MASLD with mild and significant fibrosis, suggesting that the APRI might be a useful non-invasive hepatic fibrosis score [ 30 ]. The evaluation of a few hepatic fibrosis scores developed in adults such as AST/ALT ratio, APRI, MASLD fibrosis score, and the fibrosis-4 index concluded that those scores had poor performance in identifying significant fibrosis in children [ 31 ]. Nevertheless, it was reported that only APRI had a fair diagnostic accuracy for the presence of hepatic fibrosis among those non-invasive scores. Additionally, pediatric MASLD fibrosis score, which includes ALT, alkaline phosphatase, platelet count, and gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase has been proposed as a scoring system with improved performance for diagnosing hepatic fibrosis in pediatric MASLD [ 32 ]. In pediatric patients with MASLD, it is difficult to determine whether non-invasive scoring systems are helpful in diagnosing hepatic fibrosis, as there are not many cases of hepatic fibrosis with various severities.

In our study, the APRI ranged between 0.16 and 1.57 in the participants with MASLD confirmed by ultrasonography. A previous study in a large pediatric population with MASLD diagnosed by ultrasonography demonstrated that a cut-off value for detecting MASLD was 0.036, with high sensitivity (100%) and relatively low specificity (61.6%) [ 14 ]. In a study performed among Korean children and adolescents, cut-off value for predicting MASLD was 0.458, with low sensitivity (24.1%) and high specificity (90.4%) [ 33 ]. This discrepancy between the two values requires further investigation to propose a new cut-off value.

This study investigated the usefulness of APRI in predicting fibrosis in pediatric patients with MASLD. The degree of hepatic fibrosis was assessed by measuring the liver hardness using methods other than liver biopsy. Although liver biopsy is the most accurate method to diagnose and grade hepatic fibrosis, TE is significantly correlated with histological fibrosis in children [ 28 ]. LSM value is affected by abdominal obesity. Excessive subcutaneous fat thickness and lack of cooperation were regarded as causes of TE failure in a previous study [ 34 ]. The LSM cut-off level for hepatic fibrosis in pediatric MASLD has not yet been established. In a recent study, 5.5 kPa was suggested as the reference threshold for diagnosing liver fibrosis in Korean children. In the above-mentioned study, the LSM values showed a significant positive correlation with APRI, with higher APRI scores in a fibrosis group than those in a non-fibrosis group [ 35 ]. Nevertheless, LSM values corresponding to minimal fibrosis confirmed by histologic findings started from 4.6 kPa or 4.8 kPa in children with chronic liver disease [ 28 ]. Additionally, the median value corresponding to minimal fibrosis was 5.4 kPa in children with chronic liver disease [ 29 ]. In a study, 4.5 kPa was suggested as the cutoff value for predicting hepatic fibrosis after liver biopsy in pediatric chronic liver disease [ 36 ]. In our study, abnormal LSM cut-off values ranged between 4.59 and 4.88 kPa, according to age, based on the reference values of normal LSM in healthy children [ 17 ].

In this study, the BMI-SDS score was significantly higher in participants with abnormal LSM. Children and adolescents with MASLD have shown insulin resistance and dyslipidemia, although the pathogenesis of MASLD is not completely understood [ 37 ]. Thus, MASLD is regarded as a hepatic manifestation of metabolic syndrome. Severe obesity is more likely associated with severe metabolic derangement [ 38 , 39 ]. In this study, the transaminase levels were also associated with the LSM values. Thus, the early phase of fibrosis should be considered in cases of increased steatosis accompanied by high transaminase levels in pediatric patients with MASLD. However, transaminases are indirect markers of liver fibrosis, and their peak levels are not related to prognosis [ 40 ]. Advanced fibrosis and cirrhosis have been observed in patients with normal transaminase levels [ 24 , 41 , 42 ]. Thus, transaminases are not suitable parameters for detecting advanced fibrosis, necessitating more appropriate indicator for hepatic fibrosis. The LMS results of our patients with abnormal LSM ranged between 4.5 and 7.72 kPa, which corresponded to mostly minimal fibrosis [ 28 ]. Thus, there was a limitation to evaluate the usefulness of APRI as a predictor for hepatic fibrosis. The AUC of APRI for predicting abnormal LSM is 0.650, which means insufficient performance for predicting hepatic fibrosis. Nevertheless, APRI showed weak correlations with US-FLI and LSM values, suggesting that the APRI might show stronger association with severe or advanced fibrosis in pediatric MASLD.

This study has some limitations. First, this study is ultrasound-based approach, and does not include liver biopsy or magnetic resonance imaging for evaluating the degree of hepatic steatosis or fibrosis. Secondly, it is retrospective and cross sectional study. Body composition analysis and waist circumference measurements were performed in only a small subset of patients. ## Conclusions

Conclusions

MASLD in children and adolescents is a public issue as pediatric obesity is increasing. It is helpful to identify and apply non-invasive predictors for assessing the severity of pediatric MASLD because the early stages of MASLD are reversible, and severe metabolic complications can be prevented through early lifestyle intervention. It has not been established whether non-invasive score can be used to identify hepatic fibrosis in children with MASLD. Further studies encompassing various severities of pediatric MASLD is needed.
