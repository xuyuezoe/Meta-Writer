# Camrelizumab vs Placebo in Combination With Chemotherapy as Neoadjuvant Treatment in Patients With Early or Locally Advanced Triple-Negative Breast Cancer: The CamRelief Randomized Clinical Trial.

## Abstract

Importance: Preferred neoadjuvant strategies for early or locally advanced triple-negative breast cancer include a 4-drug chemotherapy regimen containing anthracyclines, cyclophosphamide, taxanes, and platinum. Blockade of the programmed death receptor 1/ligand-1 (PD-1/PD-L1) pathway may improve efficacy of classic neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Camrelizumab, an anti-PD-1 antibody, has showed antitumor activity in advanced triple-negative breast cancer.

Objective: To evaluate the efficacy and adverse events of camrelizumab plus chemotherapy vs placebo plus chemotherapy as neoadjuvant therapy for patients with early or locally advanced triple-negative breast cancer.

Design, setting, and participants: This randomized, double-blind, phase 3 trial enrolled patients from 40 hospitals in China between November 25, 2020, and May 12, 2023 (data cutoff: September 30, 2023). A total of 441 eligible patients were enrolled.

Interventions: Patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either camrelizumab 200 mg (n = 222) or placebo (n = 219) combined with chemotherapy every 2 weeks. The chemotherapy included nab-paclitaxel (100 mg/m2) and carboplatin (area under the curve, 1.5) on days 1, 8, and 15 in 28-day cycles for the first 16 weeks followed by epirubicin (90 mg/m2) and cyclophosphamide (500 mg/m2) every 2 weeks for 8 weeks.

Main outcomes and measures: The primary end point was pathological complete response (defined as no invasive tumor in breast and lymph nodes [ypT0/Tis ypN0]).

Results: Among 441 females randomized (median age, 48 years), the median (range) follow-up duration from randomization was 14.4 (0.0-31.8) months. Pathological complete response was achieved in 126 patients (56.8% [95% CI, 50.0%-63.4%]) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group and 98 patients (44.7% [95% CI, 38.0%-51.6%]) in the placebo-chemotherapy group (rate difference, 12.2% [95% CI, 3.3%-21.2%]; 1-sided P = .004). In the neoadjuvant phase, adverse events of grade 3 or higher occurred in 198 patients (89.2%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group and 182 (83.1%) in the placebo-chemotherapy group; serious adverse events occurred in 77 patients (34.7%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group and 50 (22.8%) in the placebo-chemotherapy group, with fatal adverse events occurring in 2 patients (0.9%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group.

Conclusions and relevance: Among patients with early or locally advanced triple-negative breast cancer, the addition of camrelizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy significantly improved pathological complete response.

Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04613674.

Does the addition of camrelizumab to chemotherapy increase the pathological complete response rate when used as neoadjuvant therapy for patients with early or locally advanced triple-negative breast cancer?

In this randomized clinical trial that included 441 patients with early or locally advanced triple-negative breast cancer, camrelizumab plus chemotherapy, compared with placebo plus chemotherapy, significantly improved the rate of pathological complete response (56.8% vs 44.7%).

Among patients with early or locally advanced triple-negative breast cancer, a neoadjuvant treatment strategy of adding camrelizumab, compared with placebo, to chemotherapy resulted in improved pathological complete response rate.

This phase 3 randomized clinical trial examines the efficacy and adverse events of camrelizumab plus chemotherapy vs placebo plus chemotherapy as neoadjuvant therapy for patients with triple-negative breast cancer.

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) accounts for approximately 15% of breast cancers and is characterized by the absence of immunostaining of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2. 1 , 2 TNBC represents the most aggressive subtype of breast cancer, demonstrating earlier and more frequent recurrence in operable stages (stages I-III) and worse survival outcomes in the inoperable stage (stage IV). 2 , 3 Over the past decade, the treatment strategy for operable TNBC has shifted from surgery followed by adjuvant therapy to a preference for neoadjuvant therapy before surgery, especially for patients with stage II-III disease. The main purpose of neoadjuvant therapy is to downstage the tumor (shrink the tumor to a smaller size and a less extensive state) as well as to provide information on drug sensitivity, which can guide subsequent treatment. The shift to neoadjuvant strategies promotes accelerated drug development, deescalation of breast and axillary surgery, more accurate prognostication, and opportunities for personalized decisions regarding postoperative treatment escalation or deescalation. 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9

Cytotoxic chemotherapy is a mainstay systemic treatment for early-stage TNBC; 10 however, acquired resistance may develop and substantially impact survival. Therefore, new therapeutic strategies are urgently needed to improve treatment outcomes in TNBC. TNBC is the most immunogenic breast cancer subtype because of its genomic instability, high variation burden, and high level of immune infiltration. 1 , 11 , 12 Studies have shown that approximately 40% to 83% of patients with TNBC have positive programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) expression. 12 , 13 , 14 This finding suggests that patients with TNBC are more likely to benefit from adding an anti–PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitor to the chemotherapy backbone. Based on the mature results of the KEYNOTE-522 trial, neoadjuvant pembrolizumab in combination with platinum-containing chemotherapy has been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for patients with high-risk early TNBC. 14 Trials investigating anthracycline-based chemoimmunotherapy, such as GeparNuevo, IMpassion031, and KEYNOTE-522, have reported rates of pathological complete response (pCR) of 53.4%, 58%, and 64.8%, respectively; the addition of immunotherapy increased the pCR rate by 9.2% to 17% in nonselect patients with operable TNBC. 11 , 12 , 14 , 15 Response to neoadjuvant therapy has consistently shown strong association with favorable long-term outcomes, with recurrence and mortality rates of less than 10% observed in patients with pCR. 4 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 Nevertheless, the adoption of neoadjuvant immunotherapy has also introduced questions, particularly regarding selecting the appropriate partner chemotherapy intensity for specific patients in clinical practice. The triweekly administration of anthracycline plus cyclophosphamide in the KEYNOTE-522 trial chemotherapy backbone underscores the necessity for further research into dose-dense anthracycline-cyclophosphamide regimens, especially in higher-risk patient groups.

Camrelizumab is a humanized, anti–PD-1 immunoglobulin G4-κ monoclonal antibody that has shown robust antitumor activity and favorable tolerability across various tumor types, including advanced TNBC. 16 , 17 , 18 In a single-group phase 2 study in patients with early TNBC (>80% with stage II disease), neoadjuvant camrelizumab plus chemotherapy led to a promising pCR rate of 64.1%, with acceptable safety. 19 Herein, this randomized, double-blind, phase 3 CamRelief trial was conducted to further evaluate the efficacy and safety of camrelizumab vs placebo in combination with chemotherapy as neoadjuvant treatment for early or locally advanced TNBC. Specifically, the study enrolled patients with any nodal status (N3 disease included) and used an intensive chemotherapy regimen of nab-paclitaxel plus carboplatin, followed by dose-dense anthracycline plus cyclophosphamide.

This was a randomized, double-blind, phase 3 trial conducted in 40 hospitals in China (eTable 1 in Supplement 3 ). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Good Clinical Practice Guideline. The study protocol and all its amendments ( Supplement 1 ) were approved by the institutional review board or independent ethics committee of each study site. All patients provided written informed consent before enrollment. This study was reported following the CONSORT reporting guidelines by meeting requirements from the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials ( CONSORT ) reporting guideline 2010 checklist.

Eligible patients were females aged 18 to 75 years with stage II or III (T2N0-1M0/T3N0M0 or T2N2-3M0/T3N1-3M0 per American Joint Committee on Cancer, 8th edition 20 ) histologically confirmed invasive TNBC per guidelines of the American Society of Clinical Oncology–College of American Pathologists. 21 , 22 Patients self-reported their sex. Patients were required to have no prior systemic treatment for breast cancer, an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance-status score 23 of 0 or 1, centrally assessed tumor PD-L1 expression (E1L3N assay; Amoy Diagnostics), and adequate organ function. PD-L1 expression was evaluated using the combined positive score, calculated as the number of PD-L1–positive cells (tumor cells, lymphocytes, and macrophages) divided by the total number of tumor cells multiplied by 100. Key exclusion criteria included bilateral breast cancer, history of ductal or pleomorphic lobular carcinoma in situ, history of or active autoimmune disease requiring treatment, primary immunodeficiency, history of or preexisting interstitial lung disease, serious infection within 4 weeks, and clinically significant cardiovascular disease. The full eligibility criteria can be found in the trial protocol ( Supplement 1 ).

Patients were randomly assigned to receive neoadjuvant chemotherapy with camrelizumab or placebo in a 1:1 ratio. Randomization was conducted using a centralized interactive web-response system with a randomly selected block size of either 4 or 6, stratified according to clinical stage (II vs III) and PD-L1 expression (combined positive score 20 mm to ≤50 mm; T3: tumor size >50 mm (without invasion of the chest wall or skin).

N0, no regional lymph node metastases; N1, metastases in movable ipsilateral low or mid axillary lymph node; N2, metastases in ipsilateral low or mid axillary lymph nodes that are fixed or matted, or in ipsilateral internal mammary nodes in the absence of axillary lymph nodes metastases; N3, metastasis in ipsilateral infraclavicular lymph nodes, or ipsilateral internal mammary lymph nodes and axillary lymph nodes, or ipsilateral supraclavicular lymph nodes.

Stage II (T2N0-1M0/T3N0M0) or III (T2N2-3M0/T3N1-3M0) based on American Joint Committee on Cancer Staging Manual, 8th Edition. See above for definitions of T and N stage; M0 indicates no evidence of distant metastases.

A pCR was achieved by 126 of 222 patients (56.8%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group and 98 of 219 patients (44.7%) in the placebo-chemotherapy group (rate difference, 12.2% [95% CI, 3.3%-21.2%]; 1-sided P = .004). A numerically higher pCR rate was observed with camrelizumab-chemotherapy across the prespecified subgroups ( Figure 3 ). In a post hoc subgroup analysis of patients with N3 disease, the pCR rate was 52.2% (12/23) with camrelizumab-chemotherapy vs 29.4% (5/17) with placebo-chemotherapy.

As of data cutoff, event-free survival, disease-free survival, and distant disease-free survival were not mature (eFigure 1 in Supplement 3 ). The estimated event-free survival rate at 18 months was 86.6% (95% CI, 79.9%-91.1%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group vs 83.6% (95% CI, 76.2%-88.9%) in the placebo-chemotherapy group (HR, 0.80 [95% CI, 0.46-1.42]). The disease-free survival rate at 12 months was 91.9% (95% CI, 85.5%-95.5%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group vs 87.8% (95% CI, 80.3%-92.6%) in the placebo-chemotherapy group (HR, 0.58 [95% CI, 0.27-1.24]); the distant disease-free survival rate at 12 months was 91.9% (95% CI, 85.5%-95.5%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group vs 88.4% (95% CI, 80.8%-93.0%) in the placebo-chemotherapy group (HR, 0.62 [95% CI, 0.29-1.33]).

Before surgery, radiological objective responses were recorded for 194 patients (87.4% [95% CI, 82.3%-91.5%]) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group vs 181 patients (82.6% [95% CI, 77.0%-87.4%]) in the placebo-chemotherapy group (between-group difference, 4.7% [95% CI, −1.8% to 11.1%]; eTable 3 in Supplement 3 ).

During the neoadjuvant phase, the median (range) duration of treatment exposure was 25.1 (2.0-34.9) weeks with camrelizumab and 25.3 (2.0-38.9) weeks with placebo; exposure of chemotherapy was comparable between the groups (eTable 4 in Supplement 3 ). The safety summary for the neoadjuvant phase is presented in eTable 5 in Supplement 3 . AEs of grade 3 or higher were reported in 198 of 222 patients (89.2%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group and 182 of 219 patients (83.1%) in the placebo-chemotherapy group, which were predominantly (frequency ≥10%) hematological toxicities in both groups ( Table 2 ). AEs led to discontinuation of any study drug in 41 patients (18.5%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group and 13 (5.9%) in the placebo-chemotherapy group (eTable 6 in Supplement 3 ). Serious AEs occurred in 77 patients (34.7%) in camrelizumab-chemotherapy group and 50 (22.8%) in placebo-chemotherapy group, with decreased neutrophil count (9.5% and 6.4%), decreased platelet count (7.2% and 4.6%), and decreased white blood cell count (6.8% and 3.7%) being the most common (eTable 7 in Supplement 3 ). Fatal AEs occurred in 2 patients (0.9%) in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group and none in the placebo-chemotherapy group.

Adverse events were classified according to Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities and graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0. Grading ranges from 1 through 5 (1, mild; 2, moderate; 3, severe; 4, life-threatening; and 5, death). 25

Adverse events of any grade with an incidence of ≥10% or grade ≥3 with an incidence of ≥2% in either group are shown.

In the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group, 205 patients (92.3%) had immune-related AEs, with 21 (9.5%) experiencing an event grade of 3 or higher. The most frequent immune-related AE was reactive capillary endothelial proliferation (87.8%), followed by hypothyroidism (15.3%) and hyperthyroidism (9.0%) (eTable 8 in Supplement 3 ). Reactive capillary endothelial proliferation was mostly grade 1 or 2 (71.2% and 14.4%, respectively), and only 2.3% of patients had grade 3 events.

No new safety signal was noted with continued camrelizumab treatment in the adjuvant phase regarding grade 3 or higher AEs, serious AEs, or immune-related AEs (eTables 7-10 in Supplement 3 ).

In this randomized, phase 3 trial in early or locally advanced TNBC, the addition of camrelizumab to nab-paclitaxel plus carboplatin followed by epirubicin plus cyclophosphamide (dose-dense) as neoadjuvant treatment resulted in a significantly higher pCR rate. The benefit of camrelizumab-chemotherapy in achieving pCR was generally consistent across subgroups. These findings aligned with previous studies of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab or atezolizumab with chemotherapy for TNBC. 11 , 14

For decades, the standard systemic therapy for early or locally advanced TNBC has been anthracycline plus cyclophosphamide, followed by or preceded by a taxane regimen. Recent efforts to improve pCR rates, a surrogate for long-term survival, have explored the incorporation of platinum agents, immunotherapy, or targeted therapies into this regimen. Three large-scale, randomized studies, GeparSixto, CALGB 40603, and BrighTNess, consistently showed benefits in pCR with the addition of carboplatin. 25 , 26 , 27 The KEYNOTE-522 trial further demonstrated the benefit of adding immunotherapy to a carboplatin-containing chemotherapy backbone. 14 On the other hand, although the clinical advantages of a dose-dense, biweekly anthracycline-cyclophosphamide schedule are well documented in the context of conventional chemotherapy regimen, 28 its use in combination with immunotherapy and platinum remains underexplored. A notable feature of the current trial is the inclusion of a platinum-containing, dose-dense anthracycline-cyclophosphamide based chemotherapy backbone, which potentially increases the antitumor activity and enables the assessment of adding immunotherapy to a more intensive neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimen.

This study enrolled a higher percentage of patients with more advanced TNBC, including 35.8% with stage III disease compared with 21.2% and 24.9% in the IMpassion031 and KEYNOTE-522 studies, respectively. 11 , 14 Specifically, for patients with stage III TNBC, pCR rates with carboplatin-containing chemotherapy with and without camrelizumab were 49.4% and 38.0%, respectively, which is higher than the rates of 45% and 23% reported in the IMpassion031 study using platinum-free chemotherapy with and without atezolizumab. These data extended evidence on the importance of platinum in neoadjuvant treatment of stage III TNBC. Regarding the subset with nodal involvement, patients achieved pCR rates of 64.8% and 44.1% with non–dose-dense anthracycline-cyclophosphamide–based chemotherapy, with and without pembrolizumab, respectively, in the KEYNOTE-522 study. The rates were in accordance with those observed with dose-dense anthracycline-cyclophosphamide chemotherapy with and without camrelizumab. However, because the current study included patients with N3 disease, unlike KEYNOTE-522, the results may suggest that dose-dense anthracycline-cyclophosphamide could potentially further enhance pCR rates. Taken together, the current results demonstrate the added benefit of camrelizumab when combined with an intensive chemotherapy regimen, particularly in higher-risk patients, and support it as a potentially new therapeutic option for treating early or locally advanced TNBC.

In line with other phase 3 trials of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in operable TNBC, the improvement in pCR with the addition of camrelizumab was present regardless of PD-L1 expression. This outcome deviates from the IMpassion130 and KEYNOTE-355 trials, 29 which demonstrated efficacy of an anti–PD-1/L1 inhibitor exclusively in patients with PD-L1–positive metastatic TNBC. 30 These inconsistent results may potentially be attributed to variations in chemotherapy backbone, disease stages, PD-L1 assays, or a combination of these factors. The suggestion that disease stage may serve as a predictive factor in terms of response to immunotherapy in nonselect patients with PD-L1 aligns with KEYNOTE-522 findings. 14

AEs observed in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group were generally consistent with the known safety profiles of the chemotherapy components and camrelizumab monotherapy, 14 , 16 , 17 , 31 , 32 with no new signals identified. The more intense chemotherapy regimen in this study did not appear to compromise treatment tolerability, with similar rates of serious AEs and AEs leading to treatment discontinuations compared with the KEYNOTE-522 study. 14 The most common immune-related AE was low-grade, self-resolving reactive capillary endothelial proliferation, consistent with prior studies of camrelizumab. 16 , 17 , 31 , 32 , 33 , 34 , 35 , 36

This study has limitations. First, the short follow-up precludes the assessment of mature survival data and the long-term safety profile. Follow-up is ongoing. Second, the optimal duration of adjuvant camrelizumab treatment requires further investigation, potentially involving comprehensive biomarker analysis to tailor treatment. Third, the small sample size in some subgroups warrants cautious interpretation of the findings. Fourth, the study was conducted in Chinese patients with TNBC. The efficacy and safety of adding camrelizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in other racial and ethnic groups require further investigation.

The addition of camrelizumab to platinum-containing, intensive neoadjuvant chemotherapy significantly improved the pCR rate. The benefits of camrelizumab-chemotherapy with respect to pCR were generally consistent across subgroups. AEs were more frequent in the camrelizumab-chemotherapy group, but most did not compromise chemotherapy exposure and aligned with the known profiles of each regimen.
