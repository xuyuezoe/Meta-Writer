# Immune checkpoint inhibitors-associated acute kidney injury: a systematic review and meta-analysis of incidence, kidney recovery, and recurrent risk.

## Abstract

Background: Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are widely used in cancer therapy but can cause adverse events, including acute kidney injury (AKI). The incidence of ICI-associated AKI (ICI-AKI) varies across studies, and while steroids may aid in kidney recovery, their full effects require further investigation. Additionally, the risk of recurrent AKI with ICI rechallenge remains uncertain. This study assesses the incidence of ICI-AKI, evaluates steroid efficacy, and examines the risk of recurrent AKI after ICI-AKI.

Methods: A comprehensive search of PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library was conducted through May 2024. Outcomes included the incidence of ICI-AKI, kidney recovery after steroid therapy, and the risk of recurrent AKI with ICI rechallenge. A random-control model was used for analysis.

Results: This analysis included 16 studies involving 10,726 participants. The pooled incidence of ICI-AKI after following up for one year was 1.4% (95% CI 1.0-2.1%), with the incidence rate being 4.3 per 100 patient-years (95% CI 2.3-6.3 per 100 patient-years). ICI-AKI patients treated with steroid were more likely to achieve kidney recovery (OR 1.82, 95% CI 1.17-2.83, P = 0.008). No additional benefit was found from combining intravenous and oral steroids (OR 0.928, P = 0.863). The incidence of recurrent AKI after ICI-AKI was 18.0% (95% CI 13.2-23.9%).

Conclusions: A substantial incidence of ICI-AKI of those receiving ICI was identified. Steroid treatment promotes kidney recovery, but adding intravenous steroids offers no additional benefit. There is an elevated risk of recurrent AKI with ICI rechallenge, underscoring the need for careful monitoring of kidney function.

## Background

Background

Over the past decade, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have emerged as a new therapeutic option for various advanced cancers. By targeting cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4), programmed cell death 1 (PD-1), or programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1), these monoclonal antibodies enhance the immune response against tumors [ 1 – 3 ]. This approach has led to sustained anticancer effects and improved overall survival rates in patients with different types of cancer [ 4 ].

The augmented immunity induced by ICI can also lead to immune-related adverse events (irAEs) [ 5 , 6 ]. Although the exact pathophysiology of these events remains unknown, T cell, antibody, and cytokine responses are thought to be involved [ 7 , 8 ]. Acute kidney injury (AKI) is one notable but less commonly reported irAE [ 9 , 10 ]. According to previous studies, the incidence of ICI-associated AKI (ICI-AKI) ranges from 0.8 to 4.9% [ 11 – 14 ]. However, pooled data on the incidence of ICI-AKI are still lacking.

ICI-AKI is classified into four grades according to the severity. For Grade 1 AKI (i.e., 1.5–2 times above baseline creatinine), temporarily holding ICI may be considered. For Grades 2–4 AKI (i.e., more than 2 times above baseline creatinine), discontinuation of ICI and initiation of glucocorticoids are recommended [ 15 , 16 ]. Although previous studies had demonstrated the potential benefit of steroid treatment for ICI-AKI, a comprehensive synthesis of its full effects was still lacking [ 17 ]. When AKI resolves, rechallenging with ICI is considered a feasible strategy for patients with prior AKI [ 18 , 19 ]. Nevertheless, the incidence of recurrent AKI after ICI rechallenge remained unknown.

This meta-analysis aims to clarify these critical issues by determining the pooled incidence of ICI-AKI, evaluating kidney recovery following steroid treatment in ICI-AKI patients, and assessing the incidence of recurrent AKI after ICI rechallenge.

## Methods and materials

Methods and materials

This study was registered prospectively on PROSPERO (registration number CRD42024507721) and conducted in alignment with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines [ 20 ], as detailed in Supplementary Table S1 and Supplementary Material PROSPERO. We conducted a systematic review about the incidence of ICI-AKI in patients with malignancies, the kidney recovery comparison between steroid and nonsteroid treatments in patients after ICI-AKI, and the incidence of recurrent AKI upon rechallenging of ICI after ICI-AKI.

Search strategy

The authors comprehensively searched for studies published prior to 13 May 2024 in the databases of PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, without any restrictions regarding language or publication date. The search was carried out using the following search terms: [(immune checkpoint inhibitor) or (ipilimumab) or (cemiplimab) or (dostarlimab) or (nivolumab) or (pembrolizumab) or (retifanlimab) or (tislelizumab) or (toripalimab) or (atezolizumab) or (avelumab) or (durvalumab)) and ((acute kidney injury) or (acute kidney diseases)]. The titles and abstracts of the studies retrieved from the search were assessed, and the entire texts of relevant articles were thoroughly examined to ascertain the eligibility of the studies for inclusion in this study. The details of our search strategy are delineated in Supplementary Table S2.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

We included retrospective cohort and observational studies enrolling adults (≥ 18 years) with malignancy who had received ICI. Studies were eligible if they reported (1) the incidence of ICI-AKI; (2) kidney function recovery after steroid therapy for ICI-AKI compared with nonsteroid treatment; or (3) the incidence of recurrent AKI after ICI rechallenge. Descriptive studies, reviews, meta-analyses, case reports, and those lacking follow-up time were excluded [ 21 – 26 ]. Full-text papers were selected for data synthesis and quality assessment.

Study selection

The selection process is depicted in the PRISMA flowchart presented in Fig. 1 . After removing duplicates, two researchers (CW Ho and NW Kang) independently screened the titles and abstracts of all identified records to identify irrelevant articles. This was followed by a full-text review to determine eligibility for inclusion. In this study, all the data were successfully extracted from published articles. Data extraction and outcomes

The essential data from eligible studies were extracted by two reviewers (CW Ho and ZS Ye) independently through a standardized data abstraction form. The extracted data encompassed author, publication year, study design, country, age at administration of ICI, proportion of female, baseline creatinine, comorbidities including hypertension and diabetes mellitus. Besides, drugs including proton pump inhibitor (PPI) and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) which may be associated to adverse kidney outcomes were also retrieved [ 27 – 29 ]. Lastly, follow-up duration, treatment modalities and number of enrolled patients, incidence of ICI-AKI after ICI, kidney recovery rates comparing steroid and nonsteroid treatments among individuals with ICI- AKI, and the number of patients who underwent rechallenge with ICI after ICI-AKI were also recorded.

Our outcomes included the incidence of ICI-AKI, the kidney recovery with steroid versus nonsteroid treatments for those had ICI-AKI, and the risk of recurrent AKI upon rechallenging of ICI. AKI was defined as a ≥ 1.5-fold increase in baseline serum creatinine, and the definition of ICI-AKI must meet at least one of the following criteria: (1) diagnosis confirmed by kidney biopsy; (2) evaluation by clinical specialists; (3) unexplained sustained AKI coinciding with another immune-related adverse event; and (4) consistent with the proposed definition of ICI-AKI by Gupta et al.[ 10 ] The incidence of ICI-AKI was presented into incidence (follow-up for one year) and incidence rate (patient-year) due to the available data and the different contribution of each individual participating in the studies over time. Kidney recovery was defined as serum creatinine returning to ≤ 1.5 times the baseline level or within 0.35 mg/dl above the baseline [ 11 , 17 , 21 , 30 ]. Recurrent AKI after ICI-AKI was defined as a ≥ 1.5-fold increase in serum creatinine from the new baseline at rechallenge.

Quality assessment

Two authors (CW Ho and MH Chuang) independently evaluated the methodological quality of each single-arm study using the critical appraisal tool for noncomparative studies derived by Murad et al.[ 31 ] and modified Newcastle–Ottawa scale (NOS) for double-arm study [ 32 ]. For both the analysis of ICI-AKI incidence and the risk of recurrent AKI after ICI rechallenge, we utilized four evaluation domains: selection (0–1 scores), ascertainment (0–2 scores), causality (0–4 scores), and reporting (0–1 scores). These domains consist of an eight-item checklist, with scores ranging from 0 to 8. For assessing kidney recovery in patients with ICI-AKI, we considered three domains for evaluation, including selection (0–4 scores), comparability (0–1 scores) and outcome (0–3 scores). The quality and the risk of bias were evaluated by CW Ho and MH Chuang and discussed with the third author (JY Chen) when a disagreement occurred. Generally, there were low methodological bias in the included studies. Information is listed in Supplementary Table S3.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

The incidence of ICI-AKI after ICI administration was determined by calculating patient-years using the total number of patients and the mean or median follow-up time. To address the inherent heterogeneity in proportional data across studies, we used a random-effects model to calculate the pooled incidence rate estimates and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A P-value 50% were regarded as considerable heterogeneity. Meta-regression analyses were conducted to assess whether the proportion of female and baseline creatinine level were correlated with the incidence of ICI-AKI. To compare the treatment effects of the steroid and nonsteroid groups in ICI-AKI patients, odds ratio (OR) with 95% CI were obtained. The incidence of recurrent AKI after ICI rechallenge was calculated using the total number of rechallenge patients and recurrent AKI events, applying a random-effects model to obtain pooled incidence estimates and 95% CIs.

To ensure the robustness of this meta-analysis, sensitivity analyses were performed using the one-study removal method. Funnel plot analysis was conducted to assess publication bias in studies related to ICI-AKI incidence, kidney recovery, and recurrent AKI risk. All statistical analyses were carried out using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis software (version 4.0.0, released September 1, 2022).

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination of this research.

## Results and discussion

Results and discussion

Study selection

A total of 638 studies were initially search for the incidence of ICI-AKI, kidney recovery, and recurrent AKI. After removing 79 duplicates, we screened the titles and abstracts of the remaining studies, excluding 514 citations that were irrelevant. Additionally, 5 studies without full articles were deleted. Furthermore, 24 studies were excluded due to ineligible study designs, lack of follow-up time, and insufficient data. Ultimately, 16 studies were included in this systematic review and meta-analysis, with 9 for the incidence of ICI-AKI, 5 for kidney recovery, and 8 for the incidence of recurrent AKI. The study selection process is detailed in Fig. 1 .

Study characteristics

A total of 10,726 participants receiving ICI for malignancies from 16 cohort studies were included in our analysis. The sample sizes ranged from 13 to 2207 participants, with the median age of patients spanning from 61 to 72 years. Most participants were from the USA, with additional populations from Canada, China, Italy, and Türkiye. All the articles were published between 2016 and 2024. The incidence of ICI-AKI

There was a total of nine articles reporting the incidence of ICI-AKI. Based on the study design, these studies were separated into two analysis groups: incidence and incidence rate. Six studies with 9,645 patients were included in the incidence analysis, showing an overall pooled incidence of 1.4% for ICI-AKI after one year of follow-up (95% CI 1.0–2.1%, P < 0.001, I 2 = 78.04%, Fig. 2 ). Seven studies were included in the incidence rate analysis, showing an incidence rate of 4.3 per 100 patient-years (95% CI 2.3–6.3 per 100 patient-years, P < 0.001, I 2 = 91.60%, Fig. 2 ). The sensitivity analysis revealed no significant change in the trend of ICI-AKI incidence (Supplementary Figure S1 ). However, moderate to high heterogeneity was observed. Meta-regressions were performed to examine whether proportional of female and baseline creatinine could affect the incidence of ICI-AKI. Both the proportion of females (coefficient: -0.478 per percentage point, p = 0.048) and baseline creatinine levels (coefficient: -0.418 per 0.1 mg/dL, P < 0.001) were associated with a decrease in ICI-AKI incidence (Supplementary Figure S2). The funnel plot of the studies included in the ICI-AKI incidence and incidence rate analysis showed some asymmetry (Supplementary Figure S3, S4). Egger’s test showed a P-value of 0.218 for ICI-AKI incidence studies and 0.002 for incidence rate studies, indicating potential publication bias of studies included in the incidence rate analysis.

## Conclusion

Conclusion

This study found a 1.4% incidence of ICI-AKI after one year of follow-up, with an incidence rate of 4.3 per 100 patient-years. Steroid therapy improved kidney recovery in ICI-AKI patients, with no difference between oral and combined oral-intravenous dosages. An increased AKI risk (18.0%) was noted upon ICI rechallenge. Clinicians should closely monitor kidney function in patients receiving ICIs, especially those undergoing rechallenge. Further research is needed to determine optimal steroid dosing and to better understand the incidence of recurrent AKI.
