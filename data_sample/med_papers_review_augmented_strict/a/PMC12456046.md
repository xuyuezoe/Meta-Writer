# Associations of triglyceride glucose index and atherogenic index of plasma with plaque, inflammation, flow, and functional ischemia assessed by cardiovascular computed tomography imaging in hypertensive patients: a study of different glucose metabolism status.

## Abstract

Background: The triglyceride glucose (TyG) index and atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) are indicators of insulin resistanceand atherosclerosis, respectively. Nevertheless, their relationship with coronary plaque, inflammation, flow and functional ischemia in hypertensive individuals with different glucose metabolism status has not been studied.

Materials and methods: The study involving 1752 hypertensive participants underwent coronary CT angiography (CCTA), stratified by diabetes mellitus (DM) or the biomarker quantiles. Plaque volume (lipid, fiber-lipid, fiber, calcification), burden, plaque characteristics, CT-derived fractional flow reserve (FFRCT), and fat attenuation index (FAI) were compared across groups. Spearman and logistic regression analysis to assess the association of biomarkers with CCTA parameters.

Results: Baseline analysis revealed that the group with the greatest TyG index and AIP had lower FFRCT, higher plaque volume (PV), and FAI than the lowest group ( p p p > 0.05). AIP and TyG index proved independently linked to functional ischemia, regardless of DM ( p < 0.001).

Conclusion: Among hypertensive participants, significant correlations were observed between the AIP, TyG index and coronary plaque, flow, and inflammation. The coexistence of DM has different effects on these associations. Regardless of DM, functional ischemia was independently associated with both TyG index and AIP.

## Introduction

Introduction

Hypertension presents a cardiovascular risk factor and a growing global health concern [ 1 ]. In hypertensive individuals, the coexistence with diabetes mellitus (DM) is attributed to shared molecular mechanisms, such as insulin resistance (IR), endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and oxidative stress [ 2 ]. This comorbidity is considered a critical risk factor for elevated cardiovascular risk [ 3 ]. Research has indicated that elevated cardiovascular risk and the prognosis of adverse cardiac events are linked to specific plaque components and burden, coronary hemodynamic parameters, high-risk plaque characteristics (HRPCs), and pericoronary vascular inflammation as assessed by coronary CT angiography (CCTA) [ 4 ].

Recently, several novel indicators based on the integration of metabolic parameters have been developed to stratify cardiovascular risk and predict adverse cardiovascular outcomes. Among these, the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) and the triglyceride glucose (TyG) index are particularly noteworthy [ 1 , 5 , 6 ]. As a trustworthy and recently identified biomarker for IR evaluation, the increase in the TyG index is tightly linked to the incidence of cardiovascular conditions, including hypertension, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and metabolic-related disorders [ 5 ]. According to previous studies, the TyG index provides valuable prognostic data for individuals diagnosed with coronary artery disease (CAD) and is strongly correlated with CAD severity. The TyG index may also be utilized for forecasting future cardiovascular risk in individuals with CAD and DM [ 7 , 8 ]. The AIP is calculated as the logarithmic transformation of the molar ratio of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) to triglyceride (TG). It is closely associated with atherosclerotic burden and cardiovascular events, reflecting the balance between pro- and anti-atherosclerotic factors [ 9 , 10 ]. Elevated AIP is associated with myocardial infarction, cardiovascular mortality, the onset and prognosis of T2DM, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in CAD, and an increased risk of revascularization [ 11 , 12 ]. To date, no study has conducted a comprehensive CCTA analysis to explore the linkage between AIP, TyG index and CCTA imaging parameters in hypertensive individuals or how these relationships change in the context of coexisting diabetes.

CCTA is a valuable non-invasive morphological evaluation tool for coronary atherosclerosis [ 13 ]. Parameters derived from CCTA can quantify and stratify CAD risk, offering potential benefits in prevention, diagnosis, and treatment planning [ 14 ]. CT-derived fractional flow reserve (FFRCT) has shown excellent diagnostic accuracy in identifying functional ischemia in those with suspected or confirmed CAD, using invasive fractional flow reserve (FFR) as the gold standard. Functional ischemia, defined by a threshold of 0.8 or below, is a predictor of MACE due to unplanned revascularization [ 15 , 16 ]. Furthermore, advanced CCTA imaging and image processing can be used to quantify the volume and burden of calcification, fibrous, fibre–lipid, and lipid plaques. Quantitative plaque imaging data are emerging as a novel indicator of CAD risk. HRPCs assessed by CCTA, including positive remodeling (PR), low-attenuation plaque (LAP), spotty calcification (SC), and napkin-ring sign (NRS), have been matched with plaque vulnerability by invasive imaging modalities and are closely linked with the development of future CAD [ 4 ]. Vascular inflammation is a crucial factor contributing to atherosclerosis and plaque rupture. Recent studies have shown that CCTA-based pericoronary adipose tissue (PCAT) is an innovative, sensitive marker of coronary artery inflammation that could potentially utilized to detect and classify CAD [ 17 ].

Nevertheless, we intended to investigate the relationship between the TyG index and AIP in hypertensive individuals and plaque, flow, and inflammation assessed by CCTA, to better clarify the benefit of the AIP and TyG index in CAD management. Furthermore, individuals with both DM and hypertension are more susceptible to cardiovascular events. This study sought to assess the impacts of DM on CCTA parameters in hypertensive participants and explore how DM influences the relationships between TyG index, AIP, and CCTA parameters, in order to strengthen risk estimation and encourage the creation of personalized prevention and management measures for CAD in this high-risk group. Moreover, a disconnect between coronary artery stenosis and functional ischemia is common [ 18 ]. The correlation between TyG index, AIP, and functional ischemia under different glucose metabolism states is another clinically relevant question worth exploring.

## Materials and methods

Materials and methods

Study design

This cross-sectional study enrolled hypertensive participants with no prior history of CAD who underwent CCTA for chest pain at the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University between January 2022 and February 2023. All participants provided fasting blood samples for laboratory examination after hospitalization. The interval between clinical evaluation and imaging was ≤7 days. Following is an account of the exclusion criteria: (1) prior usage of drugs that decrease TG; (2) malignant tumors, severe liver and kidney dysfunction, impaired thyroid function; (3) incomplete clinical and biochemical information; (4) AI recognition errors or poor image quality (Likert scale = 1). Ultimately, 1752 participants were recruited ( Figure 1 ). The population was separated into four categories in accordance with TyG index and AIP quartiles, respectively. Subgroup analysis was also conducted based upon whether DM was present or not. Finally, the correlation between the AIP and TyG index in hypertensive individuals and plaque, inflammation, flow and it derived functional ischemia assessed by CCTA was investigated.

This study was carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University (approved No. of Ethics Committee: 2023-41). As this was a retrospective study, the commission waived the requirement for informed consent.

Data sources and collection

Trained medical professionals gathered clinical information about each patient from the electronic medical record system, including pertinent medical imaging records, clinical history, blood test results, and important demographics. Demographic details included variables like height, weight, age, sex, smoking, and alcohol use. The clinical history included a history of hypertension and DM. Records of all medications taken before admission were also obtained, including antihypertensive, hypoglycemic, and lipid-lowering drugs. Venous blood was gathered after overnight fasting for analysis of biochemical indicators, such as HDL-C, total cholesterol (TC), fasting blood glucose (FPG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), TG, apolipoprotein A1 (ApoA1), apolipoprotein B (ApoB), and lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)].

Definition of terms

Any of the following was considered hypertension: hypertension history, therapy with antihypertensive medicines, systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥140 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥90 mmHg [ 19 ]. Patients were considered DM based on their prior history of DM, current use of anti-diabetic medications, or American Diabetes Association diagnostic standards [ 20 ]. The formula for body mass index (BMI) was weight (kg)/height (m 2 ) squared. Smoking was defined as ingesting at least one cigarette per day for over a year. Drinking was defined as consuming at least 1 alcoholic beverage daily throughout the preceding year [ 21 ]. Hyperlipidemia was defined as TC ≥6.2 mmol/L, TG ≥ 2.3 mmol/L, LDL-C ≥ 4.1 mmol/L, or HDL-C ≤ 1.0 mmol/L [ 22 ]. The derivation formula for AIP is as follows: log10 (TG [mol/L]/HDL-C [mol/L]). The formula for the TyG index is ln (TG [mg/dL] × FPG [mg/dL]/2) [ 9 ]. Given the inclusion of multiple parameters in this study, we have provided a comprehensive summary of the definitions for the primary variables in the supplementary materials .

CCTA image acquisition

Each scan was performed using a 320-row CT scanner (Aquilion ONE, Toshiba, Tokyo, Japan) (for hospitalized participants). 1 h before to CCTA scanning, those with a higher heart rate received oral metoprolol (25–75 mg). At a rate of 4.5 mL/s, inject 60–80 mL of non-ionic iodinated contrast agent (Iohexol for injection; GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA). CCTA images are obtained utilizing the expected ECG trigger. Below are the standards of acquisition and reconstruction: gantry rotation speed: 275 ms/time, tube potential: 120 kV, tube current automatically adjusted based on subject’s BMI, slice increment: 0.5 mm; image reconstruction matrix is 512 × 512.

Image quality evaluation

Two observers assessed image quality utilizing a four-point Likert scale (1 = poor, unusable; 2 = acceptable, moderate artifacts, but usable; 3 = good, slight artifacts; 4 = excellent, no visible artifacts) [ 23 ]. A consensus was reached in cases of disagreement between the two observers. Images scoring 1 on the Likert scale were excluded from the study.

CCTA assessment of plaque, inflammation, and flow

Once the raw CCTA images are imported to the ‘Coronary Doc’ software (Shukun Technology, v8.07.0728), the software automatically performs reconstruction, segmentation, and naming in accordance with the cardiovascular computed tomography society guidelines. The ‘Coronary Doc’ software segments the coronary artery tree on CCTA images using VB-net, which was created by U-Net Network [ 24 ]. It may automatically generate scan-specific thresholds for plaque composition and provides completely automated quantitative evaluation of coronary plaque. Total PV, calcified PV (>350 HU), and non-calcified PV consisting of lipid (−30–30 HU), fibro-lipid (31–130 HU), and fibrous PV (131–350 HU) were recorded [ 24 ]. Percent plaque burden was calculated as [(plaque volume/vessel volume) ×100%] [ 25 ]. Additionally, two seasoned radiologists qualitatively evaluated plaques under double-blind circumstances. HRPCs were further assessed based on the four characteristics of SC, LAP, NRS, and PR. Supplementary Material for detailed description [ 26 ]. High-risk plaque (HRP) is considered to have two or more of these characteristics [ 27 ]. Evaluations were conducted as efficiently as possible as to minimize intraobserver variability. Subgroup comparisons between DM and non-DM groups

In contrast to the control group, the DM group exhibited significantly higher total PV and plaque burden. Analysis of plaque composition revealed that the DM group had greater volume of calcified, non-calcified, lipid, fiber–lipid, and fibrous plaques, as well as higher percent plaque burden for each component (all p < 0.001). HRP and HRPCs, including PR, LAP, SC, and NRS, were more prevalent in patients with DM (all p < 0.05). Notable differences were noted between the two groups, with DM patients exhibiting higher FAI (LAD), FAI (LCX), FAI (RCA), and lower FFRCT (all p < 0.001) ( Supplementary Table 1 ).

## Discussion

Discussion

In this study, the relationship between serum AIP and TyG index level in hypertensive participants was investigated in relation to plaque composition (calcification, lipid, fiber–lipid, fibrous PV), morphology (HRPCs), inflammation (FAI), flow (FFRCT) and it derived functional ischemia (FFRCT ≤0.80) as assessed by CCTA and stratified by DM status.

The primary findings of this research can be summarized as follows: (1) participants in the highest quartiles of the AIP and TyG index exhibited higher plaque volume (PV) and burden for each component, elevated FAI, lower FFRCT, and a greater proportion of individuals with HRPCs compared to those in lowest quartiles. (2) Correlation analysis revealed that both the TyG index and AIP exhibited positive correlations with plaque volume, burden of each component, and FAI assessed by CCTA, and negatively correlated with FFRCT. The association between the CCTA parameters and TyG index was slightly higher than that of AIP. (3) DM had cumulative adverse effects on the increase of plaque volume and burden for all components, the formation of HRP, the elevated of FAI in three major vessels, and the decrease of FFRCT in hypertensive patients. (4) The coexistence of DM reduced the correlation between the TyG index and calcified PV, FAI (LAD), and FAI (RCA), as well as the correlation between the AIP and calcified PV. (5) Both AIP and TyG index showed independent associations with functional ischemia, irrespective of DM status.

Correlation between TyG index and AIP with plaque quantification and characteristics

Insulin resistance (IR) serves as a risk factor for cardiovascular disease and is correlated with accelerated development of atherosclerosis [ 30 ]. Using cardiac CT imaging, Wang et al. revealed a substantial association between the TyG index and the burden and presence of atherosclerotic plaque [ 31 ]. The presence and progression of coronary artery calcification are independently correlated with the TyG index [ 8 , 32 ]. Nevertheless, few research has quantitatively evaluated this relationship between plaque composition and the TyG index. When we focus on patients with DM and hypertension who are at high risk for cardiovascular disease, identifying a correlation between non-calcified plaques and the TyG index crucial, as non-calcified plaques are more prone to rupture, leading to cardiovascular morbidity and mortality [ 13 ]. At present, the TyG index is regarded as a reliable surrogate index of IR, and the exact mechanism linking coronary plaque formation and the TyG index remains unclear, but it may be related to the biological significance of TyG index. First, IR induces endothelial dysfunction by reducing NO production and increasing the release of procoagulant factors, promoting platelet aggregation. In the IR state, the PI3K pathway is impacted, while the MAP kinase pathway remains unaffected. This results in the mitogenic effects of insulin on endothelial cells, thereby promoting plaque formation [ 33 ]. Second, IR is often accompanied by dyslipidemia. In the IR state, abnormal insulin signaling promotes the accumulation of LDL-C within the vascular wall. Circulating monocytes, via scavenger receptors, engulf oxidized LDL-C (ox-LDL) with high affinity, differentiate into arterial macrophages, and subsequently transform into foam cells. This process leads to the formation of atherosclerotic fatty streaks, thereby promoting the coronary plaque development [ 34 ]. Finally, IR can further induce oxidative stress and generate a large amount of reactive oxygen species (ROS). Increased ROS levels promote inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and plaque instability, further accelerating plaque formation [ 34 , 35 ]. Additionally, previous reports have demonstrated an association between HRPCs and TyG index [ 27 ]. The findings of this study, derived from a larger population and confirmed their results. This indicates that the TyG index and HRPCs are closely correlated, with high TyG index levels often corresponding to higher HRP and HRPCs levels. This may be attributed to IR promoting the atherosclerosis progression and inducing plaque instability. The correlation between coronary plaque characteristics and the TyG index further elucidates the potential mechanism linking the TyG index to recurrent coronary events at both pathological and anatomical levels.

Beyond predicting plasma atherosclerosis, the AIP provides information on the severity of IR. In addition to the TyG index, AIP is also a straightforward and practical tool for identifying individuals with IR at higher cardiometabolic risk, which is crucial for predicting cardiovascular events outcomes [ 11 ]. The underlying pathophysiological information explain the relatively similar correlations between the AIP, TyG index and CCTA parameters. In addition to its role in IR, AIP has recently been identified as a sensitive indicator for predicting lipoprotein particle size. Elevated AIP levels indicate a predominance of pro-atherosclerotic lipoproteins, which may represent an additional pathological mechanism through which AIP contributes to coronary plaque formation [ 36 ]. Previous research has demonstrated that the AIP is closely linked to cardiovascular risk and CAD [ 10 , 12 ], serving as an independent predictor of coronary artery calcification [ 37 ]. These findings provide partial evidence supporting the reliability of the results in this study. The potential association between AIP, plaque composition and HRPCs suggests that AIP could become a potential indicator for risk stratification in CAD participants and predict the occurrence of cardiovascular events.

Correlation between TyG index, AIP and coronary artery inflammation

CCTA-derived FAI represents a novel, non-invasive marker for assessing coronary artery inflammation [ 14 ]. Inflammation prevents local adipogenesis of perivascular fat, alters its composition around inflamed arteries, and shifts the attenuation of CCTA from lipids to the aqueous phase [ 38 ]. Therefore, theoretically, if vascular inflammation occurs, FAI will increase. Our results indicate a positive relationship between the TyG index, AIP, and FAI in hypertensive participants, and the pro-inflammatory properties of IR may mediate the linkage between FAI and TyG index. IR is characterized by a mild systemic inflammatory state that leads to increased production of inflammatory cytokines. Proinflammatory molecules released from the vascular wall can interfere with the differentiation and lipid storage of PCAT preadipocytes, resulting in elevated FAI [ 27 , 38 , 39 ]. These findings align with previous studies [ 27 , 38 ]. As far as we are aware, no research has examined the relationship between AIP and FAI in hypertensive individuals. The close association between vascular inflammation and atherosclerosis may explain the correlation between the AIP and FAI.

Relationship between TyG index and AIP and FFRCT

FFRCT is a viable and safe alternative to invasive coronary angiography (ICA). Using computational fluid dynamics, FFRCT provides comprehensive anatomical and functional information of coronary lesions, enabling accurate identification of lesion-specific ischemia, which aids in clinical decision-making and patient management [ 15 ]. This study showed that in hypertensive patients with or without DM, TyG index and AIP were negatively correlated with FFRCT and independently correlated with functional ischemia. This suggests that the TyG index may be helpful in predicting potential functional ischemia in hypertensive patients. Recently, Jiang et al. noticed that the TyG index induced coronary microvascular dysfunction by reducing slow coronary flow [ 40 ]. There was a substantial association between the MPI ischemia parameters and the TyG index in individuals diagnosed with ischemia without obstructive CAD [ 41 ]. Additionally, the results of Wu et al. revealed a substantial association between new-onset CAD (defined as coronary artery anatomical stenosis ≥50%) and the TyG index and AIP

## Limitations

Limitations

The research has several limitations. First, this was a single-center, retrospective research. Consequently, the potential effects of selection bias cannot be entirely excluded, and the findings might not be applicable to a wider population. Second, despite the large sample size, we could not obtain a causal relationship. This has to be confirmed in other prospective cohort studies. Moreover, the study lacks data on lifestyle factors such as diet and exercise, which may confound the relationships between TyG, AIP, and plaque. Future research should incorporate comprehensive lifestyle assessments to elucidate these complex interrelationships more clearly. Finally, functional ischemia was determined based on the FFRCT results. Although FFRCT demonstrates good agreement with ICA, it is still not the gold standard for diagnosing lesion specific ischemia. Retrospective study may increase the measurement error of FFRCT by including more participants with complex diseases. Furthermore, it is challenging to accurately evaluate the potential impact on the results because the data of FFRCT and invasive FFR of the same participants cannot be obtained. Future studies should incorporate invasive FFR to more accurately reveal the relationship between the AIP, TyG index, and functional ischemia.

## Conclusion

Conclusion

The TyG index and AIP were closely associated with CCTA-assessed plaque, flow, and inflammation in hypertensive patients, and both exhibited similar patterns of association with CCTA parameters. Compared to the non-DM group, the coexistence of DM exerted differential effects on the associations between TyG index, AIP, and CCTA parameters. Further clinical and mechanistic research is warranted in the future. In addition, both the AIP and TyG index maintained an independent association with functional ischemia, regardless of the presence of diabetes mellitus.
