# Prognostic value of immunotrophic inflammatory markers in ESCC undergoing chemoradiotherapy combined with immunotherapy.

## Abstract

This study aimed to investigate the role of immunotrophic inflammatory markers in assessing the prognosis and treatment-related toxicity in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) undergoing first-line radiotherapy and chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy. We retrospectively enrolled 67 patients with ESCC and determined the optimal cutoff values for prognostic nutrition index (PNI), neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and platelet to lymphocyte ratio (PLR) using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0. The one-, two-, and three-year overall survival (OS) rates were 88.1%, 62.3%, and 57.6%, respectively. The overall effective rate was 82.1% (55/67). ROC curve analysis revealed optimal cutoff values for PNI, NLR, and PLR as 48.35, 3.84, and 150.49, respectively. Patients in the high PNI group, low NLR group and PLR group exhibited significantly higher mOS and mPFS time compared to the control group. Notably, the incidence of grade 2 toxicity and side effects in the PNI ≥ 48.35 group was significantly lower than that in the PNI < 48.35 group. Our findings suggest that pretreatment values of PNI, NLR, and PLR can serve as valuable biomarkers for evaluating the prognosis of ESCC patients undergoing first-line radiotherapy and chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy. Further studies with larger cohorts are warranted to validate these results.

## Introduction

Introduction

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a highly aggressive malignant tumor, and more than 50% of the world’s incidence and mortality from ESCC occurs in China 1 .

Although preoperative neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy combined with surgical treatment for patients with locally advanced resectable esophageal cancer, represented by CROSS in the Netherlands and the NEOCRTEC5010 study in China, has made great progress in the survival of these patients, it is still difficult to meet the survival expectations of patients 2 – 4 .

The emerging immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have shown encouraging efficacy by improving tumor response and long-term disease control for various solid malignant tumors 5 .

Increasing evidence indicates that monotherapy and immunotherapy-based treatments can significantly enhance outcomes in patients with ESCC. The results of ATTRITION-3, ESCORT, and KEYNOTE-181 have demonstrated the superior effects of these ICIs as second-line treatments for esophageal cancer patients 6 – 8 , with KEYNOTE-181 exhibiting beneficial effects in Asian subgroups. First-line ICIs treatment with KEYNOTE-590 and ESCORT-1 has also improved the overall survival rate (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in esophageal cancer patients 9 , 10 . Additionally, Checkmate-577 has increased disease-free survival (DFS) in patients undergoing esophageal cancer surgery 11 .

Although immunotherapy has shown great potential in the treatment of esophageal cancer, and a number of phase III studies of immunotherapy combined with radiotherapy are ongoing (such as ESCORT-CRT, KEYNOTE 975, RATIONALE 311, KUNLUN, SKYSCRAPER-07and so on), due to the lack of sufficient evidence-based medical evidence, there is an urgent need to combine the latest research data of immunotherapy and chemoradiotherapy for esophageal cancer, and more studies are still needed to optimize treatment regimens, improve treatment efficacy, and reduce side effects.

Despite the promising results achieved through the use of ICIs in esophageal cancer, identifying the beneficiary population remains uncertain. This uncertainty primarily stems from the lack of reliable biomarkers to identify this population. In recent years, serology-related immunotrophic inflammatory indicators, such as the prognostic nutritional index (PNI), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), have played a crucial role in predicting the prognosis of esophageal cancer patients, particularly those undergoing surgery 12 . However, the relationship between these markers and ESCC patients receiving chemoradiotherapy (CRT) combined with ICIs has not been reported. To further elucidate the connection between immunotrophic inflammatory markers and the prognosis and toxicity of ESCC patients undergoing CRT plus ICIs, we conducted this study.

## Materials and methods

Materials and methods

Patient enrollment

The criteria for enrollment included patients who were (1) diagnosed with ESCC by histopathology; (2) categorized with stage IIB to IV esophageal squamous cell carcinoma based on the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 2017 TNM staging criteria for esophageal cancer; (3) characterized by an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) ≤ 2, and patients can at least consume liquid food; (4) treated with immunotherapy for at least 3 cycles; (5) All patients were treated as first-line therapy.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Combined with a second primary cancer; (2) Received other anti-tumor therapy before enrollment; (3) Abnormal myeloproplasia and other hematopoietic system diseases before treatment; (4) Active infection, HIV infection, pre-treatment viral hepatitis requiring treatment; (5) Patients with autoimmune diseases and other serious systemic diseases require drug intervention; (6) The dysfunction of important organs such as the heart, liver, and kidneys affects treatment.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Ethics No: 2024KY155). Research involving human research participants were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Methods of treatment

Enrolled patients were treated with different ICIs, including Camrelizumab (38; 56.7%), Toripalimab (13; 19.4%), Pembrolizumab (10; 14.9%), and Sintilimab (6; 9.0%). The number of immunotherapy cycles ranged from 3 to 39 cycles, with a median of 5 cycles. A total of 28 (41.8%) patients received treatment for fewer than 6 cycles, while 39 (58.2%) patients received 7 or more cycles of treatment. All enrolled patients underwent simultaneous modulated accelerated radiotherapy (SMART), involving field irradiation. The irradiation target included specific lesions inpatients and all lesions in cases. There were 50 (74.6%) and 17 (25.4%) patients in the Radiotherapy for all lesions (Radiotherapy for all lesions, RAL) group and the Radiotherapy for partial lesions (Radiotherapy for partial lesions, RPL) group, respectively, and all the 17 patients in the RPL group were patients with distant lymph node metastases, including 7 cervical lymph node metastases and 8 abdominal lymph node metastases. Prescription dosage: DT 50–60 Gy/25–30f, 1.8–2.0 Gy/f, 5 times/week, the median equivalent biological dose (a/b = 10 Gy) was 56 Gy.

Chemotherapy was administered for 2 to 6 cycles, with a median of 4 cycles. Among these, 23 (34.3%) patients received platinum with paclitaxel, while 44 (65.7%) patients received platinum and albumin paclitaxel. The treatment strategy groups involving ICIs and CRT were as follows: 12 (17.9%) for the post-ICIs group, 25 (37.3%) for ICIs and CRT, and 30 (44.8%) for post-CRT sequential ICIs.

Response criteria

To mitigate the impact of pseudoprogression in immunotherapy, we conducted efficacy evaluations after three months of treatment. The effectiveness evaluation criteria were based on immune-related Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumors (irRECIST). Efficacy was categorized as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease (PD). The overall response rate (ORR) was calculated by dividing the number of patients who achieved CR and PR by the total number of participating patients. The disease control rate (DCR) was calculated by dividing the number of patients treated for CR, PR, and SD by the total number of patients.

Determination of serological and hematological parameters

Serological and hematological tests were conducted within one week before treatment, and PNI (serum albumin value + 5 × total number of peripheral blood lymphocytes), NLR (the number of neutrophils divided by the number of lymphocytes), and PLR (the number of platelets divided by the number of lymphocytes) were calculated.

Calculation of relevant indicators: It was calculated according to the results of blood routine and biochemical examination within 1 week before treatment. Onodera prognostic nutritional index (prognostic nutritional index, PNI) = serum albumin value + 5 × total number of peripheral blood lymphocytes; neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio (neutrophil lymphocyte ratio, NLR) = neutrophil count/lymphocyte count ratio; Platelet lymphocyte ratio (platelet lymphocyte ratio, PLR) = platelet count/lymphocyte count ratio.

Evaluation of treatment-related toxicity and side effects

Response and progression during and after immunotherapy were assessed using the Immune-related Response Criteria (irRC). The evaluation of treatment-related toxicity was based on the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE). In this study, only grade ≥ 2 toxicity side reactions were analyzed.

Follow-up

Enrolled patients underwent regular reviews and follow-ups, including blood tests, chest and abdominal CT scans, esophageal barium examinations, and superficial lymph node ultrasounds. Immunotherapy was administered every 2–3 cycles and every 1–3 months after the end of immunotherapy. The last follow-up date was December 31, 2022.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 software. The χ2 or Fisher exact test was employed to analyze differences in categorical variables, while the Mann–Whitney U test was used for continuous variables. Survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan–Meier method and the log-rank test, with the median follow-up calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Univariate and multivariate analyses were performed using the Cox proportional hazards regression model. Multivariate analysis was conducted for variables with P values ≤ 0.05 in the univariate analysis. The optimal cutoff values of the PNI, NLR and PLR were determined by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve with the highest Youden’s index according to the OS of patients. The area under the curve (AUC) was measured to evaluate the predictive ability of the PNI, NLR and PLR, and the combined PNI, NLR and PLR for OS. Calculate AUC using non parametric integration method and generate P value based on standard error Z-test to verify the statistical significance of the model’s discriminative ability. P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The primary endpoints of this study were OS and PFS. OS was defined as the time from the start of treatment to the date of death or the last follow-up, while PFS was defined as the time from the onset of treatment to the first documented disease progression, death, or last exposure, whichever occurred first.

## Results

Results

Analysis results of the general situation of the enrolled patients

Between March 2018 and May 2021, a total of 67 ESCC patients treated at The Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University were included in this study. The male-to-female ratio was 3.2:1, and the median age was 64 years (ranging from 39 to 78 years). Esophageal lesions were located in the cervical, upper thoracic, mid-thoracic, and inferior thoracic segments in 7, 13, 31, and 16 patients, respectively. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) scores were distributed as follows: 10 cases with a score of 0, 32 cases with a score of 1, and 25 cases with a score of 2. Tissue differentiation revealed 17 cases with low differentiation, 8 cases with medium differentiation, and 42 cases with high differentiation. According to the 2017 American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) esophageal cancer TNM staging criteria, there were 10 cases at clinical TNM stage II, 31 cases at stage III, and 26 cases at stage IV. Among the patients, 4 had lung metastasis, 1 had liver metastasis, and 21 had lymph node metastasis in distal sites. Within the 21 cases with lymph node metastasis, 7 had cervical lymph node metastasis, 8 had abdominal lymph node metastasis, and the remaining 6 had cervical lymph node metastasis.

## Discussion

Discussion

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is closely related to the occurrence and development of cancer and is currently a hot topic in the development of new treatment strategies 13 , 14 .

TME is composed of many different types of cells, extracellular matrix, blood vessels, and immune cells. These components interact with each other and play an important role in tumor growth, metastasis, and response to treatment. The cells in TME include tumor cells themselves and non-tumor cells (such as inflammatory cells, fibroblasts, etc.), among which non-tumor cells can also have an impact on the growth and metastasis of tumor cells, For example, cells in the inflammatory response can release pro-inflammatory factors that promote tumor growth and invasion. In recent years, various immunotrophic inflammatory cells have been used to predict the prognosis of various cancer patients and have played a significant role in prognostic evaluation 15 – 17 . NLR and PLR are considered indicators of systemic inflammation and have been validated in various types of cancer patients. They have also been employed to evaluate the host’s anti-tumor immune responses 18 – 20 . However, these inflammatory-based biomarkers only consider two circulating immune cell types, and there is no consensus regarding their relationship with the prognosis of such patients.

Additionally, the nutritional status of cancer patients is closely related to treatment-related complications and overall survival time 21 , 22 . Combining nutritional immunity and inflammatory indicators may enhance their ability to predict patient prognosis 23 . The PNI, defined based on serum albumin levels and peripheral blood lymphocyte counts, has been used to assess the nutritional status of cancer patients and has been proven to be a powerful prognostic marker for various malignant tumors, including esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) 24 – 26 .

To the best of our knowledge, this study is one of the few that utilize immunotrophic inflammatory indicators to predict the prognosis and toxicity of patients receiving chemoradiotherapy (CRT) combined with ICIs for first-line treatment of ESCC. Our research results indicate that CRT combined with ICIs is safe and effective in treating locally advanced and metastatic ESCC patients. Patients with high PNI, low NLR, and PLR before treatment exhibit significantly higher overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) rates at 1, 2, and 3 years compared to their counterparts. PNI is closely associated with treatment-related side effects in patients. Although this is a retrospective study, it provides insights into the potential of immune nutritional inflammatory indicators in predicting outcomes for such patients and warrants further sustained research.

The mechanisms underlying how increased systemic inflammatory response and low nutritional status promote tumor cell invasion, proliferation, and metastasis remain incompletely understood. Studies have indicated that as many as 10–57% of cancer patients simultaneously experience thrombocytosis 27 , and reports of poor prognosis due to elevated platelet counts are relatively common 28 . Elevated platelet counts can stimulate tumor angiogenesis, maintain tumor vascular system integrity, and promote tumor invasion and metastasis. Consequently, emerging clinical applications incorporate platelet-based biomarkers into “liquid biopsy” to improve the diagnostic accuracy and predict treatment responses in cancer patients 29 .

In contrast to lymphocytes, which play an anti-tumor role by inducing cytotoxic cell death, inhibiting tumor cell proliferation and migration, and establishing host immune responses to cancer 30 , inflammatory cells such as neutrophils play a pivotal role in promoting inflammatory factors, facilitating tumor growth, metastasis, and recurrence within the TME, and aiding tumor cells in evading immune detection 31 . Thus, before undergoing tumor treatment, ESCC patients with higher NLR and PLR should receive early anti-inflammatory treatment to enhance the effectiveness of their anti-tumor therapy.

Malignant tumor patients often encounter nutritional challenges at various stages of disease progression, especially in advanced cases leading to cachexia. Research suggests that hypoalbuminemia and low lymphocyte counts create a favorable TME for tumor progression 32 , 33 . Additionally, poor nutritional and immune status prolongs treatment durations and increases treatment-related side effects in patients. A study of non-small cell lung cancer patients by Tang et al. 34 , 35 revealed a higher likelihood of radiation esophagitis ≥ grade 2 in the low hemoglobin level group. Similarly, Dong et al.'s research on esophageal patients undergoing CRT demonstrated the predictive value of hemoglobin levels for ≥ grade 2 radiation esophagitis. PNI, composed of serum albumin concentration and lymphocyte count, is employed to assess immune nutritional status. Our study results indicate that PNI is a superior predictor of patient prognosis and an important indicator for predicting treatment-related side effects.

Limitations of this study include its retrospective, single-center design, which may introduce inherent biases in case selection. Additionally, numerous inflammatory indicators that affect the prognosis of esophageal cancer patients, not covered in this study, could impact outcomes. Inconsistent use of immune drugs may also influence treatment efficacy. Lastly, we only collected data before treatment for serological indicator calculations, which may change over time during treatment due to various pathological conditions. Therefore, future large-scale prospective studies, especially confirmatory studies, are needed to validate our preliminary results.

## Conclusions

Conclusions

In summary, our research demonstrates that PNI, NLR, and PLR before treatment can serve as valuable biological indicators for evaluating the prognosis of patients undergoing radiotherapy and chemotherapy combined with first-line immunotherapy for ESCC. Moreover, PNI can assess treatment-related toxicity and side effects effectively.
