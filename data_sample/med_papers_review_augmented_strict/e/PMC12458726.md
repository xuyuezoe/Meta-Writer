# Immune-related adverse events occurring rapidly after a single dose of immune checkpoint blockade.

## Abstract

Background: The optimal dosing regimen for immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) remains a critical question in oncology. Although immune-related adverse events (irAEs) are common with ICB, the incidence and severity of irAEs following a single dose of anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-(L)1) blockade have not yet been investigated.

Methods: We conducted a single-center prospective cohort study at Gustave Roussy (Villejuif, France), based on a population of patients with advanced or metastatic cancer, using data from the French Registre des Effets Indésirables Sévères des Anticorps Monoclonaux (REISAMIC) registry, a pharmacovigilance database dedicated to irAEs. Between December 2014 and December 2023, we included adults with solid or hematologic malignancies who presented with irAEs after the first infusion of anti-PD-(L)1 therapy, regardless of the treatment indication, and before the administration of the second dose of ICB. The main outcomes included the incidence and characteristics of irAEs, particularly severe (grade 3-4) and fatal (grade 5) events.

Results: Of the 3565 patients prospectively followed in REISAMIC, 70 (1.96%) experienced irAEs following a single infusion. Of these 70 patients, severe irAEs (grade 3-4) occurred in 20 patients (37.1%), and fatal irAEs (grade 5) were recorded in three cases (4.3%), indicating significant toxicity risks. The most frequently affected organ systems were the skin (14 (20%)), musculoskeletal system (11 (15.7%)), endocrine system (9 (12.9%)), and cardiovascular system (9 (12.9%)). Most irAEs developed within 20 days after treatment, with a median onset time of 14 days (IQR, 5-21). Multiorgan toxicities were observed in 10% of patients. Despite the severity, no predictive markers for fatality, multiorgan involvement, or early onset were identified, including pre-existing autoimmune conditions.

Conclusions: This study underscores the notable risk of severe and fatal irAEs following a single dose of anti-PD-(L)1 therapy in patients with advanced/metastatic cancer. The absence of predictive markers for fatality, multiorgan toxicities, or early onset highlights the need for enhanced patient monitoring during the initial treatment phase. Further research is needed to optimize dosing regimens, balancing safety and efficacy for safer clinical use of immune checkpoint blockade.

## Main text

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) is frequently associated with immune-related adverse events (irAEs), which typically manifest during the early weeks to months of therapy. However, the incidence and severity of irAEs after a single dose of anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-(L)1 therapy, particularly in patients with advanced or metastatic cancer, have not been characterized. Understanding early-onset toxicity is critical for optimizing treatment regimens and patient safety.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

This study is the largest prospective analysis to date describing irAEs after only one infusion of anti-PD-(L)1. We show that nearly 2% of patients develop irAEs after a single dose, with over 40% of these being severe or fatal. Most events occurred within 20 days, and no clinical predictors were identified for early onset or fatal outcomes.

HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY

These findings highlight the need for close monitoring during the initial treatment phase, even after a single dose of ICB. They support reconsideration of dosing strategies in clinical practice and emphasize the importance of identifying predictive biomarkers for early severe toxicity. This study may inform future trial designs evaluating shorter or individualized ICB regimens.

## Introduction

Introduction

The utilization of immune checkpoint blockade (ICBs) has rapidly progressed, bringing about unprecedented treatment benefits for patients with cancer, notably improved patient survival rates. 1 3 Nonetheless, more than 80% of patients encounter immune-related adverse events (irAEs) or toxicities, including 15%–30% severe grade 3–5 toxicities according to the ICB used, or with the use of combination therapy. 4 These irAEs can lead to treatment interruption or cessation, impair patients’ quality of life, and, in some cases, be fatal. Complications arising from these toxicities can result in long-term irreversible organ damage and other chronic health conditions, impairing health-related quality of life. Toxicities may affect various organ systems, with frequent occurrences observed in the skin, endocrine, gastrointestinal, hepatic, pulmonary and rheumatologic systems. 4 Early detection of these toxicities during the initial phases of treatment can be difficult, and subsequent management is often challenging.

These irAEs, across all grades, tend to appear in the first weeks and months following the introduction of treatments. 5 Some symptoms may even appear in the days following the first immunotherapy infusion. 6 7 Sznol et al were able to report within their cohort of 889 patients that patients treated with ipilimumab for melanoma showed an onset delay of dermatological irAEs of 2–3 weeks after the initiation of immunotherapy. 8 Gastrointestinal and hepatic irAEs, on the other hand, manifested after 6 weeks, and endocrine irAEs occurred between 6 and 9 weeks. 8 Grade 5 irAEs tend to appear earlier following ICB administration, with a median of 40 days and 14.5 days in the context of monotherapy and combination therapy, respectively. 9

Similar results in a single-center prospective cohort study (Registre des Effets Indésirables Sévères des Anticorps Monoclonaux (REISAMIC)) illustrate that very severe (grade 4–5) irAEs occurred earlier than mild severe toxicities: 41 days for grades 4–5 versus 91 days for grades 2–3. 10 Both studies describe the onset of irAEs occurring only a few days after the administration of anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-(L)1) monotherapy. 9 10

Effective management of ICB therapy remains an active area of research, aiming to improve efficacy, reduce toxicity, and optimize cost-effectiveness. Recent studies challenge the traditional high-dose approach, suggesting that lower doses or extended dosing intervals may sustain antitumor activity while reducing toxicity and healthcare costs. 11 12 In particular, pharmacokinetic modeling and retrospective clinical series have indicated that reduced dosing intensity and less frequent administration of anti-PD-(L)1 agents can maintain sufficient drug exposure and clinical efficacy, especially in advanced non-small cell lung cancer and melanoma. 1113 16 These alternative regimens have been applied to reduce hospital visits, improve patient convenience, and potentially mitigate cumulative toxicities. However, safety during the initial treatment period remains a concern. However, while emerging evidence suggests that durable antitumor effects may be achieved in selected patients after limited exposure to ICB, using reduced doses, longer intervals, or shorter treatment durations, further research is needed to optimize the balance between clinical benefit and risk of toxicity. 11 17 Importantly, these strategies must be balanced against initial clinical reports that early immune activation and the risk of irAEs can occur rapidly, even after a single infusion of pembrolizumab, as reported by Vos et al in early breast cancer. 18 Although irAEs typically develop within 4–6 months after commencing immunotherapy, the incidence of irAEs after a single dose of anti-PD-(L)1 is not well described. It is not clear whether the grade of toxicity could be moderate or severe leading to a discontinuation of the immunotherapy. 18 19

The aim of this study was to estimate the incidence of irAEs after a single dose of anti-PD-(L)1 therapy whatever the indication and to describe their characteristics and management in the prospective REISAMIC registry at Gustave Roussy Cancer Center (Villejuif, France).

## Methods

Methods

Since 2014, the REISAMIC registry has prospectively recorded all significant irAEs defined as grade ≥2 according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) in adult patients (aged ≥18 years) treated with ICBs under real-life conditions at Gustave Roussy Cancer Center. Each irAE was reported by the patient’s physician in medical files and all irAE grade 2 or more are recorded in REISAMIC by the registry’s pharmacovigilance team. Patients who developed irAEs following their first infusion of anti-PD-(L)1 and prior to receiving their second dose of ICB were identified. The population-based cohort study in REISAMIC included 70 patients among 3,565 patients enrolled in the REISAMIC registry between June 2014 and December 2023 ( figure 1 ). We excluded patients who had received several immune checkpoint blocker therapies, a combination of ICBs prior to the first administration of an anti-PD-(L)1 infusion and patients who had been treated with a combination of immunotherapy with chemotherapy. Clinical data regarding demographic characteristics, the cancer diagnosis and the immunotherapy anti-PD-(L)1 was collected. Detailed clinical features of irAEs, including CTCAE grade, their management, and their evolution were described. The number of patients and the safety of ICB rechallenge were reported in this analysis for those who discontinued immunotherapy after a single dose.

The relationship between the administered ICBs and the irAEs was systematically evaluated by members of the pharmacovigilance team, as previously described. 10

Data were quoted as the mean or the median (range) or the median (IQR), unless otherwise stated. A multivariate analysis was performed to compare variables: using the χ 2 test or Fisher’s exact test for qualitative variables and the unpaired Kruskal-Wallis test for quantitative variables. The threshold for statistical significance was set to p<0.05 in all cases.

## Results

Results

Patient characteristics and irAE prevalence

Over a 9.5-year period, 3,565 patients were enrolled in the REISAMIC registry. Among these, 70 patients experienced one or more irAEs following their first anti-programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1)(L1) infusion, indicating a prevalence of 1.96%. The median age of this cohort was 72 years (IQR, 64–82). Most patients had melanoma (33 (47.1%)) or lung cancer (28 (40.0%)) and 71% presented with stage IV cancers. Prior to ICB therapy, over 80% of patients had undergone other cancer treatments, such as chemotherapy (20 (26.8%)) and surgery (17 (22.9%)). Pre-existing autoimmune diseases were observed in 8 (11.4%) patients of the cohort, consistent with previous studies.

irAE characteristics and severity

Most irAEs developed within 21 days of ICB initiation, with a median onset time of 14 days (IQR, 5–21). The organ systems most frequently affected included the skin (14 (20%)), musculoskeletal system (11 (15.7%)), endocrine system (9 (12.9%)), and cardiovascular system (9 (12.9%)) ( figure 2 ). Time to occurrence

The majority of irAEs occurred within the first 21 days post-ICB infusion. The median time to onset was 14 (IQR, 5–21) days and showed no significant differences across all grades: there were no significant differences in the median time to occurrence between lower-grade irAEs, severe-grade irAEs, and fatal irAEs (14 (IQR, 4–21) vs 14 (IQR, 6–21) vs 20 (IQR, 19–20.5) days; p=0.48) ( figure 3A ). Similarly, no significant differences in median time to onset were observed when comparing grade 2 and grade 3–4–5 (14 days vs 20 days, p=0.19) or between grade 2–3 and grade 4–5 (14 days vs 17 days, p=0.52) ( figure 3B,C ). Furthermore, the median time to onset of irAEs was consistent across the four most common types of irAEs: cutaneous, rheumatologic, endocrinological, and cardiac disorders, with no significant differences observed (p=0.99) ( figure 3D ).

Treatment and follow-up

Systemic corticosteroids were the most frequently used treatment for irAEs, administered in 30 (35.7%) cases. Topical corticosteroids were used in 15 (17.9%) cases ( table 2 ). ICB was permanently discontinued in 16 cases (22.9%). Of the 46 (65.7%) patients who underwent ICB rechallenge, only one experienced a recurrent irAE, presenting as grade 2 cutaneous psoriasis ( figure 4 ; online supplemental table S2 ).

Fatal irAEs

Three patients died from irAE-related toxicities, including two cases of myocarditis and one of renal failure. All three patients had metastatic melanoma and were aged between 54 and 87 years. The myocarditis cases occurred in older females with cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidities but no pre-existing autoimmune diseases. Fatal irAEs had a median onset time of 19 days (IQR, 19–20.5). All three received systemic corticosteroids, and one myocarditis case was treated with abatacept in addition to steroids ( online supplemental table S3 ).

Multisystem toxicity

Seven patients (10%) developed multisystem irAEs ( online supplemental table S1 ). The most common patterns involved myocarditis/myositis, cutaneous disorders, and arthralgia. Severity ranged from grade 2 to grade 5. Two patients had notable pre-existing autoimmune diseases: Sjögren syndrome/Hashimoto’s disease and minimal change glomerulopathy/hypothyroidism.

Comparison of monotherapy versus combination therapy

Because of the significant number of irAEs occurring within the first weeks after the initial infusion of anti-PD-(L)1 monotherapy in the cohort of 70 patients, we investigated whether this early toxicity pattern was also observed with combination therapy (anti-PD-1/anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA-4)). Specifically, we compared these findings to those from a separate cohort of 22 patients who received anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 combination therapy (ipilimumab/nivolumab) for metastatic melanoma in the REISAMIC study ( figure 1 ). In this combination therapy cohort, 77.2% of patients developed severe irAEs (grade ≥3), compared with 41.4% in patients receiving anti-PD-(L)1 monotherapy ( online supplemental table S4 and figure S1–S3 ). Additionally, multisystem irAEs were more frequent in the combination group compared with monotherapy (36.4% vs 10%). These findings highlight the need for close monitoring early in treatment, particularly for patients receiving combination ICB, which is associated with a significantly higher risk of severe irAEs to monotherapy. This is especially relevant in patients with melanoma with brain metastases or high tumor burden, where combination therapy is often selected as standard of care due to its improved clinical outcomes in this high-risk setting. 20 21 While careful follow-up is warranted for all patients, those treated with combination regimens require enhanced surveillance for early recognition and management of toxicities.

## Discussion

Discussion

Our study provides valuable insights into irAEs following a single dose of anti-PD-(L)1 blockade, with a focus on their frequency, severity, and lack of predictive risk factors. Thanks to a large, prospective cohort, we reported that severe (grade 3–4, 34.7%) and even fatal irAEs (grade 5, 4.3%) can occur at a substantial rate in the first 3 weeks after a first dose of anti-PD-1(L)1, highlighting the need for close monitoring after just one dose of anti-PD-(L)1 blockade. While single-dose regimens have been proposed to mitigate toxicity risks, the potential for severe irAEs remains a concern, as they can lead to treatment discontinuation or life-threatening complications. 18 22

Shulgin et al identified minimal dose dependence for irAEs in PD-1 blockade therapies due to their high specificity and saturating effects at lower doses. 23 This characteristic differentiates these therapies from conventional treatments, where a direct dose-response relationship is often observed. Hirsch et al advocated optimizing dosing regimens to reduce irAE risks and improve treatment efficacy, especially given that prolonged exposure to ICBs increases late-onset toxicity. 22

Evidence on toxicities following a single ICB dose remains limited, primarily derived from case reports and small neoadjuvant studies. 1824 26 Vos et al investigated a cohort of 54 early patients with breast cancer treated with a single dose of pembrolizumab in the neoadjuvant setting, finding that 41% experienced irAEs. However, all these events were mild (grade 1–2). 18 Similarly, Huang et al studied 29 patients with melanoma who received a single dose of PD-1 therapy, reporting that irAEs were predominantly mild or moderate, with no grade 4–5 events observed. 27 These findings suggest that single-dose regimens in neoadjuvant settings may be associated with a favorable safety profile. The broader applicability of these results remains constrained by the small sample sizes of the studies.

In contrast, our study showed that 41.4% of irAEs were severe or fatal (grade 3–5), underscoring a substantial difference in toxicity profiles. This difference likely reflects variations in patient populations, as patients with advanced cancer typically present with higher tumor burdens, which can provoke stronger immune responses and lead to more severe irAEs. 28 The broader time span of our study may have influenced findings, as irAEs management was less effective in the early years of therapy approval compared with the period after 2019, when standardized protocols were implemented. Despite similar severe irAE cases before 13 and after 2019, 14 standardized management may have mitigated risks and improved outcomes over time.

In our cohort, the majority of irAEs occurred within the first 20 days following a single dose of anti-PD-(L)1 therapy, with a median onset time of 14 days. Interestingly, the timing of irAE onset did not significantly differ between lower-grade, severe, and fatal events, suggesting that onset timing alone may not predict irAE severity. This finding contrasts with previous studies that reported shorter onset times for severe irAEs. 9 10 29 The lack of a clear pattern in our study could be attributed to the limited number of severe cases.

In our study, there were six cases of cardiotoxicities, such as myocarditis, aligning with literature that identifies myocarditis as a rare but life-threatening irAE with high fatality rates. 1930 32 We also observed four cases of severe pneumonitis (grade 3–4), a well-documented irAE with a fatality rate of up to 10–15%. 19 33 Additionally, four cases of immune-mediated hepatitis were recorded, occurring much earlier than the typical 8–12 weeks post-therapy initiation, with a median onset of 20.5 days. 34 35 These findings highlight the critical need for vigilant monitoring to promptly detect and manage irAEs, even when symptoms appear mild or non-specific initially.

Early and structured monitoring enables timely detection of adverse events and strengthens patient engagement. Oncology nurses play a pivotal role in educating patients, monitoring for early symptoms, and ensuring they know when and how to seek help, facilitating timely communication with the healthcare team to support effective management of adverse events and treatment adherence. 36 38 These close interactions build trust and improve communication, helping address concerns patients may hesitate to express initially. ICB expand to broader indications and combinations, structured monitoring pathways will be critical to improve safety and support treatment adherence.

Severe early irAEs following ICB therapy can affect multiple organs, often occurring within the first few weeks of treatment and posing life-threatening risks if not promptly identified and managed. Multiorgan irAEs were identified in 10% of patients in our study, often affecting combinations of the cardiac system, skin, and joints. Patients with ICB-induced myocarditis were more likely to experience multiorgan irAEs, complicating management and increasing the need for multidisciplinary care. 39 We could not demonstrate any difference in the onset timing of irAEs between patients with single organ irAE and those with multisystem irAEs in agreement with the findings of Shankar et al. 40 Severe multiorgan irAEs led to treatment discontinuation in four patients, yet two were successfully rechallenged with ICBs without recurrence of irAEs. This aligns with studies suggesting that rechallenging ICBs may be feasible for some patients under controlled conditions. 41 42 Interestingly, several studies have noted improved overall survival among patients who experience multiorgan irAEs compared with those who do not, reflecting the amplified immune response associated with these events. 40 43

Two of five patients with pre-existing autoimmune conditions in our cohort experienced multiorgan toxicities, suggesting that underlying autoimmune diseases may exacerbate irAE risk. While pre-existing autoimmune diseases are often considered a risk factor for irAEs, neither our study nor prior research established a direct correlation between these conditions and fatal outcomes. 44 46 Tang et al reinforced that while patients with autoimmune diseases may face a higher baseline risk for adverse events, their mortality rates during anti-PD-(L)1 therapy are comparable to those without such conditions. 47 These findings support the cautious but safe use of ICBs in patients with autoimmune diseases, provided they are closely monitored throughout treatment.

Combination ICB therapies, anti-PD-1/anti-CTLA-4, were associated with a higher incidence, broader range, and earlier onset of irAEs compared with monotherapy. 19 21 48 49 In our study, 77.2% of patients receiving ipilimumab/nivolumab experienced severe irAEs (grade 3–5), compared with 41.4% among those receiving anti-PD-(L)1 monotherapy. Additionally, multitoxicities were significantly more frequent in the combination therapy group (36.4%) than in the monotherapy group (10%).

While combination therapies synergistically enhance antitumor immunity, they also disrupt regulatory mechanisms, such as regulatory T cell-mediated tolerance (Treg), increasing the likelihood of irAEs. Early onset irAEs observed after a single dose of anti-PD-(L)1 or anti-PD-1/anti-CTLA-4 therapy may arise from the reactivation of latent autoimmune conditions, rather than exclusively inducing new immune responses. Notably, most autoimmune disease flares occur shortly after the initiation of ICB therapy. 50 51 The dose-dependent increase in irAEs associated with CTLA-4 inhibitors suggests that these therapies may unmask auto-reactive immune effectors that are normally suppressed by Tregs. 52 55 This challenges the notion that irAEs result exclusively from new immune activation, instead pointing to a dual mechanism involving both de novo immune responses and the reactivation of latent autoimmunity. Understanding and balancing these mechanisms is essential for optimizing ICB therapy and mitigating irAEs.

## Conclusion

Conclusion

This study highlights that even a single dose of anti-PD-(L)1 therapy can lead to severe or fatal irAEs, particularly in advanced or metastatic patients. With no clear predictive markers identified for early onset, multiple toxicities, or fatal outcomes, these findings emphasize the urgent need for reinforced patient monitoring, especially during the first month of treatment when early-onset irAEs are severe.

Additionally, the lack of predictive tools underscores the importance of identifying biomarkers to better stratify patients at risk and adjust treatment strategies accordingly. Our data also advocates for a more nuanced approach to ICB dosing regimens. Future research should focus on understanding the relationship between dosing schedules and safety profiles to optimize therapeutic outcomes while minimizing adverse events. Strengthened monitoring and predictive tools are essential to ensure safer and more effective use of ICBs in clinical practice.
