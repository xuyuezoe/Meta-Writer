# Pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy for advanced esophageal cancer: 5-year extended follow-up for the randomized phase III KEYNOTE-590 study.

## Abstract

Background: The global KEYNOTE-590 trial was the first to show a significant improvement in survival with immunotherapy plus chemotherapy in the first-line treatment of esophageal cancer (EC) and supported the approval of pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in this setting. After a median follow-up of 22.6 months, pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy compared with placebo plus chemotherapy had a manageable safety profile and significantly improved survival of participants with previously untreated advanced EC. Results of a median follow-up of 58.8 months (range 49.2-70.6 months) are reported.

Patients and methods: Adults with previously untreated advanced EC or Siewert type 1 gastroesophageal junction cancer were randomly assigned 1 : 1 to receive chemotherapy with or without pembrolizumab. Study outcomes were efficacy and safety.

Results: Overall, 749 participants received pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (n = 373) or placebo plus chemotherapy (n = 376). Median overall survival was 12.3 months versus 9.8 months [hazard ratio (HR) 0.72, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.62-0.84] with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy; 5-year survival rates were 10.6% and 3.0%. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 6.3 months versus 5.8 months (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.54-0.75); 5-year PFS rates were 5.5% and not reached. Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 71.9% and 67.6% of participants in the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy and placebo plus chemotherapy groups, respectively.

Conclusion: Data from KEYNOTE-590 continue to show durable efficacy after 5 years, and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy more than tripled the 5-year overall survival rate compared with placebo plus chemotherapy, with no new safety signals. These results support first-line therapy with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy as standard for previously untreated patients with advanced EC with programmed death-ligand 1 combined positive score ≥1.

## Introduction

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is frequently diagnosed at a late stage with a poor prognosis; in the United States, it is estimated that in 2025 there will be 22 070 new cases and 16 250 deaths from EC. 1 , 2 The 5-year overall survival (OS) rate for EC is ∼20%. 1 , 2 Globally, the incidence of esophageal cancer in 2022 was estimated at 511 000 new cases with incidence rates varying significantly by region, with the highest rates observed in Eastern Asia and Eastern Africa. 3 The most common type of EC globally is squamous-cell carcinoma, whereas adenocarcinoma is more prevalent in Western countries. 1 The KEYNOTE-590 study evaluated first-line pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy in participants with advanced EC. KEYNOTE-590 was the first randomized phase III study to show statistically significant and clinically meaningful survival benefits and manageable safety with a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor in participants with esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma (ESCC) or adenocarcinoma (EAC). 4 The primary end points of OS and progression-free survival (PFS) were met at the first analysis, leading to the approval of this regimen by the United States Food and Drug Administration and a change in practice. Results after 5 years of follow-up are presented.

## Material and methods

Material and methods

Study design and participants

KEYNOTE-590 was a global randomized phase III study (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03189719 ). Detailed methods were reported previously. 4 Eligible participants had previously untreated histologically or cytologically confirmed locally advanced unresectable or metastatic EAC or ESCC or Siewert type 1 gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) adenocarcinoma, at least one measurable lesion per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors, version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1) per the investigator or radiology assessment, and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score of 0 or 1. 1 Participants were enrolled regardless of tumor programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) status. Histologic classification was based on the American Joint Committee on Cancer, version 7 guidelines, which was the guideline at the time of study protocol initiation. The study was conducted in accordance with principles of Good Clinical Practice and was approved by the appropriate institutional review boards and regulatory agencies. Written informed consent was provided by all participants before enrollment.

Study procedures

Participants were randomly assigned 1 : 1 to receive pembrolizumab 200 mg or saline placebo intravenously once every 3 weeks for ≤35 cycles plus chemotherapy (5-fluorouracil 800 mg/m 2 on days 1-5 and cisplatin 80 mg/m 2 on day 1 for six or fewer cycles). Randomization was stratified by geographic region (Asia versus non-Asia), tumor histology (ESCC versus EAC), and ECOG performance status score (0 versus 1). No crossover between treatment groups was allowed. Administration of study drug continued until confirmed disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, intercurrent illness that prevented further administration, physician or participant decision to withdraw, nonadherence to study drug regimen or procedure requirements, completion of 35 cycles of treatment with study drug or achievement of complete response, or discontinuation for administrative reasons.

Assessments and end points

Dual primary end points were OS and PFS per RECIST v1.1 by investigator assessment. Secondary end points included objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DOR), and safety and tolerability.

Tumor response was assessed by the investigators locally at week 9 from the date of random assignment and every 9 weeks thereafter per RECIST v1.1. Disease progression was verified by central imaging review. During follow-up, survival was assessed every 12 weeks until death, withdrawal of consent, or end of the study, whichever occurred first. Adverse events (AEs) were evaluated throughout the study and 30 days (90 days for serious AEs and AEs of clinical interest) after treatment using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 4.0.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis methodology has been described previously. 4 , 5 Briefly, efficacy was assessed in all randomly assigned participants. Safety and tolerability were assessed for all randomly assigned participants who received at least one dose of study treatment. Post hoc analyses were conducted to evaluate between-group treatment differences by histology and PD-L1 status and liver metastasis status.

The statistical analysis plan is provided in the study protocol (online only). The protocol prespecified an interim analysis, which was the time point for testing interim OS and final PFS data. At the interim analysis (median follow-up, 22.6 months), OS and PFS met the prespecified boundary for statistical superiority; therefore, the interim analysis was considered the final time point for formal statistical analyses. No formal hypothesis testing was conducted for this follow-up analysis.

Statistical analyses were done using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC). The database cut-off date was 10 July 2023.

## Results

Results

Participants

Overall, 749 participants were randomly assigned to receive pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy ( n = 373) or placebo plus chemotherapy ( n = 376; Figure 1 ). Baseline characteristics were generally balanced between treatment groups ( Table 1 ). 4 Median follow-up [time from randomization to data cut-off (10 July 2023)] was 58.8 months (range 49.2-70.6 months). The most common reason for treatment discontinuation was disease progression ( Figure 1 ). A total of 180 participants (48.6%) in the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group and 188 participants (50.8%) in the placebo plus chemotherapy group received subsequent anticancer therapy; 34 (9.2%) and 49 participants (13.2%), respectively, received subsequent immunotherapy ( Supplementary Table S1 , available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105854 ). Duration of treatment exposure

The median duration of treatment exposure was 5.7 months (range 0-39.8 months) in the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group and 5.1 months (range 0.1-26.6 months) in the placebo plus chemotherapy group. The median number of treatment cycles was 8 (range 1-35) for pembrolizumab, 6 (range 1-6) for cisplatin, and 6 (range 1-35) for 5-fluorouracil in the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group and 7 (range 1-35) for placebo, 6 (range 1-6) for cisplatin, and 6 (range 1-35) for 5-fluorouracil in the placebo plus chemotherapy group.

Efficacy

Median OS with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy was 12.3 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 10.5-14.0 months] versus 9.8 months [95% CI 8.8-11.0 months, hazard ratio (HR) 0.72, 95% CI 0.62-0.84] for all randomly assigned participants. Median PFS with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy was 6.3 months (95% CI 6.2-7.1 months) versus 5.8 months (95% CI 5.0-6.0 months, HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.54-0.75) for all randomly assigned participants ( Figure 2 ). In all randomly assigned participants, 5-year OS rates were 10.6% with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy and 3.0% with placebo plus chemotherapy; 5-year PFS rates were 5.5% and 0%, respectively. Among the 17 participants who survived ≥5 years, 12 (70.6%) were in the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group ( Supplementary Table S2 , available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105854 ). ## Discussion

Discussion

Until recently, a lack of innovative treatment options meant that survival rates were low for patients with EC. KEYNOTE-590 showed that pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy provided clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvement with manageable safety for patients with EAC or ESCC, leading to a global change in practice. 4 After 5 years, pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy continued to provide clinically meaningful benefit compared with placebo plus chemotherapy in participants with advanced EC in the intention-to-treat population; the safety profile remained manageable with no new safety signals. 4 , 6 OS and PFS rates in the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group (10.6% and 5.5%, respectively) continued to be higher at 5 years than in the placebo plus chemotherapy group in all study populations, and participants with PD-L1 CPS ≥1 tumors experienced clinical benefit, whereas those with PD-L1 CPS <1 did not. Among 17 participants who survived ≥5 years, the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group included three participants (25%) with liver metastases at baseline. Rates of subsequent anticancer therapy were similar between treatment groups (pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, 48.6%; placebo plus chemotherapy, 50.8%). Results from this long-term follow-up analysis suggest an increased possibility of long-term survival with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy.

The survival benefit observed in KEYNOTE-590 (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.62-0.84) was consistent with that of published reports for other PD-1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment of patients with advanced EC. 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 The observed OS benefit with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy was similar to the benefit observed after 3 years of follow-up in the CheckMate 649 study of nivolumab plus chemotherapy as first-line therapy versus chemotherapy alone in patients with advanced gastric/GEJ cancer (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.71-0.88). 11 Similar OS benefit with other PD-1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy compared with placebo plus chemotherapy was observed in the ESCORT-1st (advanced ESCC), 8 ORIENT-16 (advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma), 12 RATIONALE-306 (in advanced/metastatic ESCC), 13 and JUPITER-06 (in advanced ESCC) studies. 7 However, subgroup analyses across these studies consistently show that PD-L1 negative patients do not derive meaningful survival benefit from the addition of PD-1 inhibitors to chemotherapy. Furthermore, patients with adenocarcinoma histology generally show a lower magnitude of benefit compared with those with squamous-cell carcinoma which is supported by a 5-year OS rate of 7.1% versus 11.8%, respectively, in the current study. Long-term survivors in KEYNOTE-590 were more frequently observed among patients with squamous histology and PD-L1 CPS ≥10. Among patients alive at 5 years, approximately one-third had liver metastases, and some were rendered disease-free with treatment. Although microsatellite instability (MSI) status was not routinely reported in KEYNOTE-590, data from CheckMate 649 indicate that MSI-high tumors are associated with a greater magnitude of benefit from PD-1 blockade, suggesting a potential biomarker for durable response. 11

To our knowledge, this is the first report of 5-year survival data for immunotherapy in EC, with an encouraging long-term survival rate of 10.6%. Notably, strengths of the current study include the 5-year survival rates observed in all subsets, especially in ESCC, and the 5-year survival rates for pembrolizumab in participants with liver metastases. Direct comparisons between KEYNOTE-590 and other studies should be interpreted with caution because of differences in study design, participant demographics and disease characteristics, sample sizes, follow-up duration, and chemotherapy regimen. In addition, the small sample sizes of the subgroup analysis preclude definitive conclusions.

Conclusions

In this 5-year follow-up analysis, first-line pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy had a manageable safety profile and continued to result in improved OS and PFS compared with placebo plus chemotherapy for participants with advanced EC. These results continue to support pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment of advanced EC in patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1.
