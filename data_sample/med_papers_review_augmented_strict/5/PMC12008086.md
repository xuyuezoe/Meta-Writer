# Efficacy and safety of RC48 in combination with PD-1 inhibitors for the treatment of locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma: a single-center, real-world study.

## Abstract

Objective: This study aims to assess the efficacy and safety of RC48 in combination with PD-1 inhibitors for patients diagnosed with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (mUC).

Methods: A retrospective analysis was performed on clinical data from 53 patients with locally advanced or metastatic bladder cancer, who were treated at the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical College between January 2023 and December 2023. The cohort was stratified into two groups: the RC48 combined immunotherapy group (RC48 + PD-1, n = 27) and the conventional chemotherapy group (gemcitabine and cisplatin regimen, GP, n = 26).

Results: The RC48 + PD-1 group demonstrated significantly higher objective response rates (ORR) and median progression-free survival (PFS) compared to the chemotherapy group (P < 0.05). Notably, the incidence of grade 3 or higher adverse events was elevated in the chemotherapy cohort, predominantly due to hematologic toxicities, with no treatment-related fatalities reported. In contrast, the RC48 combined PD-1 group primarily experienced immune-related adverse events, without any incidents of grade 3 or higher adverse effects or treatment-related deaths.

Conclusion: The combination of RC48 and PD-1 inhibitors exhibits promising antitumor activity and a manageable safety profile in patients with locally advanced or metastatic UC.

## Introduction

Introduction

Urothelial carcinoma (UC), a menacing and pervasive malignant neoplasm within the urinary system, has witnessed a relentless rise in global incidence year after year. In the case of metastatic urothelial carcinoma (mUC), despite the implementation of aggressive treatment strategies, the prognosis remains profoundly unfavorable, characterized by a median overall survival (OS) of approximately 15 months [ 1 ]. Currently, cisplatin-based chemotherapy regimens stand as the primary therapeutic choice for mUC, achieving an overall response rate (ORR) of 44.6%, a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 7.8 months, and a median overall survival (OS) of 14.0 months [ 2 ]. The patients with karnofsky performance status (KPS) less than 80% and visceral (lung, liver, or bone) metastasis have poorer prognosis [ 3 ]. Nevertheless, nearly half of patients with mUC are unable to endure cisplatin-based chemotherapy regimens due to factors such as renal insufficiency physical frailty, or the presence of other comorbidities [ 4 ].

The PD-1 (programmed death-1)/PD-L1 (programmed death-ligand 1) axis represents a pivotal immune evasion mechanism exploited by tumor cells. Consequently, immunotherapy utilizing inhibitors of PD-1 and PD-L1 has emerged as a cornerstone therapeutic strategy for an array of solid malignancies, including UC [ 5 , 6 ]. Immune therapies directed against PD-1 and PD-L1 have demonstrated extraordinary efficacy in mUC patients who are refractory to platinum-based chemotherapy, leading to their sequential approval for second-line treatment and heralding a transformative era of immunotherapy for advanced UC [ 7 – 10 ]. Currently, clinical studies are underway on the application of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy or anti-HER-2 ADC drugs in the first-line treatment of mUC.

RC48-ADC (disitamab vedotin, DV), a humanized HER2-targeted antibody–drug conjugate that links monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE) to the antibody via a cleavable linker, represents a notable advancement in the treatment landscape for mUC. Following favorable outcomes from the RC48-C005 [ 11 ] and RC48-C009 [ 12 ] clinical trials, RC48-ADC received approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and Chinese National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). Furthermore, in an ongoing Phase Ib/II trial (RC48-C014), the combination of RC48-ADC with the PD-1 antibody toripalimab demonstrated an ORR of 73.9% in previously untreated metastatic UC patients, alongside a favorable tolerability profile [ 13 ]. Based on the results from RC48-C014 study, the combination therapy of RC48-ADC and toripalimab was recommended application to mUC patients as a first-line treatment by Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guideline in 2022.

To date, the combination therapy of RC48 and PD-1 inhibitors in the treatment of UC has not been thoroughly documented, and the available evidence regarding its safety and efficacy predominantly derives from phase II clinical trials. Due to the stringent participant selection criteria often employed in these trials, specific populations, including elderly patients, individuals with renal impairment, and those with severe comorbidities, are frequently excluded. Consequently, this study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of RC48 in a more comprehensive cohort of patients with la/mUC. Utilizing real-world data, we seek to assess the effectiveness and safety of the RC48 and PD-1 inhibitor combination compared to conventional chemotherapy within actual clinical practice settings. This retrospective analysis intends to investigate the efficacy and safety of the RC48 and PD-1 inhibitor combination in treating a more diverse patient population than typically represented in standard chemotherapy regimens.

## Materials and methods

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

This study is a single-center, non-interventional, retrospective analysis conducted in a real-world setting. It included patients with la/mUC who received either RC48 combined immunotherapy (RC48 + PD-1) or gemcitabine plus cisplatin (GP) as first-line treatment at the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University between January 1, 2023, and December 31, 2023. A variety of clinical characteristics were collected from the patients, including age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS), HER2 expression level, PD-L1 expression status, primary tumor location, sites of metastasis, and treatment-related adverse reactions.

Patient selection for treatment

In clinical practice, the choice of treatment regimen (RC48 combined with PD-1 inhibitors [RC48 + PD-1] vs. gemcitabine plus cisplatin [GP]) was determined by the treating physician based on a comprehensive evaluation of the following factors: ECOG PS: Patients with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–1 were considered eligible for either regimen. Given that ECOG PS ≥ 2 patients have lower tolerance for traditional chemotherapy and face potential treatment—related risks, clinicians may choose monotherapy or combination therapy for them after weighing the pros and cons.

Renal Function: GP was contraindicated in patients with impaired renal function (creatinine clearance < 60 mL/min). These patients were preferentially offered RC48 + PD-1 as an alternative.

HER2 Expression Status: RC48 + PD-1 was considered for patients with HER2 expression ≥ 1 + (assessed by immunohistochemistry), given RC48’s HER2-targeted mechanism. However, treatment decisions were individualized. Some HER2-expressing patients might choose chemotherapy or combination therapy after evaluation. A few patients initially on chemotherapy switched to the combination therapy due to side effects intolerance.

Patient Preference and Comorbidities: Patients who declined chemotherapy due to concerns about toxicity (e.g., nausea, myelosuppression) were offered RC48 + PD-1. Individuals with significant comorbidities (e.g., cardiovascular disease, diabetes) that could exacerbate chemotherapy-related toxicities were more likely to receive RC48 + PD-1.

Inclusion criteria

1.Patients aged 18 years or older; 2.Histopathological diagnosis of UC, with clinical staging classified as locally advanced or metastatic UC; 3.The patients with treatment of RC48 combined PD-1 inhibitors had HER2 expression levels of at least 1 + by immunohistochemical testing and cannot accept the chemotherapy; 4. No prior treatment or completion of postoperative treatment more than six months prior; 5. Confirmation of at least one measurable lesion according to RECIST 1.1 criteria via CT or MRI imaging; 6. Normal function of major organs (including renal function); 7. Availability of complete clinical data and regularly scheduled follow-up data.

Exclusion criteria

1. A history of solid organ transplantation or bone marrow transplantation; 2. Acute autoimmune diseases requiring systemic treatment; 3. Presence of autoimmune deficiencies or need for steroids and other immunosuppressive therapies; 4. Known hypersensitivity or allergic reactions to any components of the study treatment; 5. Pregnancy or breastfeeding; 6.Inability to complete the study or provide informed consent.

The follow-up cutoff date was established as June 30, 2024. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All data acquisition was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University.

Treatment protocol

This study employed two treatment regimens: the RC48 combined with PD-1 inhibitors (RC48 + PD-1) and the gemcitabine and cisplatin (GP) regimen. RC48 + PD-1 Inhibitor Regimen: RC48 (produced by Rongchang Biopharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) was administered at a dose of 2 mg/kg intravenously every two weeks. For PD-1 inhibitors: Tislelizumab (produced by Guangzhou Baiyue Biopharma Co., Ltd.) was given at a dose of 200 mg intravenously every three weeks. Toripalimab (produced by Junshi Biosciences) was administered at a dose of 3 mg/kg, also intravenously every three weeks. Gemcitabine and Cisplatin (GP) Regimen: Gemcitabine was administered at a dose of 1.0 g/m 2 intravenously on Days 1 and 8 of each treatment cycle. Cisplatin was given at a dose of 75 mg/m 2 intravenously on Day 1 of each treatment cycle or alternatively at a dose of 20–30 mg/m 2 on Days 1 to 4 of each treatment cycle. Efficacy assessments were conducted every two months, and treatment continued until patients could no longer tolerate toxicity or disease progression occurred. All patients provided written informed consent prior to treatment initiation.

Follow up

Follow-up was conducted through a combination of inpatient medical records, outpatient visits, and telephone contacts. This process commenced with the initiation of treatment using RC48 + PD-1 inhibitors or GP and continued until patient death, disease progression, inability to tolerate treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), or voluntary discontinuation of treatment. The follow-up period extended until June 30, 2024. For patients receiving standard doses of RC48 (2.0 mg/kg), tislelizumab (200 mg), and toripalimab (3 mg/kg), any dose adjustments during treatment were documented in detail. The administration of PD-1 inhibitors was determined by the treating physician based on the patient’s clinical condition. The expression status of HER2 was primarily assessed through IHC testing, with IHC scores evaluated according to the HER2 testing guidelines for breast cancer [ 14 ].

Efficacy evaluation and adverse reactions

Efficacy evaluations were conducted based on the RECIST 1.1 criteria, which include complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease (PD). The objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) were recorded. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from the initiation of treatment to disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Duration of response (DOR) was defined as the time from the first assessment of CR, PR, or SD to the occurrence of PD. Tumor response evaluations were performed using computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in accordance with the RECIST 1.1 criteria. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were collected through patient self-reporting and laboratory test results, and they were assessed according to the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses in this study were performed using SPSS 25.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA), Prism 5.01 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA), and R 4.0.2 (The R Project for Statistical Computing). Descriptive statistics were initially employed to summarize the baseline characteristics, treatment management, antitumor activity, and safety data of the patients. Subsequently, the Kaplan–Meier method was utilized to estimate PFS, while a Cox proportional hazards model was applied for multivariate analysis of PFS. Additionally, logistic regression analysis was conducted to evaluate factors influencing the ORR and DCR. A two-sided P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all statistical tests.

## Discussion

Discussion

This study highlights the significant efficacy of RC48 combined with immunotherapy in patients with 1a/mUC. Compared to traditional chemotherapy, the mPFS for patients treated with RC48 + PD-1 (8.53 months) was significantly superior to that of the chemotherapy group (5.87 months). The ORR was 77.78% versus 46.15%, demonstrating a significant advantage over the chemotherapy group. In the past decade, advancements in sequencing technology have deepened our understanding of the pathogenesis of bladder cancer and expanded treatment options. Given the high mutation burden in bladder cancer, ICIs have become a recommended treatment for some patients with advanced UC. Additionally, novel therapeutic strategies targeting molecular markers such as HER2, including antibody–drug conjugates and fibroblast growth factor receptor inhibitors, are currently under development. HER2 overexpression is associated with tumor progression and poor prognosis [ 15 ], compared to HER2-negative muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) patients, those with HER2-overexpressing MIBC exhibit greater resistance to conventional chemotherapy and radiotherapy [ 16 ]. However, the anti-tumor effects of HER2 antibodies and tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in HER2-positive urothelial carcinoma have not met expectations and face certain challenges [ 17 , 18 ]. Antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) are novel therapeutics that combine the targeting ability of monoclonal antibodies with the cytotoxicity of chemotherapeutic agents, significantly reducing the potential toxicity associated with traditional chemotherapy drugs [ 19 , 20 ]. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that ADCs exhibit superior efficacy against specific targets compared to the use of antibodies alone [ 21 , 22 ]. RC48 is an innovative ADC that contains a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting HER2, a member of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) family. The small molecule toxin carried by RC48 is monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE), a synthetic derivative from the auristatin family known for its potent antimitotic activity, widely utilized as a cytotoxic component in ADCs [ 23 , 24 ]. In RC48, MMAE is linked to the antibody via a cleavable linker that remains stable in the bloodstream but releases the toxin in the presence of specific intracellular proteases. The valine-citrulline linker covalently binds MMAE to the antibody, ensuring a very stable connection during circulation; only after the ADC is internalized by tumor cells and enters the lysosome does the release of MMAE occur, leading to the destruction of the target cancer cells [ 25 ]. This design ensures precise drug delivery while minimizing damage to healthy cells, enhancing therapeutic efficacy and reducing side effects.

A series of published multicenter randomized controlled trial (RCT) results have allowed RC48-ADC to overcome the limitations of traditional HER2-targeted therapies, providing new options for patients with mUC. Two key phase II studies, RC48-C005 and RC48-C009, thoroughly investigated the efficacy of RC48 in mUC patients who had received at least one line of systemic chemotherapy.The RC48-C005 study included 43 patients, reporting an ORR of 51.2%, mPFS of 6.9 months, and mOS of 13.9 months. Notably, among patients with liver metastases, the ORR reached as high as 65%, and even in the group with low HER2 positivity, the ORR was 40% [ 11 ]. The RC48-C009 study comprised 64 patients, yielding an ORR of 46.9%, with a median PFS of 4.3 months and a median OS of 14.8 months [ 12 ]. Compared to standard treatment based on platinum chemotherapy, which has a median OS of approximately 14–15 months, the efficacy of RC48 is comparable.Furthermore, RC48 combined therapy exhibited relatively fewer side effects compared to traditional chemotherapy, resulting in better patient tolerability and significantly enhancing treatment adherence. Based on these favorable outcomes, RC48-ADC was designated as a breakthrough therapy by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for use as second-line treatment in patients with locally advanced or mUC who have progressed after platinum-based chemotherapy and exhibit HER2 overexpression.

Although ADC monotherapy has shown significant survival benefits in cancer treatment, there remains a desire for improvement. On one hand, researchers are focused on maximizing the anti-tumor effects of ADCs; on the other hand, addressing the issue of resistance to ADC monotherapy has led to an increasing interest in combination strategies. Previous studies have demonstrated that the combination of RC48-ADC with PD-1 antibodies can produce synergistic anti-tumor effects [ 26 , 27 ]. In the Phase Ib/II clinical trial NCT04264936 (RC48-C014), the safety and pharmacokinetic characteristics of RC48 combined with toripalimab were assessed in patients with mUC. The study included a total of 41 mUC patients, of whom 61% had not previously received systemic treatment, 54% had visceral metastases, 59% were HER2-positive, and 32% were PD-L1-positive.The results showed a confirmed ORR of 76.7%, including a 10% rate of complete response, with a DCR as high as 96.7%. Although data for mPFS and mOS have not yet matured, the combination therapy of RC48 with toripalimab demonstrated good tolerability and significant anti-tumor activity in patients with mUC [ 28 ]. Additionally, in a real-world study involving 40 patients who received either RC48 monotherapy or combined treatment with ICIs, ORR and mPFS were reported as 63.2% and 8.2 months [ 29 ]. In another multicenter real-world study examining RC48 alone or in combination with immunotherapy for locally advanced or mUC, 36 patients were included; among them, 18 received RC48 monotherapy, while the other 18 received RC48 in combination with a PD-1 antibody. The median PFS for patients receiving RC48 combined with immunotherapy was 8.5 months, compared to 5.4 months for those receiving RC48 alone. The conclusion drawn from these studies is that both RC48 monotherapy and its combination with immunotherapy can benefit patients with locally advanced or metastatic bladder cancer (BCa), regardless of whether their renal function is impaired [ 30 ]. The HOPE-03 trial is a multicenter, open-label Phase Ib/II study designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of RC48-ADC in combination with toripalimab as neoadjuvant therapy for HER2-positive locally advanced MIBC. Preliminary efficacy assessments indicated that out of four patients, three achieved T0 status and one achieved Tis status, resulting in a complete response rate (cCR) of 100%. Adverse events included grade 2 myositis in one patient and grade 1 elevations in transaminases in another patient, along with grade 1 elevations in transaminases, erythema, and sensory abnormalities in three other patients.The combination therapy of RC48-ADC demonstrates better safety compared to other ADCs. In this study, 27 patients with locally advanced or mUC who receiving RC48 in conjunction with a PD-1 inhibitor showed an ORR of 77.78%. Another study showed a ORR of 88.9, but that study did not include the metastatic bladder cancer patients [ 29 ]. Our result is similar to that of the RC48-C014 study [ 13 ] (76.7%) but higher than the 38.8% reported by Chen et al. [ 30 ]. The mPFS was 8.53 months, slightly lower than the 9.2 months observed in the RC48-C014 study, but consistent with the real-world data from Chen et al. (8.5 months). ## Conclusion

Conclusion

In patients with locally advanced or mUC with HER2 expression (IHC 1 + /2 + /3 +), the combination of RC48 and PD-1 inhibitors showed significant improvements in PFS and ORR. In this real-world study, the promising safety profile and anti-tumor activity of RC48 combined with PD-1 inhibitors provide a solid foundation for exploring phase II clinical trials of this combination therapy in patients with locally advanced or mUC.
