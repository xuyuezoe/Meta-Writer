# Effects of anlotinib combined with camrelizumab and chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma on tumor immune microenvironment.

## Abstract

Background: Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) presents significant therapeutic challenges due to limited treatment options. While immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) combined with chemotherapy have demonstrated efficacy, variable response rates underscore the necessity for enhanced therapeutic approaches. Anlotinib, a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor, has shown synergistic effects with ICIs in clinical practice. However, its influence on the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) in ESCC requires further elucidation. This study aimed to investigate anlotinib's effects on the TIME.

Methods: In our phase II trial (NCT04471480), we analyzed 8 advanced ESCC patients who achieved operability following treatment with anlotinib, camrelizumab, and chemotherapy (TCAC group). A control group (TCC group) comprised 8 patients from a separate neoadjuvant trial who received identical treatment, excluding anlotinib. Tumor-infiltrating immune cell populations (CD4^+, CD8^+, CD20^+, CD68^+, FOXP3^+) were evaluated using multiplex immunofluorescence. Additional mechanistic insights were obtained through RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).

Results: Baseline immune cell profiles showed no significant intergroup differences. Post-treatment analysis revealed the TCAC group had significantly elevated CD8^+ cell proportions and reduced FOXP3^+ cell proportions compared to controls (P<0.05). Within the TCAC cohort, pretreatment immune cell distributions were comparable between pathological complete response (pCR) and non-pCR patients. However, post-treatment pCR patients demonstrated significantly higher CD8^+ and lower FOXP3^+ cell levels versus non-pCR cases (P<0.05). RNA-seq and ELISA analyses suggested anlotinib's potential to enhance the immune microenvironment through C-C motif chemokine ligand 5 (CCL5) upregulation.

Conclusions: The combination of anlotinib with camrelizumab and chemotherapy appears to modify the ESCC immune microenvironment by promoting cytotoxic CD8^+ T cell infiltration while suppressing FOXP3^+ regulatory T cells, possibly mediated through CCL5 induction. These results warrant further investigation of anlotinib's immunomodulatory potential in ESCC combination therapies.

Highlight box

Key findings

• After treatment with anlotinib for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, the immune microenvironment can be reshaped, specifically manifested by a significant increase in cytotoxic CD8 + T cells in the tumor microenvironment and a significant decrease in immunosuppressive FOXP3 + regulatory T cells.

What is known and what is new?

• Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) has limited treatment options. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) (e.g., camrelizumab) plus chemotherapy show variable efficacy. Anlotinib (a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor) exhibits synergy with ICIs in other cancers, but its impact on ESCC tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) is unclear.

• Anlotinib combined with camrelizumab/chemotherapy (TCAC) significantly increased cytotoxic CD8 + T cell infiltration and reduced immunosuppressive FOXP3 + regulatory T cells (Tregs) versus controls (TCC: camrelizumab/chemotherapy alone; P<0.05). Pathological complete response (pCR) patients in the TCAC cohort showed higher post-treatment CD8 + T cells and lower FOXP3 + Tregs than non-pCR cases (P<0.05). Anlotinib potentially enhances TIME via C-C motif chemokine ligand 5 (CCL5) upregulation [supported by RNA sequencing and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)].

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• TCAC reprograms TIME to favor antitumor immunity, providing a novel strategy to improve ESCC therapy. Validate findings in large-scale phase III trials assessing TCAC for advanced/neoadjuvant ESCC. Investigate CCL5-mediated immunomodulation to optimize combination regimens.

## Introduction

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have shown encouraging results in the treatment of various solid tumors. However, only a limited number of patients benefit from these therapies. Moreover, most patients who initially respond to ICIs ultimately develop drug resistance, leading to disease recurrence ( 1 - 3 ). Currently, programmed death-1 (PD-1) and programmed cell death ligand-1 (PD-L1) monoclonal antibodies are the most widely used ICIs. These antibodies restore the anti-tumor activity of T lymphocytes, enhance immune responses, and inhibit tumor cell proliferation and metastasis by blocking the interaction between PD-1 and PD-L1 ( 4 ). A successful anti-tumor immune response requires a complete “cancer immune cycle”, including T cell initiation and activation, immune cell recruitment, and the recognition and elimination of cancer cells. Among these processes, the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) plays a crucial role in determining the therapeutic efficacy of ICIs ( 5 , 6 ). The tumor microenvironment (TME) is composed of various immune cells [such as T cells, B cells, dendritic cells (DCs), and tumor-associated macrophages], stromal cells (including tumor-associated fibroblasts, pericytes, and mesenchymal stromal cells), the extracellular matrix, and secreted molecules like growth factors, cytokines, and chemokines. It also includes blood and lymphatic vascular networks ( 7 - 9 ). Each component within the TME uniquely influences tumor immune escape, impacting tumor initiation, growth, invasion, and metastasis ( 10 , 11 ). Additionally, the TME contributes to the primary and acquired resistance of tumors to conventional therapies ( 12 , 13 ). Therefore, targeting and reprogramming both the cellular and non-cellular components of the TME may enhance the effectiveness of conventional cancer treatments, presenting a promising strategy for future cancer therapy.

One of the primary indicators of immune suppression in the TME is the inactivation of cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs). Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) directly impairs the function of CD8 + T cells and DCs while promoting the activity of regulatory T cells (Tregs) and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). Additionally, vascular leakage and poor perfusion contribute to tumor hypoxia, which limits CTL recruitment and increases T cell apoptosis. This hypoxic state further upregulates VEGF expression in the tumor, creating a self-sustaining cycle of immune suppression. The majority of tumors (>80%) exhibit immune rejection or an immune desert phenotype, leaving only a minority of patients responsive to immunotherapy. Anti-angiogenic agents can convert “cold” tumors to “hot” tumors ( 13 ). Combining anti-angiogenesis therapy with immune checkpoint blockade can eliminate or repolarize tumor-promoting immune cells and recruit anti-tumor immune cells, thereby reshaping both the local and systemic immune environments. This synergy enhances therapeutic efficacy ( 14 ). Preclinical and clinical studies have demonstrated that the tumor vascular system is a critical factor in determining whether immunotherapy induces anti-tumor responses ( 5 , 6 , 15 , 16 ). Therefore, vascular targeting is a promising approach to improve cancer immunotherapy outcomes. Anlotinib, a small molecule multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor, effectively inhibits kinases such as VEGF receptor 1-3 (VEGFR1-3), platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha/beta (PDGFR α/β), fibroblast growth factor receptor 1-4 (FGFR1-4), c-Kit, etc. Previous studies ( 17 - 19 ) have shown that anlotinib reprograms the TME, including the remodeling of tumor vasculature, tumor cells, and the immune microenvironment, offering new avenues to enhance treatment efficacy. Our study ( NCT04471480 ) ( 20 , 21 ) also confirmed that the addition of anlotinib to immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy can significantly improve the objective remission rate (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS) in advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) patients. However, the underlying mechanisms remain unclear, and the effects of anlotinib combined with immunotherapy and chemotherapy on the TIME in humans have not yet been reported.

In this study, we investigated the combined efficacy of anlotinib, camrelizumab, and chemotherapy using tissue samples and clinical data from patients with ESCC. To understand the underlying mechanisms of the observed therapeutic effects, we employed multiple immunofluorescence (mIF) detection to analyze the spatiotemporal distribution of tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIICs). This approach allowed us to explore the impact of anlotinib in combination with camrelizumab and chemotherapy on the reprogramming of the immune microenvironment in ESCC. mIF enables the staining and quantification of multiple antigens within tissue sections at single-cell resolution. A previous study ( 22 ) has demonstrated that quantitative scoring using mIF can accurately predict prognosis in triple-negative breast cancer. Additionally, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) of esophageal squamous cancer cells (ECA109) revealed that C-C motif chemokine ligand 5 (CCL5) was significantly upregulated following anlotinib treatment. This finding was further validated in both cell culture supernatants and patient samples. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR reporting checklist (available at https://jgo.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jgo-2025-30/rc ).

## Methods

Methods

Human subjects

All participants were recruited from Daping Hospital, Chongqing, China. This study included patients in the study ( NCT04471480 ) who underwent surgical treatment through conversion therapy and had biopsy specimens before treatment and surgical specimens after treatment retained in our pathology department. An equal number of patients were randomly selected from another clinical study on neoadjuvant therapy for ESCC conducted in our department (ChiCTR2000035237) to serve as the control group (TCC group). These patients all met the following conditions: (I) they received neoadjuvant immunotherapy + chemotherapy; (II) there were sufficient preoperative biopsies and postoperative surgical specimens available for mIF detection. The exclusion criteria included patients without sufficient pathological specimens. Ultimately, each treatment group in this study comprised 8 adult participants. All subjects underwent medical history recording and postoperative multiple immunohistochemical testing of cancer tissue. The study was in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Daping Hospital (approval No. 2020-120 and No. 2020-84). All patients provided informed consent for the use of biological samples, and all patient data were anonymized.

Conversion therapy or neoadjuvant therapy

Patients in the TCAC group were treated with camrelizumab [200 mg, intravenously (IV) on day 1], 135–185 mg/m 2 paclitaxel (IV on day 1) or 240–260 mg/m 2 albumin-bound paclitaxel and 75 mg/m 2 cisplatin (IV on day 1) or carboplatin AUC 5 (IV on day 1), with oral anlotinib [8 mg, orally (PO) on days 1–14] treatment (drugs information are listed in Table S1 ). Patients in the TCC group received an identical treatment plan, except for anlotinib, which was excluded. Each therapy was administered at intervals of 21 days for 2–4 cycles.

Specimens collection and initial processing

Before treatment, tissue specimens were obtained from each patient via a biopsy performed using gastroscopy before treatment. All patients in this study underwent radical surgical resection, and after treatment, tissue specimens were obtained from postoperative surgical samples. All specimens were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 24 h, then processed into paraffin blocks and continuously sliced into 3 μm sections for the following procedures. Additionally, peripheral blood samples were collected from patients before treatment and surgery for future analysis.

Multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) staining

Before microwave treatment (MWT), the slides underwent dewaxing, rehydrating, and washing with tap water. Both endogenous peroxidase and protein blockade were performed using antibody diluents/blockers (72424205, PerkinElmer). Each antigen requires a round of labeling, including first antibody incubation, second antibody incubation, and tyramine signal amplification (TSA) visualization, followed by the labeling of the next antibody. The primary antibodies used were anti-CD8 antibody (Abcam Cat# ab178089, RRID:AB_2756374, 1:100 AB_3060471:200), anti-CD4 antibody (Abcam Cat# ab133616, RRID:AB_2750883, 1:100), anti-CD68 antibody (ZSGB-Bio Cat# ZM-0060, RRID:AB_2904190, 1:500), anti-FoxP3 antibody (Abcam Cat# ab20034, RRID:AB_445284, 1:100), anti-CD20 antibody (Abcam Cat# ab64088, RRID:AB_1139386, 1:100) and anti-panCK antibody (Abcam Cat# ab7753, RRID:AB_306047, 1:200). The Opal 7-Color IHC Kit (NEL811001KT, PerkinElmer) was utilized for TSA visualization. The PerkinElmer Vectra (Vectra 3.0.5, PerkinElmer) was used to scan the glass slide. CD8 + , CD4 + , CD68 + , CD20 + , and FOXP3 + cells in fields of view nearly encompassing the entire tumor and stromal compartments were quantified, and their ratios to total nucleated cells were calculated. Two pathologists confirmed the quality and results of the experiments.

Reagents

Anlotinib was provided by Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd.

Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA) was used to dissolve the drug or as a control. Working solutions were prepared by diluting the stock solution with RPMI 1640 medium. The final DMSO concentration was 0.1%.

Cell lines, culture conditions, and drug treatment

Human ECA109 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Roswell Park Memorial Institute, Pricella, PM150110, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Pricella, 164210-50, China) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Pricella, PB180120 , China) in a 5% CO 2 atmosphere at 37 °C. The cells were inoculated at a density of 2×10 5 cells per well in 96-well plates containing RPMI-1640 with 10% FBS. After overnight incubation at 37 °C in a 5% CO 2 incubator, the cells were allowed to grow until they reached 30–40% confluence. Subsequently, the experimental group (ECA109-Anlotinib) was treated with 5 μM anlotinib solution every 24 hours, while the control group (ECA109-CON) was treated with DMSO only. After 72 hours, cells were collected for RNA-seq analysis, and the supernatant was collected for enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) detection.

Total RNA extraction, library construction, sequencing, and data analysis

Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol ® reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Magen). RNA quality was assessed by measuring the A260/A280 absorbance ratio with a Nanodrop ND-2000 system (Thermo Scientific, USA), and the RNA integrity number (RIN) was determined using an Agilent Bioanalyzer 4150 system (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). Only RNA samples meeting quality standards were used for library construction. Paired-end libraries were prepared using an ABclonal mRNA-seq Lib Prep Kit (ABclonal, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Messenger RNA (mRNA) was purified from 1 μg of total RNA using oligo (dT) magnetic beads. The purified mRNA was then fragmented by incubation with divalent cations at elevated temperatures in the ABclonal First Strand Synthesis Reaction Buffer. Subsequently, first-strand complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using random hexamer primers and reverse transcriptase (RNase H) with mRNA fragments as templates. Second-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using DNA polymerase I, RNase H, buffer, and dNTPs. The synthesized double-stranded cDNA fragments were then adapter-ligated to prepare the paired-end library. Adaptor-ligated cDNA was subjected to pathological complete response (pCR) amplification, and the pCR products were purified using the AMPure XP system. Library quality was assessed on an Agilent Bioanalyzer 4150 system. Finally, sequencing was performed using an Illumina Novaseq 6000 or MGISEQ-T7 instrument.

The data generated from the Illumina/BGI platform were used for bioinformatics analysis. All analyses were performed using an in-house pipeline developed by Shanghai Applied Protein Technology. Raw data (or raw reads) in FASTQ format were initially processed using in-house Perl scripts. During this step, adapter sequences were removed, and low-quality reads (defined as those where the number of bases with a quality score of ≤25 accounts for more than 60% of the entire read) and reads with an N ratio greater than 5% (where N indicates undetermined base information) were filtered out. The remaining clean reads were then used for subsequent analysis. The clean reads were aligned to the reference genome in orientation mode using HISAT2 software ( http://daehwankimlab.github.io/hisat2/ ) to obtain mapped reads. FeatureCounts ( http://subread.sourceforge.net/ ) was used to count the number of reads mapped to each gene. The FPKM (Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads) for each gene was calculated based on the length of the gene and the number of reads mapped to it. Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 ( http://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/DESeq2.html ). Genes with |log2FC| >1 and an adjusted P value (P-adj) <0.05 were considered significantly differentially expressed. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed to explore the functional enrichment of differentially expressed genes and to clarify the functional differences between samples. Anlotinib regulates multiple immune microenvironment-related signaling pathways in ESCC

To investigate the mechanism by which anlotinib reprograms the TIME, we performed gene expression profiling on RNA-seq data from ECA109-CON and ECA109-anlotinib. Differential expression analysis revealed that 793 genes exhibited significant differences between the two groups (P-adj<0.05), with 536 genes upregulated and 256 genes downregulated ( Figure 5A,5B ). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis showed that these differentially expressed genes were significantly enriched in biological processes such as multicellular organismal processes, cell communication, and signaling ( Figure 5C ). Further KEGG pathway analysis indicated that these differential genes may be involved in immune response-related pathways and the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway ( Figure 5D ). These findings suggest that anlotinib may impact tumor cell proliferation, survival, and immune evasion mechanisms through multiple pathways, particularly those related to immune responses, potentially enhancing antitumor immunity and inhibiting tumor growth. Therefore, these differential genes and their associated pathways should be further investigated as potential drug targets or biomarkers to assist in cancer diagnosis and treatment. Notably, the gene CCL5 (P<0.0001) showed significant upregulation in the drug-treated group. ## Discussion

Discussion

ICIs have become promising treatments for various malignant tumors. Preclinical studies ( 23 , 24 ) have demonstrated that combining ICIs with chemotherapy enhances CD8 + T cell infiltration into tumors and boosts anti-tumor responses. Multiple phase III randomized controlled trials in patients with advanced or metastatic ESCC ( 25 - 31 ) indicate that PD-1 monoclonal antibody combined with chemotherapy surpasses chemotherapy alone. However, the ORR of this combination therapy ranged from only 37.3% to 72.1%. Another phase II study using a combination of camrelizumab, apatinib, and chemotherapy for advanced ESCC reported an ORR of 80% ( 32 ). Our study ( NCT04471480 ), however, increased the ORR to 90%, marking the first instance of this high response rate and suggesting a novel option for combination therapy in esophageal cancer, thus warranting further investigation into its underlying mechanisms.

Cancer cells influence immune cell activation by establishing a TIME around them ( 33 ). The TME is crucial in cancer progression and cell migration ( 34 ). Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) are a primary component of the TME, representing a naturally occurring heterogeneous population of lymphocytes, especially CD4 + and CD8 + T cells, that migrate into tumor tissue. These lymphocytes perform essential cytotoxic functions in the TME and play a role in ICI responses ( 8 ). CD8 + cytotoxic T cells recognize tumor antigens by binding to major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules on antigen-presenting cells (APCs) through their T cell receptors. This interaction triggers the release of cytotoxic molecules, including perforin and granzyme B, which induce tumor cell apoptosis, directly kill tumor cells, and establish immune memory via adaptive immune responses, enabling faster and more effective responses to the same antigen in the future ( 35 , 36 ). TILs may also contribute to immune suppression and tumor immune escape. For example, Treg cells possess immunosuppressive properties that hinder the immune system’s recognition and killing of tumor cells. Treg cells form “barriers” to pro-inflammatory cell infiltration by impairing peripheral T cell activation, causing vascular system dysfunction, preventing lymphoid structure formation, and obstructing cell migration to the tumor region and surrounding stroma ( 37 ). Effectively eliminating these immunosuppressive cells remains a major focus in cancer research, with two main strategies: reducing the number of Treg cells and inhibiting their suppressive functions.

Previous study ( 18 ) has shown that combining anlotinib with PD-L1 blockade enhances the immune microenvironment in mice by increasing CD8 + IFNγ + T cells and NK cells while reducing Treg cells in tumors. In line with these findings, our study confirms, for the first time in humans, that adding anlotinib to immunotherapy and chemotherapy reprograms the TIME. Specifically, compared to the group receiving only immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy, the TCAC group (anlotinib plus immunotherapy and chemotherapy) demonstrated a significant increase in the proportion of CD8 + cells and a reduction in FOXP3 + cells in tumor-infiltrating cells after treatment. Moreover, among patients achieving pCR in the TCAC group, a more pronounced increase in CD8 + cells was observed in the TME after treatment, while non-pCR patients showed a significant increase in FOXP3 + cells in the TME after treatment. These findings suggest that the TCAC regimen can enhance CD8 + cell and FOXP3 + cell composition after treatment by reprogramming the immune microenvironment, thereby achieving a synergistic effect.

Studies ( 15 , 16 ) on the mechanisms of anti-angiogenic therapy in TIME reprogramming have demonstrated that the highly abnormal vascular system in tumors contributes to immune evasion. Normalizing tumor blood vessels can improve tissue perfusion, increase immune effector cell infiltration, and enhance immunotherapy efficacy. Additionally, stimulating immune cell function has been found to promote tumor blood vessel normalization. This reciprocal regulation between blood vessel normalization and immune reprogramming creates a positive feedback loop that reprograms the TIME, fostering sustained anti-tumor immunity. Th1 cells may play a pivotal role in determining the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade and anti-angiogenic therapies ( 38 ). A separate study on neuroblastoma in a mouse model ( 6 ) demonstrated that the multi-target anti-angiogenic tyrosine kinase inhibitor anlotinib partially normalizes tumor blood vessels via CD4 + cells, reprogramming the TME from immunosuppressive to immunostimulatory and significantly inhibiting tumor growth. These foundational studies provide a theoretical basis for the combination of multi-target anti-angiogenic tyrosine kinase inhibitors and ICIs. Notably, our study observed that in the four-drug group treated with anlotinib, the proportion of CD8 + cells increased and FOXP3 + cells decreased in the TME after treatment, indicating a shift towards a more activated immune microenvironment. This finding confirms that adding anlotinib to immunochemotherapy can effectively enhance the tumor-infiltrating immune microenvironment.

Numerous basic studies have demonstrated that anlotinib can increase CD8 + T cell infiltration in animal models. The mechanisms include anlotinib-induced T cell inflammation in the TME through vascular normalization ( 6 ), downregulation of PD-L1 expression in endothelial cells to alter the TIME ( 39 ), targeting of transferrin receptor protein 1 (TFRC), and enhancement of CXCL14-mediated CD8 + T cell infiltration ( 40 ), among others. Our study demonstrates anlotinib’s significant modulation of the ESCC TIME, particularly through CCL5 upregulation. RNA-seq and ELISA validation showed increased CCL5 expression in both anlotinib-treated ECA109 cells and TCAC patient blood samples. CCL5-CCR5 signaling plays a crucial role in recruiting CD8 + T cells and NK cells to the TME ( 41 - 43 ). This suggests anlotinib enhances anti-tumor immunity in ESCC through CCL5-mediated immune cell infiltration, consistent with immunotherapy responses observed in other cancers. These findings establish CCL5 as a potential biomarker for monitoring anlotinib’s immunomodulatory effects in ESCC. Further studies should explore CCL5’s correlation with clinical outcomes and its relationship with other immune pathways to fully elucidate anlotinib’s mechanism of action.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations. The assessment of immune infiltration at a single time point may not fully reflect dynamic changes during treatment, particularly in the TCAC group with its high pCR rate, potentially leading to underestimation of after-treatment immune cell infiltration. The lack of preoperative biopsies and postoperative pCR specimens limits the mechanistic exploration of treatment effects. Sample composition imbalances, including the absence of stage IV patients in controls and unequal pCR distribution (4 vs. 1 case), may independently influence gene expression profiles and require cautious interpretation. Our surgical eligibility criteria inherently favored patients achieving objective response or stable disease, as progressive disease would typically contraindicate curative resection. This selection bias prioritizes responders, potentially limiting generalizability to aggressive or treatment-refractory ESCC populations. However, such bias is inherent to TME-focused clinical series where tissue accessibility depends on surgical feasibility. Despite this, our cohort offers valuable mechanistic insights into responders, guiding optimization of combination therapies for eligible patients.

## Conclusions

Conclusions

This study is the first to confirm the effect of combining anlotinib, camrelizumab, and chemotherapy on reprogramming the immune microenvironment in humans. These findings offer a robust theoretical foundation for developing more effective combination therapy strategies for advanced cancer patients.
