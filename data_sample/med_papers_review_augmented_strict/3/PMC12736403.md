# Addition of Carboplatin to Sequential Taxane-Anthracycline Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy in Triple-Negative Breast Cancer: A Phase III Randomized Controlled Trial.

## Abstract

Purpose: We evaluated the survival impact of adding platinum to standard taxane-anthracycline neoadjuvant chemotherapy in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC).

Methods: In this phase III trial, patients with TNBC were randomly assigned, after stratification by stage and menopausal status, to receive neoadjuvant chemotherapy comprising once-per-week carboplatin (AUC-2) plus paclitaxel (100 mg/m^2) for 8 weeks, followed by four cycles of anthracycline plus cyclophosphamide, or same chemotherapy without carboplatin. The primary end point was event-free survival (EFS), and secondary end points were overall survival (OS) and pathologic complete response. This is the prespecified primary analysis of this study.

Results: Of 720 patients randomly assigned between April 2010 and January 2020, 717 (platinum 361, control 356) were included in modified intention-to-treat analysis. At median follow-up of 67.6 months, in platinum and control arms, there were 111/361 and 131/356 EFS events (hazard ratio [HR], 0.80 [95% CI, 0.62 to 1.03]; two-sided unstratified P = .081), with 5-year EFS of 70.7% (95% CI, 65.8% to 75.6%) and 64.1% (95% CI, 59.0% to 69.2%), respectively, and 94/361 and 121/356 deaths (HR, 0.74 [95% CI, 0.57 to 0.97]; nominal P = .029), with 5-year OS of 74.4% and 66.8%, respectively. In premenopausal patients, EFS (HR, 0.61 [95% CI, 0.43 to 0.84]; nominal P = .003; 5-year EFS 75.0% v 59.6%) and OS (HR, 0.57 [95% CI, 0.40 to 0.82]; nominal P = .002; 5-year OS 78.2% v 64.6%) were significantly higher, while in postmenopausal patients, EFS (HR, 1.19 [95% CI, 0.80 to 1.78]; nominal P = .386) and OS (HR, 1.06 [95% CI, 0.70 to 1.61]; nominal P = .772) were not significantly different, in platinum versus control arm. There was statistically significant interaction between study intervention and menopausal status for EFS and OS, with a benefit of adding platinum in premenopausal but not in postmenopausal patients. There was more grade ≥3 myelosuppression in carboplatin arm, but there was no difference in nonhematologic toxicities.

Conclusion: Carboplatin did not significantly increase EFS but significantly increased the OS in patients with TNBC, with benefits confined to premenopausal patients.

## INTRODUCTION

INTRODUCTION

In patients with nonmetastatic triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), the primary treatment comprises surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. Recently, immune checkpoint inhibitors have been shown to increase event-free survival (EFS) when added to regimens comprising anthracyclines, cyclophosphamide, taxane, and platinum as part of neoadjuvant therapy. 1

Although multiple studies have shown substantial anticancer activity of platinum agents in TNBC, 2 , 3 they have not been evaluated in adequately powered phase III studies as part of neoadjuvant treatment. Two randomized phase II studies 4 - 7 and one randomized phase III study 8 , 9 of adding platinum to an anthracycline-taxane backbone have shown a significant increase in pathologic complete response (pCR), but with discordant EFS results. The GeparSixto 4 , 5 and BrighTNess 8 , 9 studies showed an increase in EFS, but no benefit was seen in the CALGB 6 , 7 study.

Therefore, the role of platinum as part of neoadjuvant treatment in TNBC has not been definitively proven. Since adding platinum agents can lead to increased toxicity, establishing their role is of substantial clinical relevance. We conducted a randomized phase III clinical trial to evaluate the addition of carboplatin to a standard anthracycline-taxane neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimen in TNBC and report here the primary analysis of this study.

## METHODS

METHODS

Patients

We enrolled patients between 18 and 70 years of age with previously untreated, invasive breast cancer, which was estrogen receptor–, progesterone receptor–, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)–negative by standard criteria. 10 - 12 HER2 testing was initially performed according to 2007 ASCO/College of American Pathologists (CAP) guidelines, 11 and later according to the 2013 ASCO/CAP update. 12 Patients were staged according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer Staging Manual 7th Edition 13 and were required to have either large operable breast cancer, defined as clinical T3 and N1 disease, or locally advanced breast cancer, defined as any clinical T4 or any clinical N2 or N3 disease without any evidence of metastatic (M1) disease after standard staging procedures. They had adequate renal, hepatic, and cardiac function, and were considered fit to receive standard anthracycline-taxane chemotherapy. All patients provided written informed consent before inclusion in the study. Full eligibility criteria are available in the study protocol, which is available online (Data Supplement, online only).

Trial Design and Treatment

This randomized, open-label trial was conducted at a single tertiary cancer center in India. Patients were stratified on the basis of clinical stage (large operable or locally advanced) and menopausal status (premenopausal or postmenopausal) and randomized in a 1:1 ratio, using blocks of 4, to either receive or not receive carboplatin as part of neoadjuvant chemotherapy. We used a concealed, central list in the statistical office after ensuring that eligibility criteria were met.

Patients in the control arm received neoadjuvant chemotherapy with paclitaxel at a dose of 100 mg/m 2 as a 1-h infusion once per week for 8 weeks, followed by an anthracycline regimen (doxorubicin 60 mg/m 2 or epirubicin 90 mg/m 2 , plus cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m 2 ) once every 3 weeks for four cycles. Patients in the platinum arm received carboplatin at a dose of AUC-2 with paclitaxel, once per week for 8 weeks. Immune checkpoint inhibitors and capecitabine were not routinely used as part of the treatment regimen.

Patients were taken up for standard surgery, either mastectomy or breast conservation, within 6 weeks of the last cycle of chemotherapy, followed by adjuvant radiotherapy as per institutional guidelines.

Assessments

The patients underwent clinical examination and radiologic imaging, either a computed tomography (CT) scan of the chest and abdomen with a radionuclide bone scan, or a positron emission tomography (PET)-CT scan, before random assignment. They were followed up clinically during the neoadjuvant phase and underwent repeat imaging during this phase only if there was a clinical indication of disease progression. After all treatment was completed, patients were followed up as per standard institutional practice and further described in the Data Supplement.

End Points

The primary end point of this study was EFS, defined as the time interval between random assignment and disease progression, relapse, or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first. Contralateral invasive breast cancer and any other invasive cancer were included as EFS events if they were the first events. This end point has been referred to as disease-free survival in the study protocol, but the more appropriate term, EFS, is being used in the manuscript. Overall survival (OS) was a key secondary end point of this study and was defined as the time interval between random assignment and death due to any cause. Patients who did not experience an event at the time of analysis were censored on the date of their last follow-up. pCR to neoadjuvant chemotherapy was a secondary end point and was defined as the absence of invasive cancer within the breast and regional lymph nodes comprising the surgical specimen (ypT0/is ypN0). In patients unable to undergo surgery because of disease progression or any other reason, pathologic response was recorded as no-pCR. Evaluation of toxicity was a secondary end point in this study. Toxicities were recorded using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 4.03.

Trial Oversight

The institutional ethics committee approved the initial version of the study protocol and all amendments. All patients provided written informed consent before study enrollment. This trial is registered in the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI/2012/07/002802).

Statistical Analysis

It was assumed that the 5-year EFS in the control arm would be 30%. The study was powered to detect an increase in 5-year EFS by an absolute 10% with the addition of carboplatin to the neoadjuvant regimen. With the type I error rate at a two-sided alpha level of 0.05 for comparison of the primary end point between the two arms, and power of 80%, the planned sample size was 658, with 428 events. After a 10% adjustment for dropouts, the final sample size was 720 participants, equally distributed as 360 in each arm.

In June 2022, the study team considered that the event rate was considerably lower than anticipated, the last accrued patient had completed 18 months of follow-up, the total study duration exceeded 12 years, and the median follow-up of patients exceeded 5 years, which was considered adequate for TNBC. It was also considered that the original assumptions of EFS were likely underestimates (and the corresponding total events were overestimates) for this cohort of patients and unlikely to be achieved in a reasonable time frame. The study team made the decision to analyze the study for its primary and key secondary end points and report the results. This was considered the prespecified primary analysis of this study, conducted earlier than the planned number of events. Any subsequent analyses of this study would be considered post hoc.

Efficacy analyses were performed in a modified intention-to-treat population after excluding three patients with eligibility violations. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate survival end points, which were compared between the two arms using the unstratified log-rank test. The primary comparison of EFS used a two-sided unstratified log-rank test (α = .05). All other P values are nominal, two-sided, and should be interpreted descriptively. Additional EFS and OS analyses were performed in subgroups defined by the stratification factors of menopausal status and stage, and in other subgroups defined by age (≤50 years and >50 years), family history of any cancer (yes and no), preneoadjuvant clinical T size (≤5 cm and >5 cm), and preneoadjuvant clinical nodal status (negative and positive). Tests of interaction were used to explore differences in treatment effects between subgroups.

There were 215 deaths (94 platinum arm and 121 control arm) in the modified ITT population at the time of analysis. OS was significantly higher in the platinum compared with the control arm (HR, 0.74 [95% CI, 0.57 to 0.97]; nominal P = .029), with 5-year OS of 74.4% (95% CI, 69.7% to 79.1%) and 66.8% (95% CI, 61.7% to 71.9%), respectively. Among premenopausal women, OS was higher in the platinum compared with the control arm (HR, 0.57 [95% CI, 0.40 to 0.82]; nominal P = .002), with 5-year OS of 78.2% (95% CI, 72.52% to 83.88%) and 64.6% (95% CI, 57.94% to 71.26%), respectively (Fig 2 ). Among postmenopausal women, there was no difference in OS between the two arms (HR, 1.06 [95% CI, 0.70 to 1.61]; nominal P = .772), with 5-year OS of 69.0% (95% CI, 61.16% to 76.84%) and 69.9% (95% CI, 62.06% to 77.74%), respectively (Fig 2 ). Subgroup analyses of OS by menopausal status, age, family history, clinical stage, tumor size, and nodal status are shown in Figure 3 and the Data Supplement (Figure S1).

Notably, there were statistically significant interactions between study intervention and both menopausal status and age, for EFS and OS, such that there was a substantial and significant benefit of adding platinum to taxane-anthracycline chemotherapy among premenopausal (or younger) patients but no benefit among postmenopausal (or older) patients (Fig 3 ). In Cox models, after adjusting for menopausal status (or age), family history, tumor size, and nodal status, there was no significant EFS benefit (HR, 0.792 [95% CI, 0.62 to 1.02]; nominal P = .072), but there was significant benefit in OS (HR, 0.74 [95% CI, 0.57 to 0.97]; nominal P = .028), by addition of platinum (Data Supplement, Tables S1 and S2).

The distant disease-free survival (DDFS) was not significantly different between the platinum and control arms (HR, 0.78 [95% CI, 0.600 to 1.015]; P = .064), with 5-year DDFS of 72.2% and 65.4%, respectively (Data Supplement, Fig S2). Pathologic Response

pCRs in the platinum and control arms were 187/343 (54.5%; 95% CI, 49.1 to 59.9) and 137/340 (40.3%; 95% CI, 35.0 to 45.7), respectively (nominal P 50 years; Data Supplement, Table S4). In multivariable logistic regression models for factors affecting attainment of pCR, the interaction of study intervention with menopausal status (or age) was statistically significant after adjusting for study intervention, menopausal status (or age), family history of cancer, tumor size, and nodal status (Data Supplement, Tables S5 and S6). Achievement of pCR compared with no-pCR had a marked favorable prognostic impact in terms of EFS and OS, in all patients, premenopausal patients, and postmenopausal patients (Data Supplement, Figs S3 and S4).

## DISCUSSION

DISCUSSION

We report here, to our knowledge, the results of the first phase III randomized trial to specifically evaluate the addition of a platinum agent to a standard taxane-anthracycline-cyclophosphamide regimen as neoadjuvant treatment in patients with TNBC. Adding once-per-week carboplatin substantially increased EFS, OS, and pCR in premenopausal women but not in postmenopausal women and was well tolerated. The efficacy results are concordant with GeparSixto 4 , 5 and BrighTNess 8 , 9 studies and a recent meta-analysis, 14 but discordant with those of CALGB 40603. 6 , 7

Two 4 , 5 , 8 , 9 of the three previous randomized studies 4 - 9 evaluating neoadjuvant platinum in TNBC, although underpowered for survival outcomes, showed an EFS benefit, while one 6 , 7 did not. CALGB 40603 6 , 7 had a factorial design, wherein bevacizumab was the other study question. Any bevacizumab effect could have diluted platinum efficacy, and this interaction was not powered to be disentangled. Furthermore, only 76% of patients completed the four planned platinum cycles, and 41% required dose reduction. GeparSixto, like our study, used once-per-week carboplatin delivered with a higher cumulative dose and intensity, which might be associated with fewer dose interruptions compared with once-every-three-weeks dosing, with similar pCR. 15 However, GeparSixto omitted cyclophosphamide in the control arm and included bevacizumab in the control and experimental arms, which is not standard practice. Although BrighTNess was a phase III trial, its hierarchical statistical testing plan required significance for the pCR comparisons before formal testing of secondary end points such as EFS could proceed. 8 , 9 Since adding veliparib did not improve pCR, the subsequent EFS analysis of adding platinum to paclitaxel became descriptive, limiting its statistical conclusiveness. Our study has a clean design and adds carboplatin to a widely used backbone neoadjuvant chemotherapy in TNBC.

In this study, there was an interaction between the benefit of platinum and menopausal status, as well as age, for all the three important end points—EFS, OS, and pCR. The results are very similar within the corresponding (premenopausal and ≤50 years, postmenopausal and >50 years) menopausal and age subgroups because of high overlap between menopausal status and age. There was stratification by menopausal status; therefore, prognostic factors are balanced between randomized arms in menopausal subgroups. Only the CALGB 40603 study has reported a subgroup analysis of EFS and OS by age ( 5 cm, and 88.8% having clinically node-positive disease. This is representative of patients seen in regions with fewer opportunities for early detection and is reflected in the lower control and platinum arm survivals compared with TNBC studies from high-income regions. Although our patients had a higher risk profile in terms of baseline tumor burden, the pCR rates (ypT0/is ypN0) are comparable with other neoadjuvant trials, 4 , 8 but the long-term OS is lower. 5 , 9 This suggests that although pCR may be attained in a similar proportion of patients, there is a higher chance of relapse and death even after attaining pCR in patients with high baseline tumor burden.

Our study showed an OS benefit in the modified ITT population, driven by the premenopausal subgroup. Previous randomized studies 4 - 9 were not adequately powered to evaluate OS. Our analysis is being reported with an adequate follow-up of 67 months, which is longer than GeparSixto and BrighTNess studies and comparable with CALGB 40603, and with a larger sample size than all three studies.

We used once-per-week paclitaxel followed by an anthracycline-cyclophosphamide sequence. Although the results are inconsistent, 31 previous studies suggest that this sequence may be more efficacious than the conventional reverse sequence. 32 - 34 Paclitaxel was administered once per week, and cyclophosphamide was combined with anthracycline. Thus, the control arm can be considered standard in terms of chemotherapy drugs. We also administered carboplatin once per week, which was well tolerated and allowed completion of planned treatment.

Our study has clinical practice implications. The KEYNOTE 522 1 , 16 study of pembrolizumab has used a similar paclitaxel-carboplatin followed by anthracycline-cyclophosphamide backbone and has become a standard neoadjuvant regimen for TNBC. Our results reinforce this regimen as a standard in premenopausal patients. When pembrolizumab is not feasible, it may be appropriate to administer once-per-week platinum plus paclitaxel followed by an anthracycline sequence in premenopausal patients. Our results also suggest that using platinum should be reconsidered as a standard practice in postmenopausal patients.

This study has some limitations. It was conducted at a single center and recruited patients over a 10-year period, which could limit generalizability. We used paclitaxel at a dose of 100 mg/m 2 once per week for 8 weeks to shorten the duration of chemotherapy, while approximately delivering the same cumulative dose of 800 mg/m 2 compared with 960 mg/m 2 with the more widely used 12-week regimen.
