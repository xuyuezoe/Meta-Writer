# The role of Her-2 in penile squamous cell carcinoma progression and cisplatin chemoresistance and potential for antibody-drug conjugate-based therapy.

## Abstract

Background: Cisplatin-based chemotherapy has been the first choice for advanced penile squamous cell carcinoma (PSCC) in the last decade, but its utility is limited by the low response rate, systemic toxicity, and chemoresistance, which contribute to a poor prognosis. There is no standard second-line therapy for advanced PSCC. Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (Her-2)-targeted antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) are novel low-toxicity agents which have greatly improved clinical outcomes for several advanced cancers. We aimed to explore the expression pattern, clinical significance, and oncogenic roles of Her-2 and the therapeutic potential of Her-2-targeted ADCs in PSCC.

Methods: Her-2 immunohistochemistry was performed for the largest single-centre PSCC cohort to date (367 patients). PSCC cell lines, cisplatin-resistant cell lines, subcutaneous xenograft, and footpad metastatic models were used to investigate the biological roles of Her-2 in PSCC progression. Cytotoxicity, apoptosis assays, and western blotting investigated the mechanism of Her-2 induced cisplatin-chemoresistance. The efficacy of Disitamab Vedotin (RC48), a Her-2-targeted ADC, was evaluated in PSCC.

Results: Her-2 was identified as an adverse prognostic indicator associated with advanced Tumor-Node-Metastasis (TNM) stages and poor survival with an immunohistochemical expression rate of approximately 47.7% (1+, 23.2%; 2+, 18.0%; 3+, 6.5%) in PSCC. Her-2 promotes cell proliferation, migration, invasion, tumour progression, and cisplatin resistance in PSCC. Mechanistically, Her-2 inhibits cisplatin-induced cell apoptosis by the activation of Akt phosphorylation at Ser473 and disrupts the balance between proapoptotic and antiapoptotic proteins. Meanwhile, cisplatin-resistant PSCC cells present aggressive oncogenic abilities and Her-2 upregulation. More importantly, RC48 displayed remarkable antitumor activities in both Her2-positive and cisplatin-resistant PSCC tumours.

Conclusion: Our study suggests that Her-2 is an available therapeutic biomarker for PSCC. Her-2-targeted ADC might have the potential to improve clinical outcomes in high-risk Her-2-positive advanced PSCC patients and provide precious second-line clinical choice for appropriate cisplatin-based chemoresistance patients.

Keywords Penile cancer Her-2 ADCs Cisplatin resistance Apoptosis 1 Introduction Penile squamous cell carcinoma (PSCC) is a physically and mentally devastating malignancy in males [1]. Regional lymph node and distant metastasis are the most fatal clinical factors in PSCC; these factors dramatically decreased the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate by 5%–59% [2–5]. Currently, European Association of Urology (EAU) guidelines recommend platinum-based chemotherapy regimens as the first-line treatment for advanced PSCC patients [6]. However, the response rate of cisplatin-based chemotherapy is as low as 38.5%–52.6%, and patients suffer from significant toxicity with approximately 40.1% of patients exhibiting ≥ grade 3 complications [6–10]. Moreover, there is currently no standard second-line therapy [6,11,12]. Although several studies in small cohorts have tried epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors as subsequent strategies, the benefits were far from satisfactory, with only a 23.5% response rate and notable hematotoxicity [13]. For PSCC patients who progress on these regimens, the prognosis remains poor, with a median OS of less than 6 months [6,10,12]. In addition, EAU guidelines clearly state that there are still no available prognostic and therapeutic biomarkers for PSCC [6]. Therefore, the exploration of potential targets and corresponding safe and effective therapeutic strategies is urgently needed for advanced PSCC patients. Recently, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) have emerged as breakthrough treatments owing to their advantages of precise targeting, high antitumor efficiency, and minimal toxicity compared to chemotherapy [14,15]. Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (Her-2)-specific ADCs such as disitamab vedotin (RC48) are the most widely used therapeutic targets in clinical practice and have greatly improved the outcomes of Her-2-positive breast, gastric, and bladder cancers [16–18]. As a classical unfavourable prognostic biomarker, Her-2 contributes to cell cycle arrest, apoptosis inhibition, and chemoresistance in the majority of tumours, but its role is unclear in PSCC [19–21]. Jacob et al. reported that Her-2 was amplification the second most frequent genomic alteration in the EGFR family and was present in 4–10% of metastatic cases in a sequencing study [22,23]. However, the immunohistochemical expression pattern of Her-2 is not yet known and is a crucial indicator in the response to anti-Her-2 ADC therapy. More importantly, previous Her-2 studies in PSCC were limited by small sample sizes and lacked PSCC cell line and animal model validation [24,25]. The mechanism by which Her-2 expression affects cisplatin resistance also needs to be further investigated. In this study, we first confirmed that Her-2 is a poor prognostic indicator, and approximately 24.5% of PSCC patients showed Her-2-positive (18.0% 2+, 6.5% 3+) immunohistochemical expression in a large cohort of 367 patients with over a decade median follow-up. Subsequently, we demonstrated that Her-2 promoted PSCC tumour progression, inhibited cisplatin-induced cell apoptosis, and increased cisplatin chemoresistance both in vitro and in vivo. More importantly, we clarified the remarkable antitumor effects of RC48 in Her2-positive PSCC cells and especially in cisplatin-resistant PSCC cells. Our results provide additional therapeutic options for Her-2-positive PSCC patients and are especially important because they provide a treatment option for difficult-to-treat cisplatin-resistant advanced PSCC. 2 Material and methods 2.1 Patient cohorts and research ethics For this retrospective study, 367 PSCC patients treated between January 2000 and December 2021 at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (SYSUCC) were selected for this study based on the following criteria: (1) newly diagnosed PSCC patients with well-preserved paraffin-embedded tumour sections, (2) PSCC patients who underwent bilateral inguinal lymphadenectomy, and (3) PSCC patients with definitive pathological stages according to the 8th edition of the Tumor-Node-Metastasis Staging System for Penile Cancer. In addition, the mRNA-seq expression data for 39 PSCC tumours and five normal tissues from the Gene Expression Omnibus database (GSE57955) and our previously published whole-transcriptome sequencing data for eight paired PSCC tissues (SYSUCC cohort) were used for validation [26–28]. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of SYSUCC (No. 2022-FXY-069), and informed consent regarding clinical details was obtained from the patients (No. B2022-385-01). 2.2 Cell lines, culture conditions, and lentiviral vector construction PSCC cell lines, including Penl1, Penl2, 149RCA, 149RM, and 156LM, were established in our previous studies [29]. Immortalized human keratinocytes (HaCaT) cell line was used as a normal control, and two breast cancer cell lines, SK-BR-3 and MCF-7, were purchased from the Type Culture Collection of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). All cell lines were maintained in complete Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (DMEM) with 10% foetal bovine serum (Gibco, United States of America [USA]) and 1% penicillinstreptomycin (Gibco, USA)) at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Lentiviruses carrying short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting human ERBB2 and negative control (NC) plasmids (VP013-U6-MCS-CMV-ZsGreen-PGK-Puro) were purchased from Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) to silence the expression of Her-2. The sequences were as follows: shHer2-GGGAGAGAGTTCTGAGGATTG, shNC-TTCTCCGAACGTGTCACGT. The full-length human ERBB2 gene was cloned into vector plasmids (Ubi-MCS-3FLAG-SV40-puromycin) by Genechem Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) to construct the Her-2 overexpression lentiviruses. Transfection was performed according to the corresponding manuals. 2.3 RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) Total mRNA extraction (Vazyme, #R701), cDNA reverse transcription (Vazyme #R323) and target gene amplification (Vazyme #Q711) were conducted based on the manufacturer’s directions. The mRNA expression of the target genes was compared with that of glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase by the 2(-∆∆Ct) method. The primers for the target genes are listed in Table S1. 2.4 Western blot (WB) Cells were lysed, and proteins were separated using electrophoresis and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes as previously described. Antibody dilutions and incubation times were based on the instructions provided by the manufacturer. The luminescence chemical imaging system was used to detect protein expression with the ECL reagent (Vazyme #E412). Antibodies and dilutions are listed in Table S2. 2.5 Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining and scoring We consulted two independent pathologists (CKM and LJL) to perform diagnosis and IHC scoring. A total of 367 PSCC paraffin-embedded tissue sections were subjected to IHC staining as previously described. The HER2/ErbB2 antibody (clone D8F12, #4290, CST) at a 1:500 dilution was used as the primary antibody and was visualised by utilising the EnVision Detection Kit (Dako). The criteria for the Her-2 scoring were based on the detection guidelines for breast cancer [30]. In brief, no staining and <10% with incomplete tumour cell membrane staining were regarded as negative Her-2 expression (0 points). Samples with 10–50% weakly yellow staining with complete tumour cell membranes were scored as 1+. Those with ≥50% weak staining or ≥10% moderate yellow-brown staining were scored as 2+. Those with ≥50% moderate staining or ≥10% strong brown staining were scored as 3+. 2.6 Proliferation and invasion assays Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assays and colony formation assays were performed to detect the proliferation potential of PSCC cells as previously described. Wound healing assays and Transwell migration assays were conducted to explore cell migration. For cell invasion, 105 PSCC cells were seeded in the upper compartment of 24-well transwell chambers with Matrigel (356231, Corning) in serum-free DMEM and cocultured with 10% FBS-DMEM in the lower compartment for 24 h. The number of migrating cells was counted to evaluate the invasion capability of PSCC cells. 2.7 Chemicals, cytotoxicity and chemosensitivity assay RC48 is a Her-2-targeted ADC drug [31,32]. Mechanistically, RC48 combined with Her-2 receptors on the tumour cell membrane with disitamab, a novel anti-Her-2 antibody, to induce endocytosis and release the powerful cytotoxic monomethyl auristatin E, causing tumour cell death. Disitamab, the naked antibody of RC48 without killing effect, was used as the NC. Both drugs were provided by RemeGen Co., Ltd. (Yantai, China). Cisplatin (NSC 119875, S1166) and AKT inhibitor (MK-2206, S1078) were purchased from Selleck and dissolved in saline and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), respectively, according to the instruction manuals. For cytotoxicity and chemosensitivity assays, 105 PSCC cells were seeded in 96-well plates for 24 h until the cells became adherent to the wall. Drugs were diluted with gradient concentrations in the supplemented culture medium and continuously cultured for 48 h. The solvent of the corresponding drugs was used as NC. Cell number and viability were assessed and calculated by CCK-8 assays. The half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was defined as the concentration at which a drug inhibits cell growth by 50%. 2.8 Xenograft mouse model and chemotherapy assays in vivo The subcutaneous tumorigenesis assay and popliteal lymph node metastasis assay were conducted to explore the proliferative and invasive capacity of PSCC cells in vivo. For subcutaneous tumour models, six-week-old male BALB/c nude mice (n = 5) (Jiangsu GemPharmatech Co., Ltd.) were injected subcutaneously with 5 × 105 PSCC cells in 100 μl phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The sizes of the tumours were recorded every 3–4 days, and the mice were euthanized after 3 weeks. Tumour volume was calculated according to the formula volume (mm3) = 0.5 × length × width2. For lymph node metastasis models, 2 × 105 PSCC cells in 50 μl PBS were injected into the footpad of mice (n = 5), and we harvested the popliteal lymph nodes 6 weeks after inoculation. For chemosensitivity assays, mice were randomly divided into each group (n = 6) after subcutaneous tumorigenesis for 7 days. Cisplatin (5 mg/kg, 100 μl) was injected intraperitoneally, and RC48 or disitamab (20 mg/kg, 100 μl) was intravenously injected on days 1 and 7. To silence Her-2 expression in tumours, 5 nmol si-Her2 or siNC was injected peritumorally on D1, D4, D7, D10, and D14. Tumour proliferation curves were used to assess drug sensitivity. According to ethical standards, mice were euthanized when weight loss was over 20%. The Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of SYSUCC (L102012019002V) approved the animal protocols. 2.9 Immunofluorescence analysis and flow cytometry Immunofluorescence analysis was used to determine the intracellular localisation of Her-2 protein. A total of 1000 PSCC cells were seeded into 35-mm confocal dishes for 24 h, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, and permeabilized with Triton X-100 for 10 min. After blocking with 5% BSA, the cells were incubated with HER2/ErbB2 antibody (1:400, #2165, CST) overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubation with the corresponding secondary antibody and counterstaining with DAPI for visualisation. For flow cytometry, 100 μl suspensions of 106 PSCC cells were blocked with 5 μl Human TruStain FcX (#422301, Biolegend) to reduce nonspecific staining. Primary antibodies, including disitamab, RC-48 (4 μg/ml), and HER2/ErbB2 (1:200, #2165, CST), were incubated for 2 h, followed by anti-rabbit IgG 488 nm-conjugated secondary antibody (1:1000, #4412, CST). FITC (green) fluorescence was detected by FL1 channel with flow cytometry, and median fluorescence intensity was analysed by FlowJo software (V.10.1, Ashland, USA). 2.10 Apoptosis analysis The apoptosis assay was performed with Annexin V (PE-conjugated) and a 7-AAD apoptosis detection kit (Vazyme) by flow cytometry according to the manufacturer’s protocol. In brief, 106 PSCC cells were pretreated with a specific concentration of cisplatin for 24 h and mixed with 100 μl binding buffer to prepare a single-cell suspension. Five microliters of Annexin V-PE and 5 μl of 7-AAD were added to the cell suspension and incubated for 15 min in the dark at room temperature, and then the corresponding fluorescence intensity was detected. The percentage of apoptotic cells was calculated as the proportion of Annexin V-positive cells. 2.11 Bioinformatics and functional cluster analysis To explore the differential gene expression profiles of PSCC cells with different Her-2 expression levels, Her-2-silenced 149RM cells, and shNC cells were subjected to RNA sequencing by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). We used the clusterProfiler package in R to analyse the Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted by GSEA tools (version 4.1) with subsets of the Molecular Signatures Database (C2 and C5) as described previously [33]. 2.12 Statistical analyses Data are shown as the mean ± SD and were analysed by SPSS software (Version 25.0). Student’s t test or one-way ANOVA was used to compare differences between the two groups. The chi-square test determined the difference in the composition ratios. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and multivariate Cox regression analysis with forward selection were used to compare the clinical outcomes. Pearson correlation analysis was used to analyse the correlations between genes. A p value < 0.05 was considered to indicate a significant difference. 3 Results 3.1 The expression of Her-2 in PSCC cell lines and tissues To determine the expression of Her-2 in PSCC cell lines, qPCR and WB were performed. qPCR assays demonstrated that the mRNA levels of Her-2 were obviously higher in 149RM and 149RCA cells than in Penl1, Penl2, 156LM and normal control HaCaT cells (Fig. 1A). Consistent with the qPCR results, Her-2 protein was overexpressed in 149RM and 149RCA cells, similar to the Her-2-overexpressing breast cancer cell line SK-BR-3 [34,35]. The protein expression of Her-2 in Penl2 and Penl1 was as low as that in MCF-7 (breast cancer cells with low baseline Her-2 expression), while 156LM cells had faint Her-2 expression (Fig. 1B). Then, immunofluorescence analysis showed that Her-2 had typical cell membrane localisation in 149RM cells (Fig. 1C). Flow cytometric analyses demonstrated that membrane Her-2 protein was expressed more highly in 149RM and 149RCA cells than in other PSCC cell lines and HaCaT cells (Fig. 1D). For PSCC tissues, IHC assays indicated that Her-2 was expressed on the cell membrane and upregulated in tumours compared with normal tissues (Fig. 1E). Furthermore, in the cohort of eight patients with metastatic PSCC, Her-2 expression was highest in the metastatic lymph nodes, followed by primary tumour tissues, with the lowest expression in corresponding normal tissues (Fig. 1F). The external GSE57955 database with data on 39 PSCC patients also verified the overexpression of Her-2 in tumour samples (Fig. 1G). Taken together, our results suggest that Her-2 might serve as an oncogene with high expression in PSCC cell lines and tumour tissues. The immunohistochemical expression patterns of Her-2 are shown in Fig. S1. In total, 277/367 (75.5%) PSCC patients were considered to have Her-2 expression, in which 192/367 (52.3%) patients were negative for Her-2 expression (0 points) and 85/367 (23.2%) patients had a Her-2 score of 1+. To explore whether Akt activation is involved in Her2-mediated cisplatin-induced chemoresistance in PSCC, we assessed the phosphorylation levels of Akt pathway components and the expression of downstream apoptosis-related proteins. The cisplatin-induced apoptosis rescue assays revealed that apoptosis was restored, as indicated by the dramatic increase in the proportion of apoptotic cells in Her-2-overexpressing cells when these cells were cocultured with MK-2206 (Fig. 4C). Even when patients undergo thorough lymph node dissection, the prognosis remains poor (5-year OS for pN1: 75–89%, pN2: 29–67%, pN3: 0–17%) [5,10]. In 2010, Pagliaro et al. found that cisplatin-based regimens such as TIP (cisplatin, ifosfamide, and paclitaxel) displayed a 50% response rate in pN2–3 PSCC patients [7], and since then, both the EAU and National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines have recommended cisplatin-based chemotherapy as the first-line option for advanced PSCC patients [6,11]. However, patients often fail to exhibit a durable response, and up to half of advanced PSCC patients progress on first-line cisplatin chemotherapy, ultimately resulting in death within 6–24 months [1,6,10,12]. Moreover, 88.2% of advanced PSCC patients who received TIP regimens experienced severe toxic side effects leading to a drastically decreased quality of life, and only 58.8% of patients endured full courses of chemotherapy [7–9]. EGFR-tyrosine kinase inhibitors are available targeted therapies that can serve as alternatives for cisplatin-based chemotherapy, but the unsatisfactory response rates (23.5% and 32.1% for cetuximab and dacomitinib, respectively) limit their application in a larger cohort [13,45,46]. Among other widely used serological biomarkers, squamous cell carcinoma antigen has a sensitivity of less than 25% in advanced PSCC, which has greatly hindered its clinical therapeutic translation [6,47]. Therefore, the identification of targetable biomarkers and the exploration of low-toxicity strategies are urgently needed for advanced PSCC patients. ADCs are innovative drugs that directly deliver toxins to cancer cells to induce apoptosis and prevent debilitating side effects [14]. In addition, ADCs enhance the antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxic effect to inhibit tumour growth and show synergy with chemotherapy, which has great potential to make a paradigm shift in cancer chemotherapy, especially in PSCC [14,15]. Currently, Her-2-targeted ADCs are some of the most successful and widely used drugs in Her-2-positive cancers (Her-2 IHC score 2+/3+). Comparing with trastuzumab-based first generation (Trastuzumab emtansine, T-DM1) and second-generation (Trastuzumab deruxtecan, T-DXd) Her-2-targeted ADCs, hertuzumab (also called disitamab, the naked antibody of RC48) has stronger binding capacity to Her-2 receptor reducing off-target effects [48]. Moreover, RC48 with wider range of indications have greatly improved the prognosis of advanced breast cancer, gastric cancer, uroepithelial carcinoma indicating the available therapeutic potential in Her-2 positive cancers [14,16,17]. Although previous sequencing studies have suggested that Her-2 amplification is the second most frequent genetic alteration and occurred in 4%–6% of PSCC patients even with a higher rate of approximately 10% in advanced disease [22,23], the large-scale IHC staining evidence is lacking. In addition, the biological functions and specific mechanisms by which Her-2 contributes to PSCC progression remain unknown. Therefore, we first explored the IHC expression pattern and clinical significance of Her-2 in the largest single-centre cohort to date (367 PSCC patients with long-term follow-up) and investigated its oncogenic roles in PSCC. In our cohort, Her-2 IHC expression was detected in 47.7% of PSCC patients, of which 23.2%, 18.0%, and 6.5% of patients were considered Her-2 1+, 2+ and 3+, respectively. The Her-2 positive rate (Her-2 IHC score 2+/3+) was 24.5% in PSCC patients, which was similar to that in other HPV-related cancers (e.g. 39% in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma and 25%–30% in ovarian cancer) [20,49]. Consistent with the findings in breast cancer and gastrointestinal tumours [21,34], Her-2 was identified as an adverse prognostic factor predicting advanced disease and poor outcomes in PSCC patients. Clinical analysis suggested that Her-2 overexpression was correlated with advanced Tumor-Node-Metastasis stage and predicted shorter PFS and OS than low Her-2 expression (Her-2 IHC score 0/1+) in PSCC patients. Subsequently, by utilising PSCC cell lines established in our previous studies, we verified that overexpression of Her-2 promoted cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro and stimulated tumour growth and lymph node metastasis in vivo. Our results demonstrated that Her-2 serves as a crucial oncogene to maintain the malignant potential of PSCC and serves as a high-risk prognostic indicator. More importantly, the Her-2-targeted ADC RC48 displayed extraordinary antitumor activity in Her-2-positive PSCC cells and enhanced efficacy when combined with cisplatin (Fig. 5C). In conclusion, we detected that approximately one-quarter of high-risk PSCC patients with Her-2-positive expression might benefit from Her-2-targeted ADC therapy. Growing evidence has demonstrated that Her-2 not only contributes to tumorigenesis and tumour progression but also induces cisplatin chemoresistance in multiple cancers [21,37,50]. Her-2-negative non-small cell lung cancer patients exhibited a 92.3% response rate to cisplatin-based chemotherapy, which dropped dramatically to 61.9% in Her-2-positive patients [51]. Her-2-overexpressing gastric cancer patients presented shortened OS times due to cisplatin resistance, but OS time was prolonged significantly from 11.1 to 13.8 months when chemotherapy was combined with trastuzumab (a Her-2 target monoclonal antibody) [21,52,53]. Therefore, we investigated the relationship between Her-2 expression and the efficacy of cisplatin chemotherapy in 90 advanced PSCC patients. Our results showed that the objective response rates were 50% and 36.9%, but the PD rate significantly increased from 28.2% to 44.7% in patients with low and high Her-2 expression, respectively. Survival analysis also verified that Her-2-overexpressing PSCC patients receiving cisplatin-based chemotherapy had poorer survival outcomes. Furthermore, cellular and animal tumorigenesis assays indicated that Her-2 accumulation was positively correlated with cisplatin resistance, while knockdown of Her-2 expression enhanced the sensitivity of cisplatin treatment and inhibited tumour growth. Thus, our results support that the combination of Her-2-targeted ADCs and chemotherapy as a first-line strategy might improve the clinical outcomes of high-risk Her-2-positive advanced PSCC and provide an available alternative, especially for cisplatin-intolerant patients. Regarding mechanistic exploration, it is generally accepted that DNA damage and subsequent induction of apoptosis are the primary cytotoxic mechanisms of cisplatin [37]. Her-2 participates in cisplatin chemoresistance by activating downstream signals to inhibit cell apoptosis or promote cell cycle arrest. For instance, Her-2 increased CDKN1A nuclear exclusion and regulated Bcl-2 expression via mitogen-activated protein kinase/extracellular signal-regulated kinase pathways to induce cisplatin resistance in non-small cell lung cancer and breast cancer [19]. Sukawa et al. reported that the PI3K/Akt pathway is a main downstream signalling pathway activated by Her-2 that participates in cisplatin-induced chemoresistance in gastric cancer cells [41]. In this study, we detected that Her-2 increased the phosphorylation level of Akt at position Ser473, which directly inhibited the expression of the pro-apoptotic proteins Bad and Bax. On the one hand, Bad inhibition led to the dissociation of Bad/Bcl-2 and Bad/Bcl-xL heterodimers and increased the expression of the active forms of the cytoplasmic free antiapoptotic proteins Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL [54]. On the other hand, the downregulation of Bax impeded the Bax-initiated mitochondrial apoptosis pathway, suppressed the release of cytochrome c from mitochondria, and inhibited the apoptotic caspase cascade reaction [55]. In short, our results demonstrated that the detailed mechanism of Her-2 induced cisplatin chemoresistance in PSCC attributed to the disruption balance between pro-apoptotic and anti-apoptotic proteins via Akt signalling pathways to suppress cisplatin-induced cell apoptosis. Unfortunately, most patients with locally advanced PSCC will experience relapse or disease progression with distant metastasis after first-line cisplatin-based chemotherapy, and only 11.2–16% of pN3 patients achieve sustained remission over 5 years [10,12,56]. These patients have frustrating outcomes with subsequent salvage therapy, with a median OS of only approximately 5.5–9.2 months [12,57], and there is a lack of standard second-line regimens [6]. Previous studies have suggested that the acquisition of cisplatin resistance could induce Her-2 overexpression in cancers. Huang et al. found that cisplatin-resistant gastric cancer cells increased Her-2 expression and promoted cell invasion through epithelial-mesenchymal transition [21]. Marth et al. demonstrated that cisplatin resistance is associated with a reduction in IFN-γ sensitivity and upregulation of Her-2 in ovarian carcinoma cells [58]. Therefore, we established the Penl2 cisplatin-resistant cell line in PSCC and detected the promotion of tumorigenic and metastatic abilities in these cells compared with wild-type cells. Interestingly, Her-2 expression was significantly increased in Penl2-DDP cells, in agreement with previous findings. More importantly, RC48 exhibited superior killing effects in both Penl2-DDP cells and the corresponding animal subcutaneous tumour models. However, there is still a lack of clinical statistics on Her-2 expression changes in paired advanced PSCC tissues before and after cisplatin-based chemoresistance. Although there are no targeted-ADC drugs related clinical trials on penile cancer up to date, our adequate experimental results proved that targeting Her-2 ADC such as RC48 is a promising and low-toxicity regimen. For clinical translational perspective, it is better to perform a clinical trial to investigate whether there is an efficacy benefit of combining RC48 with first-line TIP chemotherapy in Her-2 high expression (IHC: 2/3+) high-risk advanced PSCC patients. Besides, we also suggest that PSCC patients who progress on cisplatin-based chemotherapy could undergo tumour biopsy to reassess Her-2 expression status or undergo genomic sequencing owing to the lack of explicit subsequent regimens. Clinical trials could be conducted to explore the clinical outcomes of Her-2 targeted ADCs in the second-line therapy of cisplatin-resistant advanced PSCC patients. In summary, we confirmed the oncogenic roles of Her-2 in promoting tumour progression and inducing cisplatin resistance in PSCC. The Her-2-targeted ADC RC48 is a potential regimen for advanced PSCC with the advantages of precision, effectiveness, and low toxicity compared to conventional chemotherapy. The IHC expression status of Her-2 needs to be further validated in other centres. We recommend that patients with high-risk Her-2-positive (IHC score 2+/3+) advanced PSCC be enroled in clinical trials to clarify the benefit of Her-2-targeted ADCs. 5 Conclusions In this study, we investigated the IHC expression of Her-2 in a large cohort of PSCC patients and demonstrated that Her-2 was an adverse prognostic factor that promotes tumour progression and induces cisplatin chemoresistance. More importantly, we found that a Her-2-targeted ADC displayed excellent efficiency in both Her-2-positive and cisplatin-resistant PSCC cells. Our results might provide innovative therapeutic strategies to improve clinical outcomes in high-risk Her-2-positive advanced PSCC patients and create a hard-won second opportunity for patients with cisplatin-based chemoresistant advanced PSCC.
